"Trusted" means high or medium confidence. The Word row keeps the 19 "dish" entries of the glossary out of the count on the day, because a Dish card wins over a Word card. Not counted: lanna_dict_full.json (1,502 entries with Thai-only glosses and no confidence score, used to understand the Vendor but never shown), plus etiquette, norms, beliefs and ethnic groups, which are excluded by decision.

Is that a lot? For a user it is not: they will see 3 to 5 cards per conversation, and the model's job is to pick one out of ~220. For the demo it is too many to check, so we script one scene and have a Thai teammate check 5 cards: kaeng hang le, nam phrik num, ยินดีเจ้า, เต้าใด, the rain Moment.

2. Three formats

The same content in three densities. Choose one format for all three types, to keep the chat consistent.

FormatWhat showsGood forCost
A. CompactOne line, tap to openKeeps the chat clean, the conversation stays in focusThe Allergy Flag cannot hide behind a tap, so it breaks the pattern
B. StandardTitle, Thai name, 1 to 2 lines, tags, Local detailReadable in 3 s at a stall, shows the pack on stageTakes about a third of the screen
C. RichB plus ingredients, source, "ask them" lineShows the depth of the pack to the juryToo long at a stall, it looks like a web page

7. My recommendation

B. Standard for all three types. It is the only format that fits the Allergy Flag inside the card, reads in 3 s, and shows the pack on stage. C is too long for a stall, and its "Ask them" line brings back the follow-up question we just dropped. A breaks as soon as there is a flag.
