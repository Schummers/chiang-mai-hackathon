# Northern Thai cooking foundations: a step-by-step guide for foreigners

*อาหารเหนือพื้นฐาน: sticky rice, three chilli dips and pork crackling, explained the way a Chiang Mai home cook would. Compiled 2026-09-26, mainly from Thai-language sources (CMU Northern Thai Information Center, Krua.co, Wongnai, Matichon Weekly, HRDI, Michelin Thailand).*

**About pronunciation.** Northern Thai (คำเมือง, *kham mueang*) has six tones on open syllables in Chiang Mai: 24 low-rising, 21 low-falling, 44ʔ high level with a catch in the throat, 33 mid, 42 high-falling and 45 high-rising. The tone numbers below were *derived* from standard tone-correspondence rules, not checked against recordings, so treat them as a rough guide. Main sound shifts from Central Thai: r becomes h (รัก → ฮัก), ch/kh/th/ph are often unaspirated (ชิ้น → จิ๊น, แคบ ≈ 'gaep'), and the r in clusters is dropped (พริก → 'phik').

## 1. The way Northerners eat
### The Lanna meal day (มื้ออาหารของคนล้านนา)
Traditionally three main meals: ข้าวงาย khao ngai (morning), ข้าวตอน khao ton (midday) and ข้าวแลง khao laeng (evening), plus an extra ข้าวเถาะ on days of heavy work. Steamed sticky rice is the staple at every meal. Traditionally rice was steamed once a day, in the morning, and kept warm in a woven rice box for the rest of the day. Everyday Northern speech also uses ข้าวเจ๊า for breakfast (Central ข้าวเช้า).

- **ข้าวงาย** *khao ngai*: breakfast (traditional term) (Central Thai ข้าวเช้า)
- **ข้าวตอน** *khao ton*: lunch (traditional term) (Central Thai ข้าวกลางวัน)
- **ข้าวแลง** *khao laeng*: dinner (Central Thai ข้าวเย็น)
- **ข้าวเจ๊า** *khao jao*: breakfast (everyday) (Central Thai ข้าวเช้า)
- **กิ๋นข้าว / กิ๋นเข้า** *kin khao*: to eat (a meal) (Central Thai กินข้าว)

### Soaking and steaming sticky rice (การหม่าข้าวและนึ่งข้าว)
Sticky rice is soaked first (Kham Mueang หม่า, 'ma'), traditionally overnight, and steamed in the morning. The Lanna steaming vessel is the ไหเข้า hai khao (Central/Isan: หวด huat): carved from teak or other wood, made from a bamboo tube, or woven bamboo. A perforated board (ตาดไห, about 10 holes) sits in the base. The vessel sits on a pot of boiling water and the joint is sealed with a strip of wet cloth. After steaming, the rice is tipped onto a wooden tray and turned with a paddle to let steam escape (otherwise it goes soggy), then packed into a woven rice box. CMU's recipe: rinse until the water runs clear, dampen the basket, steam about 20 minutes; done when grains are tender and don't stick to the hand. A Wongnai test found that for NEW-crop rice a 1-hour soak gave the best texture and overnight soaking made grains fragile; older rice needs longer. Sizing folk custom: the head of household measured a new vessel with the thumb from base to rim, chanting 'อิ่ม-อยาก' (full-hungry), hoping to finish on 'อิ่ม'.

- **หม่า / หม่าข้าว** *ma khao*: to soak (sticky rice) before steaming (Central Thai แช่ข้าว)
- **นึ่งข้าว / นึ่งเข้า** *nueng khao*: to steam rice (Central Thai นึ่งข้าว)
- **ไหเข้า (ไหนึ่ง)** *hai khao*: sticky-rice steaming vessel (Central Thai หวด)
- **ตาดไห** *tat hai*: perforated base board of the steamer (Central Thai แผ่นรองก้นหวด)
- **ก่องข้าว** *kong khao*: woven lidded sticky-rice box (household) (Central Thai กระติ๊บข้าว)
- **แอ๊บข้าว** *aep khao*: small woven rice box carried to the fields; a large one is ก่องข้าวหลวง (70-80 cm) (Central Thai กระติ๊บเล็ก)

### Eating sticky rice with your hand (การปั้นข้าวเหนียวกินด้วยมือ)
Pull a small piece of rice from the shared basket with your right hand, squeeze it into a bite-sized ball with your fingers (don't knead it long), then use the ball to scoop or dip into chilli dip or pinch up a piece of meat or vegetable. Some people rub a little oil on the palm so rice doesn't stick. Eating from one shared rice basket around a circle is described as a symbol of equality and sharing. Sticky rice also features in soul-calling (เรียกขวัญ) ceremonies.

- **ปั้นข้าว** *pan khao*: to press sticky rice into a ball by hand (Central Thai ปั้นข้าวเหนียว)
- **จิ้ม** *jim*: to dip (Central Thai จิ้ม)

### Table etiquette (มารยาทบนสำรับ)
Family eats in a circle; elders (grandparents) start first, younger people follow (CMU). Traditional don'ts: talking loudly, talking about disgusting things, tapping bowls, 'ยอแคม' (lifting a bowl to slurp curry straight from it) and 'สกหม้อแกง' (sneaking food from the pot behind others' backs). After the meal, the chilli-dip bowl is turned over, scraps go to the pigs, and older people might chew fermented tea leaves (เหมี้ยง). For visitors today: wait for the host/elder to start, take small amounts from shared dishes, use the serving spoon (ช้อนกลาง) for curries, and don't take the last piece without offering it.

- **ยอแคม** *yo khaem*: lifting a bowl to drink curry from its rim (traditionally impolite) (Central Thai ยกถ้วยซด)
- **สกหม้อแกง** *sok mo kaeng*: sneaking food from the pot (impolite) (Central Thai แอบกินจากหม้อ)
- **เหมี้ยง** *mieng*: fermented tea leaves chewed after meals (Central Thai เมี่ยง)

### Khantok (raised food tray) and the khantok dinner (ขันโตก)
ขันโตก / โตก is the Lanna pedestal tray for a meal set: a round tray (30-100+ cm), wooden ribs ('waist') raising it 30-40 cm, and a foot. Sizes: ขันโตกหลวง (royal/temple, 60-120 cm), ขันโตกฮาม (40-60 cm, large households), ขันโตกหน้อย (25-37 cm, individuals or newly-weds). Materials: wood, woven bamboo, rattan; black lacquer for ordinary homes, red lacquer for monks and nobility. Diners sit on the floor around it. The 'khantok dinner' as a hospitality event was revived in 1953 by Chiang Mai businessman Kraisri Nimmanhaeminda to welcome honoured guests and foreigners. Typical set: nam phrik num, nam phrik ong, sai ua, khaep mu, kaeng hang le, larb, fresh vegetables, with sticky rice; often with fawn leb dancing.

- **ขันโตก / โตก / สะโตก** *khantok / tok*: raised round food tray (Central Thai โต๊ะอาหารเตี้ย / ถาดสำรับ)
- **ขันโตกหน้อย** *khantok noi*: small khantok (Central Thai ขันโตกเล็ก)

### Side vegetables (ผักเคียง / ผักกับลาบ) (ผักเคียง ผักกับลาบ)
Northern chilli dips are 'paste-like, suited for dipping' (CMU) and always come with a plate of raw, blanched or steamed vegetables and often pork cracklings. Larb has its own plate (ผักกับลาบ): bitter, astringent, peppery and cooling leaves (e.g. ผักแปม, มะกอก shoots, Vietnamese coriander, mint, pennywort, cucumber, long beans) to balance the meat. Wild vegetables change with the season: hot season banana flower, tamarind leaves, young mango leaves; rainy season bamboo shoots, mushrooms, ผักหวาน; paddy fields morning glory, water clover.

- **ผักกับลาบ** *phak kap laap*: vegetable/herb plate eaten with larb (Central Thai ผักแกล้มลาบ)
- **ผักนึ่ง / ผักลวก** *phak nueng / phak luak*: steamed / blanched vegetables served with dips (Central Thai ผักนึ่ง/ผักลวก)

### What northern food tastes like (รสชาติอาหารเหนือ)
Lanna food 'inclines towards a little salty and spicy hot', rarely uses coconut milk, and curries use little liquid (CMU). Dips are thick, almost pastes. Sweetness is low compared with Central Thai food. The numbing 'เด้าลิ้น' sensation from makhwaen is a signature. Umami comes from fermented soybean, crab paste and fermented fish rather than coconut and sugar.

### Larb, luck and occasion (ลาบกับความเชื่อ)
Many Northerners associate larb with good fortune because ลาบ sounds like ลาภ ('luck, gain'). Larb is a special-occasion dish; being treated to larb is an honour. A skilled larb maker is a สล่าลาบ (sala laap, 'larb master'), and families keep their own spice-mix recipe. Beliefs vary by family and are not universal.

- **สล่าลาบ** *sala laap*: larb master (Central Thai ช่างทำลาบ)

### Spoon, fork or hand? (ช้อนส้อมกับมือ)
Sticky rice is eaten by hand, unlike jasmine rice which is eaten with spoon and fork (spoon in the right hand, fork used only to push food onto the spoon). At a northern meal you typically use your hand for the sticky rice and dips, a shared serving spoon to move curry into your own bowl, and a spoon to sip curry. Noodle dishes such as khao soi are eaten with spoon and chopsticks.

### Markets (กาด) and how to shop (กาด ตลาดล้านนา)
กาด (kat) = market. Kinds: กาดก้อม (small), กาดเช้า (morning), กาดแลง (evening), กาดหลวง (the city's big market; in Chiang Mai, Warorot), กาดงัว (originally cattle market), กาดหมั้ว (formerly morning-only, now all day). Shopping map for cooks: Warorot (Kad Luang) for ready nam phrik, sai ua, khaep mu and dry spices; Ton Lamyai opposite for fresh vegetables, fruit, meat; Kad Muang Mai by the Ping River for wholesale vegetables and fruit from about 2 am to daybreak.

- **กาด** *kat*: market (Central Thai ตลาด)
- **กาดหลวง** *kat luang*: big central market (Central Thai ตลาดใหญ่ (ตลาดวโรรส))
- **กาดแลง** *kat laeng*: evening market (Central Thai ตลาดเย็น)
- **กาดก้อม** *kat kom*: small market (Central Thai ตลาดเล็ก)

### Market and table phrases
*เจ้า (jao) is the soft polite ending used all over the North. Phrases marked (unverified) were not quoted from a source; check them with a local.*

- **อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า** *an ni ki bat jao*: How much is this?. at a stall; เจ้า is the polite Northern particle (widely used by women, and by anyone to be warm; men may use ครับ).
- **เต้าใดเจ้า** *tao dai jao*: How much? (unverified). asking a price.
- **เอา...เจ้า** *ao ... jao*: I'll take .... ordering/buying (e.g. เอาแก๋งอังเล).
- **บ่เอาเจ้า** *bo ao jao*: No thanks / I don't want it. declining.
- **ปอแล้วเจ้า** *po laeo jao*: That's enough. when a vendor is filling a bag, or a host refills your plate.
- **ลำขนาดเจ้า** *lam khanat jao*: It's really delicious!. complimenting the cook.
- **จะฮับอะหยังดีจ้าว** *ja hap a yang di jao*: What would you like? (what the vendor says). you will hear this.
- **เก็บตังค์ตวยเจ้า** *kep tang tuai jao*: The bill, please. paying.
- **บ่เผ็ดเน้อ** *bo phet noe*: Not spicy, OK? (unverified). ordering.
- **กิ๋นข้าวแล้วกา** *kin khao laeo ka*: Have you eaten? (greeting) (unverified). greeting.

## 3. Recipes
### Steamed sticky rice, Lanna style: ข้าวเหนียวนึ่ง (ข้าวนึ่ง (เข้านึ่ง))
**Say it:** khao (44ʔ) · nueng (42). Say 'khao' short and clipped, then 'nueng' falling. Central Thai says ข้าวเหนียว khao niao; in the North the everyday word is ข้าวนึ่ง ('steamed rice').

*Serves:* 4 (home scale: 500 g raw rice); CMU's household scale is 2 litres of raw rice · *Season:* all year (new-crop rice after the Oct-Dec harvest is prized) · *Occasion:* every meal

The staple of every Lanna meal. Rice is soaked (หม่า), steamed in a wooden or woven steamer over boiling water, turned out to release steam, then kept warm in a woven rice box all day.

**Ingredients**
- 500 g (CMU: 2 L) **Thai glutinous (sticky) rice** (ข้าวเหนียว, คำเมือง ข้าวนึ่ง (ดิบ)). buy: Warorot (Kad Luang) / Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot) rice shops, or any supermarket (5 kg sacks); choose: Opaque, chalky-white long grains; 'new rice' (ข้าวใหม่) cooks softer and needs shorter soaking. Sacks often name the variety (e.g. สันป่าตอง).; abroad: Thai 'glutinous' or 'sweet' rice from Asian grocers. NOT jasmine, sushi or risotto rice.
- about 3 L in the pot (CMU) + soaking water **Water** (น้ำ). 

**Equipment:** Conical woven-bamboo or wooden sticky-rice steamer (หวด / ไหนึ่งข้าว), or use: A metal steamer basket or sieve lined with muslin/cheesecloth; Tall narrow-mouthed pot (หม้อนึ่งข้าวเหนียว (หม้อทรงสูงปากแคบ)), or use: Any pot the basket sits snugly on; Strip of wet cloth to seal pot and steamer (ผ้าชุบน้ำพันรอยต่อ), or use: Wet tea towel; Tray and wooden paddle for turning the rice (ถาด / กระด้ง และไม้พาย), or use: Large plate + wooden spoon; Woven lidded rice box (ก่องข้าว / กระติ๊บ), or use: Insulated lunch box lined with a clean cloth

**Ahead:** Soak the rice: overnight is traditional (CMU says 2-3 hours to overnight); for new-crop rice Wongnai found 1 hour gave the best texture, and old rice needs longer.

**Method**
1. Rinse the rice in several changes of water, rubbing gently, until the water runs clear. (~5 min). *Look for:* Water goes from milky to almost clear; rice smells clean. *Tip:* CMU: rinse 'until the water runs clear and smells fresh'.
2. Soak (หม่า) the rice covered by a few cm of water: 1-3 h for new rice, overnight for older rice. (~180 min). *Look for:* A grain crushes easily between fingernails and is opaque white right through. *Tip:* Over-soaking new rice makes grains fragile and mushy (Wongnai test).
3. Bring about 3 L water to a boil. Wet the steamer basket, drain the rice and spread it in the basket. (~5 min). *Look for:* Rice sits loosely, not packed down. *Tip:* Wet basket = rice won't stick to it (CMU).
4. Set the basket on the pot, seal the joint with a wet cloth strip, cover and steam about 20 minutes. If using a Lao/Isan-style open cone, flip the rice mass over once halfway. (~20 min). *Look for:* Steam comes up through the rice; grains turn glossy and translucent. *Tip:* The water must not touch the rice.
5. Test: grains are soft with no hard centre, and a pinch does not stick to your damp hand. (~1 min). *Look for:* Chewy-soft, translucent, sweet smell. *Tip:* If hard-centred, sprinkle a little water and steam 5 more minutes.
6. Sprinkle a little water on a tray, tip the rice out and turn/fold it with a wooden paddle several times to let steam escape. (~2 min). *Look for:* Clouds of steam; surface loses its wet sheen. *Tip:* This stops the rice from going soggy (CMU).
7. Pack loosely into a woven rice box lined with a white cloth; close the lid. (~1 min). *Look for:* Stays warm and soft for hours. *Tip:* Traditionally rice is steamed once, in the morning, for the whole day (Matichon).

**Taste:** Neutral and faintly sweet; the vehicle for salty-spicy dips and meats. Texture: Chewy, springy, sticky enough to hold a ball but not wet. (estimated scores out of 5: sour 0, salty 0, spicy 0, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 0). Words: ตั๋ง *tang* (sticky (Chiang Rai source glosses ตั๋ง = เหนียว))

**How to eat it:** Take a small piece from the shared basket with your right hand, squeeze it into a bite-size ball, use it to dip into chilli dip or pinch up meat and vegetables. With: น้ำพริกหนุ่ม, น้ำพริกอ่อง, ลาบ, ไส้อั่ว, แคบหมู, แกง. When: every meal: ข้าวงาย / ข้าวตอน / ข้าวแลง. Etiquette: One rice basket is shared around the circle; close the lid between helpings so it stays warm; let elders start first.

**Food safety:** Cooked rice left warm for many hours can grow Bacillus cereus; eat within the day, refrigerate leftovers and re-steam until piping hot.

**Common mistakes:** Boiling sticky rice in a rice cooker with water like jasmine rice (it turns to paste); Not soaking long enough (hard white centres); Letting boiling water touch the rice; Leaving it in the steamer after cooking (soggy); Rolling rice into a big ball in the palm and putting it back in the shared basket.

**Useful phrases (unverified, assembled from Kham Mueang vocabulary):** เอาข้าวนึ่งเพิ่มเจ้า *ao khao nueng poem jao*: More sticky rice, please; ข้าวนึ่งกระติ๊บนึ่ง *khao nueng kratip nueng*: One basket of sticky rice

**Culture:** The Lanna steamer is the ไหเข้า (hai khao), carved from teak or other wood, made from a bamboo tube, or woven like the Isan หวด, with a perforated board (ตาดไห) in its base. A folk sizing custom counts 'full-hungry' (อิ่ม-อยาก) thumb-widths from base to rim, hoping to end on 'full'. Sticky rice also appears in soul-calling (เรียกขวัญ) rites.

### Roasted green chilli dip: น้ำพริกหนุ่ม (น้ำพริกหนุ่ม (น้ำพิกหนุ่ม))
**Say it:** nam (45) · phik (45) · num (21). Northerners usually drop the r in พริก ('phik'). หนุ่ม = young.

*Serves:* 4 as a dip · *Season:* all year · *Occasion:* everyday; khantok dinners; the classic Chiang Mai souvenir

The signature northern dip: long green chillies, shallots and garlic roasted until soft and blistered, peeled, pounded coarsely and seasoned simply with salt. Variants add fermented fish, banana-leaf-roasted shrimp paste, fish sauce or dried fish. Smoky, salty-sweet, only mildly hot.

**Ingredients**
- 5 (CMU) to 10-15 (Baan Lae Suan) chillies **Long green northern chillies** (พริกหนุ่ม). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot) or Muang Mai wholesale market by the Ping River vegetable stalls; any morning market; choose: Firm, glossy, pale yellow-green, straight and long; no soft spots.; abroad: Anaheim, Hungarian wax or long Turkish green peppers (+1 jalapeño for heat).
- 5 (CMU) **Shallots** (หอมแดง). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); choose: Small, firm, dry skins.; abroad: Small shallots
- 8 cloves (CMU) **Thai garlic** (กระเทียมไทย). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); choose: Small-cloved Thai garlic.; abroad: Garlic, fewer cloves
- ¼ tsp (CMU) to 1 tsp **Salt** (เกลือ). buy: any shop; abroad: Sea salt
- ½ Tbsp each (CMU) **Coriander leaf and spring onion, chopped** (ผักชี (หอมป้อม), ต้นหอม, คำเมือง หอมป้อม, ต้นหอม). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); choose: Perky, not wilted.; abroad: Same
- to taste (≈1 tsp) **Optional umami: fermented fish, roasted shrimp paste, fish sauce or dried fish** (ปลาร้า / กะปิห่อใบตองเผา / น้ำปลา / ปลาแห้ง (ไม่บังคับ), คำเมือง ปลาฮ้า). buy: Warorot (Kad Luang); choose: For raw use choose cooked/bottled pla ra sauce.; abroad: Fish sauce or a little anchovy paste

**Equipment:** Mortar and pestle (clay or granite) (ครกดินเผา / ครกหิน กับสาก), or use: Heavy granite mortar; a small food processor on pulse (texture will be smoother); Charcoal grill, or a dry cast-iron pan (เตาถ่าน หรือกระทะเหล็ก), or use: Gas flame, grill pan, or oven broiler; Skewers or grill rack (ไม้เสียบ / ตะแกรงย่าง), or use: Tongs

**Ahead:** Nothing to soak. Start the sticky rice first; steam or blanch the side vegetables while the chillies roast.

**Method**
1. Roast the whole chillies, unpeeled shallots and garlic over charcoal (or in a dry pan) until soft, turning often. (~12 min). *Look for:* Chilli skins blister and char in patches and the chillies go limp; shallots/garlic give when squeezed; smoky-sweet smell. *Tip:* Charcoal gives the best aroma (CMU). In a pan, Baan Lae Suan adds a splash of water to spread heat.
2. Peel off the burnt skins of chillies, shallots and garlic. Pull out seeds if you want it milder. (~5 min). *Look for:* Flesh is soft and bright green underneath; a few black flecks are fine. *Tip:* Seeds hold most of the heat (CMU tip).
3. Pound the shallots and garlic with the salt, then add the chillies and pound just until broken down and roughly combined. (~5 min). *Look for:* A coarse, juicy, pale-green mash with visible chilli strands, not a smooth purée. *Tip:* The101 describes the texture as pounded only roughly ('ตำพอโผละเผละ').
4. Season: taste and adjust salt; add the optional fermented fish, roasted shrimp paste or fish sauce. (~2 min). *Look for:* Salty first, then sweet-smoky, then gentle heat. *Tip:* Traditional versions don't use lime; one Wongnai recipe adds 2 Tbsp lime juice. That is a modern variation.
5. Spoon into a bowl, scatter coriander and spring onion. (~1 min).

**Taste:** Salty, sweet and balanced with a prominent roasted aroma; not very hot (Michelin). Texture: Coarse, soft, juicy mash. (estimated scores out of 5: sour 0, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 2, bitter 0, umami 2). Words: ลำ *lam* (delicious), เผ็ดหน้อยเดียว *phet noi diao* (only a little spicy (assembled phrase))

**How to eat it:** Dip a ball of sticky rice, a steamed vegetable or a piece of pork crackling into it. With: ข้าวนึ่ง, ผักนึ่ง/ผักลวก (e.g. cabbage, long beans, pumpkin, eggplant), ผักสด (cucumber), แคบหมู, ไส้อั่ว, ไข่ต้ม. When: any meal; picnics; khantok. Etiquette: One bowl in the middle for everyone; dip, don't spoon it onto your rice pile; don't double-dip a bitten piece of crackling.

**Food safety:** Uncooked fermented fish (ปลาร้าดิบ) can carry parasites (liver fluke); use cooked/bottled pasteurised pla ra. Keep made dip refrigerated and eat within a few days.

**Common mistakes:** Blending it smooth; Peeling chillies before they are fully soft (skins won't come off, raw taste); Adding sugar or lime by default; Expecting it to be fiery; heat depends on the chillies; Using bell peppers (no heat, too watery).

**Useful phrases (unverified, assembled from Kham Mueang vocabulary):** น้ำพริกหนุ่มขีดนึ่งเจ้า *nam phik num khit nueng jao*: 100 g of nam phrik num, please; เผ็ดก่อเจ้า *phet ko jao*: Is it spicy?; ใส่ปลาฮ้าก่อ *sai pla ha ko*: Does it have fermented fish?

**Culture:** A top Chiang Mai souvenir. The four long-running Kad Luang makers differ: Damrong adds fermented fish; Naem Boon Sri and Je Hong add dried fish; Sri Phan uses only chilli, garlic and seasoning (vegetarian-friendly). Around 200 baht/kg (Wongnai, date unknown). The nam phrik num + pork crackling pairing is called inseparable (Michelin).

### Northern pork and tomato chilli dip: น้ำพริกอ่อง (น้ำพริกอ่อง (น้ำพริกบะเขือส้ม))
**Say it:** nam (45) · phik (45) · ong (21). อ่อง is low-falling: 'ong' with a dip. Some sellers call it น้ำพริกมะเขือส้ม (tomato chilli dip).

*Serves:* 4-6 as a dip · *Season:* all year · *Occasion:* everyday; khantok; picnics

A reddish, chunky dip of dried-chilli paste fried with minced pork and small sour tomatoes, simmered until the liquid reduces. Chiang Mai News glosses อ่อง as a way of stir-frying and simmering a chilli dip until it slowly reduces. Sour, salty, spicy and slightly sweet (CMU); milder than most dips.

**Ingredients**
- 400 g (CMU) **Minced pork with some fat** (หมูสับติดมัน, คำเมือง จิ๊นหมูสับ). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot) or Warorot (Kad Luang) pork stalls (ask them to mince); choose: Fresh pink meat, a little white fat.; abroad: Pork mince 15-20% fat
- 200 g (CMU); Wongnai uses 20 cherry tomatoes **Small sour tomatoes / cherry tomatoes** (มะเขือเทศลูกเล็ก / มะเขือส้ม, คำเมือง บะเขือส้ม). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot) / Muang Mai wholesale market by the Ping River; choose: Ripe red-orange, tart; local ones are thumb-tip-size.; abroad: Cherry tomatoes (+ squeeze of lime if very sweet)
- 20 g (CMU); Wongnai uses 5 large **Dried bird's-eye chillies (soaked)** (พริกแห้งเม็ดเล็ก, คำเมือง พริกแห้ง). buy: Warorot (Kad Luang) dry-goods stalls; choose: Bright red, not dusty-brown; soak 10 min in warm water.; abroad: Dried Thai chillies; for milder use guajillo
- 5 g as printed by CMU (many cooks use 2-5 shallots) **Shallots** (หอมแดง). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); choose: Firm; abroad: Shallots
- 10 cloves **Garlic** (กระเทียม). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); choose: Firm; abroad: Garlic
- 1 Tbsp (CMU); Wongnai ½ Tbsp **Shrimp paste** (กะปิ). buy: Warorot (Kad Luang); choose: Fragrant, purple-brown, not dried out.; abroad: Shrimp paste / belacan
- 1 disc **Fermented soybean disc, toasted (optional, traditional)** (ถั่วเน่าแข็บ (ไม่บังคับ), คำเมือง ถั่วเน่าแข็บ). buy: Warorot (Kad Luang); choose: Thin, dry, not mouldy; toast before pounding.; abroad: A teaspoon of miso
- ½ tsp **Salt** (เกลือ). buy: any shop
- 2 Tbsp **Vegetable oil** (น้ำมันพืช, คำเมือง น้ำมัน). buy: any shop
- about ½ cup (Wongnai) **Water** (น้ำเปล่า, คำเมือง น้ำ). 
- 1 Tbsp each **Chopped coriander & spring onion** (ผักชี ต้นหอม, คำเมือง หอมป้อม ต้นหอม). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot)

**Equipment:** Mortar and pestle (clay or granite) (ครกดินเผา / ครกหิน กับสาก), or use: Heavy granite mortar; a small food processor on pulse (texture will be smoother); Wok or heavy pan (กระทะ), or use: Frying pan; Spatula (ตะหลิว), or use: Wooden spoon

**Ahead:** Soak dried chillies 10 min in warm water. Toast the soybean disc (if using) over a low flame until pale and crumbly. Steam sticky rice; steam or blanch vegetables.

**Method**
1. Pound drained chillies with salt, then shallots, garlic, shrimp paste (and toasted soybean disc) to a fine paste. (~10 min). *Look for:* Smooth, brick-red, sticky paste; strong garlicky-shrimp smell. *Tip:* Pound hard, dry ingredients first, wet ones last.
2. Heat oil over medium heat and fry the paste, stirring. (~3 min). *Look for:* Paste darkens slightly, oil turns orange, raw garlic smell becomes toasty. *Tip:* Don't let it scorch.
3. Add the pork, breaking it up, and fry until almost cooked. (~5 min). *Look for:* No pink left; pork coated red.
4. Add halved or crushed tomatoes and a little water; stir and simmer until the tomatoes collapse and the mixture is thick with just a little liquid. (~12 min). *Look for:* Tomatoes burst and melt into a chunky red sauce; surface glistens with orange oil; bubbling slows ('อ่อง'). *Tip:* This slow reduction is the 'ong' step.
5. Taste, adjust salt (or fish sauce; some add a pinch of sugar). Serve topped with coriander and spring onion. (~2 min). *Look for:* Balanced sour-salty-spicy with a touch of sweetness. *Tip:* Wongnai: skip sugar if you don't like sweet.

**Taste:** Sour, salty, spicy and sweet combined (CMU); like a spicy, funky Bolognese. Texture: Chunky meat-and-tomato relish, thick, a little oily. (estimated scores out of 5: sour 3, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 4). Words: ส้ม *som* (sour), ลำขนาด *lam khanat* (really delicious)

**How to eat it:** Scoop with a ball of sticky rice, cucumber, blanched cabbage or long beans, or pork crackling; can also be mixed into hot jasmine rice. With: ข้าวนึ่ง, ผักสด (แตงกวา, ถั่วฝักยาว), ผักต้ม/ผักนึ่ง, แคบหมู. When: lunch or dinner; khantok. Etiquette: Shared bowl; take a scoop onto your vegetable or rice ball rather than eating from the spoon.

**Food safety:** Cook pork through (no pink). Refrigerate leftovers; reheat until bubbling. Keeps 2-3 days chilled.

**Common mistakes:** Adding too much water so it becomes a soup; Using large watery salad tomatoes without reducing; Adding coconut milk (not northern); Burning the paste on high heat.

**Useful phrases (unverified, assembled from Kham Mueang vocabulary):** น้ำพริกอ่องมีก่อเจ้า *nam phik ong mi ko jao*: Do you have nam phrik ong?; บ่ใส่น้ำต้านเจ้า *bo sai nam tan jao*: No sugar, please

**Culture:** One Thai retailer's blog tells a legend that the name comes from a Burmese man called 'Uang Muang' who created it; treat as folklore, unverified. Traditional home versions may add toasted fermented soybean disc (Chiang Mai News) or fermented fish (Vanusnun).

### Chilli dip with northern crab paste: น้ำพริกน้ำปู๋ (น้ำพริกน้ำปู๋)
**Say it:** nam (45) · phik (45) · nam (45) · pu (24). ปู๋ rises (low-rising 24); Northerners write the ๋ to show that.

*Serves:* 2-4 · *Season:* all year from stored paste; new paste made Sept-Oct · *Occasion:* home meals, especially with bamboo shoots in the rainy season

A thick, almost dry dip: fresh chillies, garlic, shallots and lemongrass pounded, then mixed with nam pu, the black paste made by pounding rice-field crabs with herbs, straining and simmering down for a day. Intense, earthy, salty.

**Ingredients**
- 1 Tbsp (CMU; start with 1-2 tsp) **Northern crab paste** (น้ำปู๋). buy: Warorot (Kad Luang) northern condiment stalls (small jars); choose: Glossy black, thick; ask where it's from (Nan, Chiang Rai, Lampang are known) and when made.; abroad: No true substitute; tiny amount of shrimp paste + dark soy
- 20 bird chillies (CMU) / 7 roasted phrik num (Wongnai) **Fresh bird's-eye chillies (or roasted phrik num for a milder version)** (พริกขี้หนูสด (หรือพริกหนุ่มเผา), คำเมือง พริกขี้หนู). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); choose: Firm, shiny; abroad: Thai bird chillies
- 10 cloves (CMU) **Garlic** (กระเทียม). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); abroad: Garlic
- 5 (CMU) **Shallots** (หอมแดง). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); abroad: Shallots
- 1 Tbsp (CMU) **Lemongrass, thinly sliced** (ตะไคร้ซอย, คำเมือง ไคร้). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot); choose: Fat, pale, fragrant stalks; abroad: Lemongrass
- ½ tsp (CMU) **Salt** (เกลือ). 
- to taste **Fish sauce / a little warm water (optional)** (น้ำปลา / น้ำอุ่น (ไม่บังคับ)). 

**Equipment:** Mortar and pestle (clay or granite) (ครกดินเผา / ครกหิน กับสาก), or use: Heavy granite mortar; a small food processor on pulse (texture will be smoother); Dry pan (for the roasted version) (กระทะ (ถ้าคั่ว)), or use: Any heavy pan

**Ahead:** For the roasted version (Cookpad/Wongnai), dry-roast garlic, shallots and chillies until lightly charred first.

**Method**
1. Pound salt, lemongrass, garlic and shallots to a fine paste. (~6 min). *Look for:* Fibrous lemongrass fully broken; fragrant, pale paste. *Tip:* Salt helps grind the lemongrass.
2. Add the chillies and keep pounding. (~3 min). *Look for:* Chilli broken into small flecks; sharp chilli smell (keep face back). *Tip:* Wash hands after; don't touch eyes.
3. Add the crab paste and pound lightly to combine. (~2 min). *Look for:* Dip turns dark grey-black and thick, almost dry. *Tip:* Add warm water a few drops at a time only if too stiff.
4. Taste; add fish sauce if not salty enough. Spoon into a bowl. (~1 min). *Look for:* Very salty-earthy first, then chilli heat, herbal finish. *Tip:* Nam pu is strong: underseason first.

**Taste:** Deeply savoury, earthy crab-and-herb aroma, salty and hot. Texture: Thick, sticky, nearly dry paste. (estimated scores out of 5: sour 0, salty 4, spicy 4, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 5). Words: เก็ม *kem* (salty), เผ็ด *phet* (spicy)

**How to eat it:** A little goes a long way: dab it on boiled bamboo shoots, blanched cabbage/carrot, boiled egg or pork crackling, with sticky rice. With: หน่อไม้ต้ม, ผักลวก, ไข่ต้ม, แคบหมู, ข้าวนึ่ง. When: home meals; rainy season with fresh bamboo shoots. Etiquette: Shared small bowl; take tiny amounts.

**Food safety:** Nam pu is long-simmered, but it is made from field crabs; buy from a reputable seller, keep sealed and refrigerated after opening. Bamboo shoots must be boiled well before eating.

**Common mistakes:** Using too much nam pu; Confusing น้ำปู๋ with Central Thai crab-meat dips (น้ำพริกปู); Eating bamboo shoots raw.

**Useful phrases (unverified, assembled from Kham Mueang vocabulary):** น้ำปู๋มาจากไหนเจ้า *nam pu ma jak nai jao*: Where is this crab paste from?; ใส่น้ำปู๋หน้อยเดียว *sai nam pu noi diao*: Just a little crab paste

**Culture:** Nam pu is made once a year in the rainy season (about Sept-Oct) when field crabs are fully grown: crabs are pounded with lemongrass and turmeric leaves (some areas add guava or hog-plum leaves), squeezed, strained, rested overnight, and simmered on low heat for a long time until black and thick. It belongs to the family of fermented/concentrated seasonings like shrimp paste.

### Northern pork cracklings: buying, serving (and the traditional method): แคบหมู (แคบหมู)
**Say it:** kaep (42) · mu (24). Northern ค is unaspirated, so it sounds closer to 'gaep'.

*Serves:* a 100-200 g bag serves 4 as a side · *Season:* all year · *Occasion:* side dish with dips; snack; khantok; souvenir

แคบ = animal skin/fat rendered until crisp. Styles: ไร้มัน (skin only, airy), ติดมัน (skin with a layer of fat and meat, rich and crunchy), มันล้วน (all fat). Most Northerners buy it from a favourite shop rather than make it; the CMU method is given for reference.

**Ingredients**
- 100-200 g **Ready-made pork cracklings** (แคบหมูสำเร็จรูป, คำเมือง แคบหมู). buy: Warorot (Kad Luang) (Wongnai names Damrong, Je Hong, Sri Phan, Naem Boon Sri); Ton Phayom market; airport/souvenir shops; choose: Freshly fried that day, pale golden, light for its size, no stale oil smell; ask to taste. ติดมัน for dips and sai ua, ไร้มัน for noodles or lighter eating.; abroad: Chicharrón or pork scratchings (UK).
- 2 kg (CMU) **Pork skin with minimal fat (for making)** (หนังหมู (สำหรับทำเอง), คำเมือง หนังหมู). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot) pork stalls; choose: CMU: skin from the back or thigh, must smell fresh.; abroad: Pork rind from a butcher
- ½ Tbsp (CMU) **Dark (black) soy sauce** (ซีอิ๊วดำ). buy: any shop; abroad: Dark soy sauce
- 1 tsp **Salt** (เกลือ). 
- 2 kg (CMU) **Pork lard** (น้ำมันหมู). buy: Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot) / Warorot (Kad Luang); abroad: Lard or high-smoke-point oil

**Equipment:** Large deep wok (กระทะใบใหญ่), or use: Heavy deep pot; Spider strainer (กระบอง/ตะแกรงตัก), or use: Slotted spoon; Airtight container (ภาชนะปิดสนิท), or use: Jar/zip bag

**Ahead:** If buying: nothing. If making (CMU method), cut skin into 1 × 6 inch strips and marinate 10 minutes in dark soy.

**Method**
1. (Buying) Choose ติดมัน or ไร้มัน and ask to taste a piece; buy by the bag or weight. (~5 min). *Look for:* Shatters audibly; clean porky flavour. *Tip:* Buy on the day you'll eat it; it softens in humidity.
2. (Making, CMU) Cut skin into 1 × 6 inch pieces; marinate with dark soy 10 minutes. (~10 min). *Look for:* Evenly tan.
3. Put the skin in melted lard over very low heat for about 30 minutes. (~30 min). *Look for:* Skin tightens and stretches taut; small steady bubbles. *Tip:* Low heat dries the skin without browning.
4. Lift out, drain and cool 10 minutes. (~10 min). *Look for:* Skin looks translucent and leathery.
5. Fry in very hot oil until puffed, crisp and golden. (~5 min). *Look for:* Loud crackling as pieces puff up to double size; golden-brown. *Tip:* Stand back: hot fat spits violently.
6. Cool completely and store airtight. (~15 min). *Look for:* Stays crisp. *Tip:* CMU: raw skin keeps up to 15 days refrigerated.

**Taste:** Crunchy, rich and lightly salty; balances the heat of the dips. Texture: ไร้มัน: airy, shattering; ติดมัน: crunchy skin with a chewy-rich fat layer. (estimated scores out of 5: sour 0, salty 2, spicy 0, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 3). Words: มัน *man* (rich/fatty (also 'tasty-rich')), ลำ *lam* (delicious)

**How to eat it:** Use a piece like a chip to scoop nam phrik num or ong, alternate with sticky rice and vegetables; crumble over khanom jin nam ngiao. With: น้ำพริกหนุ่ม, น้ำพริกอ่อง, ข้าวนึ่ง, ผักนึ่ง, ไส้อั่ว, ขนมจีนน้ำเงี้ยว. When: any meal; snack. Etiquette: Put the bag or plate in the middle; don't double-dip a bitten piece into the shared dip.

**Food safety:** Deep-frying in large volumes of hot lard is dangerous (splatter, fire); never leave unattended, keep a lid nearby. Rich and fatty: eat in moderation.

**Common mistakes:** Buying hard, un-puffed rinds meant for cooking; Storing opened bags unsealed (goes soft in humid Chiang Mai); Frying wet skin (violent spitting).

**Useful phrases (unverified, assembled from Kham Mueang vocabulary):** เอาแคบหมูติดมันถุงนึ่งเจ้า *ao khaep mu tit man thung nueng jao*: One bag of pork crackling with fat, please; แคบหมูไร้มันมีก่อ *khaep mu rai man mi ko*: Do you have fatless crackling?; ขอจิมหน้อยได้ก่อเจ้า *kho jim noi dai ko jao*: May I taste a bit?

**Culture:** Khaep mu and sai ua are called a married couple ('คู่ผัวตัวเมีย') because both came from the same pig slaughter: skin for crackling, meat and intestine for sausage. Some Kad Luang crackling shops have operated for 30+ years. Wongnai's commercial method (boil skin 3 h, sun-dry, season, fry 2 h at medium then 50 min low) differs from CMU's lard method.

## Raw-meat and blood dishes: a safety note
Thailand's Department of Disease Control repeatedly warns people in the North and Northeast about *Streptococcus suis* infection (ไข้หูดับ) from raw pork and raw pork blood dishes such as ลาบดิบ and หลู้. Symptoms include high fever, severe headache, stiff neck and permanent hearing loss, and there are deaths every year. Order ลาบคั่ว (cooked larb), buy pork from inspected sources, and keep raw and cooked boards separate. Source: <https://www.ddc.moph.go.th/odpc7/news.php?news=18466&deptcode=odpc7>
