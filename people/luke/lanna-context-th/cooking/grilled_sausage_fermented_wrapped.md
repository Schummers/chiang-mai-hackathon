# Grilled, Sausage, Fermented and Leaf-Wrapped Dishes of Northern Thailand

A step-by-step guide for foreigners, compiled from Thai-language sources (retrieved 2026-09-26). Each dish gives the Thai name, the Kham Mueang (Northern Thai) name and a pronunciation guide.

> **About pronunciation.** Tone numbers are Chiang Mai Northern Thai tones: 24 low-rising, 21 low-falling, 44ʔ high with a glottal catch, 33 mid, 42 high-falling, 45 high-rising. They were *derived* from the Thai spelling with the standard Chiang Mai tone correspondences and have not been checked against native recordings, so treat them as a guide.

> **Raw pork warning.** Thailand's Department of Disease Control warns that raw pork, raw pork blood and raw fermented pork can transmit *Streptococcus suis* (ไข้หูดับ, 'deaf-ear fever'). Lime juice does not make meat safe. Every recipe here is cooked. Eat it cooked.

## Where to shop in Chiang Mai

- **Warorot / Kad Luang (กาดหลวง):** ready-made sai ua, jin som, khaep mu and nam phrik at souvenir stalls, plus chilli pastes and dry goods.
- **Muang Mai (กาดเมืองใหม่) and Ton Lamyai (กาดต้นลำไย):** fresh herbs, banana leaves, fish, chicken and pork stalls. Go early in the morning. Butchers sell casings, fresh blood and pork skin, and will mince or chop meat on request.
- **Nan OTOP shops:** kai (river-weed) sheets.

## Northern Thai herb sausage
**ไส้อั่ว**, Kham Mueang **ไส้อั่ว**, said *sai ua*: ไส้ *sai* (44ʔ) · อั่ว *ua* (21)

'sai' short and clipped with a catch in the throat, then 'ua' low and falling, like a relaxed 'oo-a'. ไส้ = intestine/casing, อั่ว = to stuff.

*From:* Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, whole Lanna region. *Occasion:* everyday side dish; souvenir food (ของฝาก); historically made when a pig was slaughtered for a festival, to use and keep the meat. *Serves:* 8-10 (about 1.3 kg filling). *Confidence:* high.

Pork casing stuffed with minced pork and belly pounded together with a Northern red chilli paste and handfuls of fresh herbs (lemongrass, makrut lime leaf, coriander, spring onion, sawtooth coriander), then slowly grilled over low charcoal. 'Ua' means 'to stuff'. MFU's Chiang Rai database describes it as a way to preserve and fully use pork after festival slaughtering; it keeps about 1-3 days.

**Ingredients**

- 10 pods large dried red chillies (พริกแห้งเม็ดใหญ่). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Guajillo or Kashmiri chillies
- 20 pods dried bird's eye chillies (พริกขี้หนูแห้ง). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Dried Thai chillies from Asian grocers; or chile de arbol
- 1 tbsp sea salt (เกลือทะเล). Buy at: any grocery. Abroad, use: Coarse sea salt
- 2 tbsp minced coriander (cilantro) root (รากผักชี). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Coriander stems
- 5 stalks, sliced lemongrass (ตะไคร้). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Frozen chopped lemongrass
- 1/4 cup Thai garlic (small cloves) (กระเทียมไทย). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Regular garlic, reduce slightly
- 7 bulbs, sliced shallots (หอมแดง). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Echalion/banana shallots
- 2 tsp minced fresh turmeric (ขมิ้นสด). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 kg minced pork (หมูบด). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning. Abroad, use: Pork shoulder mince (20% fat)
- 300 g pork belly, skinned and chopped (หมูสามชั้นลอกหนังสับ). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning. Abroad, use: Pork belly
- 2 tbsp seasoning sauce (ซอสปรุงรส). Buy at: any grocery. Abroad, use: Maggi seasoning or light soy sauce
- 1 1/2 tsp sugar (น้ำตาล). Buy at: any grocery. Abroad, use: Any sugar
- 1 tsp ground turmeric (ขมิ้นผง). Buy at: spice stalls. Abroad, use: Same
- 3 tbsp makrut lime leaves, finely shredded (ใบมะกรูดซอย). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Frozen makrut leaves (do not use regular lime leaves)
- 3 tbsp spring onion, sliced (ต้นหอมซอย). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Spring onion
- 3 tbsp coriander leaves, sliced (ผักชีซอย). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Coriander
- 3 tbsp sawtooth coriander (culantro), sliced (ผักชีฝรั่งซอย). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Extra coriander if unavailable
- 1/2 cup water (น้ำเปล่า). Abroad, use: Water
- 500 g small pork casings (ไส้หมูเล็ก). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning; ask 'ไส้อ่อน/ไส้หมูสำหรับทำไส้อั่ว'. Abroad, use: Natural hog casings (32-35 mm) from a butcher or online sausage-supply shop

**Prep ahead:** Clean the casings first (they take the longest). The filled sausage can rest in the fridge overnight, which firms it and lets flavours develop. Soak dried large chillies 10 min if they are brittle.

**Method**

1. Pound the large dried chillies and dried bird's eye chillies with the salt until fine. Add coriander root, lemongrass, garlic, shallots and fresh turmeric; pound together to a coarse paste. *What to look for:* Paste is brick-red, moist, with visible flecks of lemongrass; the kitchen smells strongly of lemongrass and toasted chilli. *Tip:* Pound the driest, hardest things first, wettest last - standard Thai paste order.
2. Combine both porks with the paste. Add seasoning sauce, sugar, ground turmeric, makrut leaf, spring onion, coriander, sawtooth coriander and water; mix thoroughly by hand. *What to look for:* Filling is evenly orange-yellow and slightly sticky. *Tip:* Fry a teaspoon of filling to taste and adjust salt before stuffing.
3. Wash the casings, peel off the fatty membrane, rinse several times squeezing out water, fat and slime. Rinse in salt water (4 cups water + 1 tbsp salt), then limewater (2 cups limewater + 2 cups water), then plain water. Drain. *What to look for:* Casings look translucent and smell neutral. *Tip:* Limewater (น้ำปูนใส) is slaked-lime water, not lime juice.
4. Tie one end of each casing. Open the other end with a finger (or funnel) and feed the filling in bit by bit, pushing out air. Tie off tightly. Prick air pockets with a pointed stick. *What to look for:* Sausage feels evenly firm but not drum-tight. *Tip:* Overstuffing makes casings burst on the grill. MFU's version makes 6-inch lengths; many cooks coil one long sausage.
5. Grill over low charcoal, turning often, until golden and cooked through. *What to look for:* Skin turns glossy golden-brown with a few charred spots; fat sizzles and drips; herbal smoke; juices run clear when pricked. *Tip:* Low heat is the key - high heat burns the casing before the centre cooks. MFU notes it can also be deep-fried; some cooks grill over coconut husk.
6. Slice into bite-size pieces and serve with fresh vegetables. *What to look for:* Cut surface is pinkish-orange with green herb flecks, no raw pink centre.

**Taste:** Dominated by lemongrass and makrut-lime aroma, medium chilli heat, salty-savoury with pork fat richness. Texture: Snappy casing, coarse juicy filling, lots of chewy herb bits. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 4 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Sliced into rounds and eaten with sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง khao nueng) rolled by hand, with raw cabbage, coriander, fresh chillies and thin slices of young ginger to cut the richness. Often served with nam phrik num and khaep mu on a khantok tray. Slices go on a shared plate; take one or two at a time with your rice ball rather than piling your own plate.

**Safety:** Cook fully - fresh pork filling is raw until grilled through. Clean casings well. Keep uncooked sausage refrigerated and cook within 1-3 days.

**Common mistakes:** Grilling on high heat (casing burns, centre raw); Using lean mince only (dry, crumbly); Substituting regular lime leaves or lime zest for makrut leaves; Eating it with a fork alone - it is designed to be eaten with sticky rice and raw vegetables; Forgetting young ginger, which locals use to cut the fat.

**Phrases:**

- ไส้อั่วลำแต๊ๆ เจ้า *sai ua lam tae tae jao*: "The sai ua is really delicious (polite)" (Central Thai: ไส้อั่วอร่อยมากค่ะ/ครับ; confidence medium)
- เอาไส้อั่วครึ่งกิโล *ao sai ua khrueng kilo*: "Half a kilo of sai ua, please" (Central Thai: ขอไส้อั่วครึ่งกิโล; confidence low)

**Culture:** The MFU Chiang Rai database links sai ua to festival pig slaughtering and preservation, and says it was once regarded as a relatively prestigious dish because of the labour involved. Today it is the classic souvenir food bought at Warorot and similar markets.

## Northern Thai fermented sour pork
**แหนมหมู (จิ๊นส้ม)**, Kham Mueang **จิ๊นส้ม**, said *jin som*: จิ๊น *jin* (45) · ส้ม *som* (44ʔ)

'jin' high and rising, 'som' short with a glottal catch. จิ๊น = meat, ส้ม = sour in Kham Mueang (Central Thai: เนื้อ + เปรี้ยว). krua.co notes many Northerners also just say แหนม (naem).

*From:* whole Lanna region. *Occasion:* preserving meat; everyday side dish, drinking snack, base for other dishes. *Serves:* makes about 2.5 kg (many parcels). *Confidence:* medium.

Lean pork, minced fine, mixed with thin strips of boiled pork skin, salt, garlic, a little cooked rice and whole bird's eye chillies, wrapped airtight in banana leaf or plastic and left to ferment until sour. Young jin som is still pinkish-red and mild; older jin som is paler, firmer and much sourer.

**Ingredients**

- 1.5 kg lean pork, finely minced (หมูเนื้อแดงบดละเอียด). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning. Abroad, use: Lean pork leg mince
- 1 kg boiled pork skin, cut into thin strips (หนังหมูต้มสุกหั่นเส้นบาง). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning; ready-boiled skin is often sold. Abroad, use: Pork rind from butcher, boiled 30-40 min then sliced
- 200 g garlic (กระเทียม). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Garlic
- 2 tbsp salt (เกลือ). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Non-iodised salt
- 1 tbsp steamed sticky rice, rinsed (ข้าวเหนียวนึ่ง (ล้างน้ำ)). Buy at: sticky-rice sellers in any market. Abroad, use: Cooked rice
- 100 g fresh bird's eye chillies (พริกขี้หนูสด). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Thai chillies
- enough to wrap banana leaves (ใบตอง). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Frozen banana leaves; or airtight plastic bags

**Prep ahead:** Boil and slice pork skin the day before. Plan 3-4 days of fermentation at warm room temperature before eating.

**Method**

1. Squeeze excess moisture out of the minced pork. *What to look for:* Meat is sticky and dense, not wet.
2. Slice the boiled pork skin into thin strands. *What to look for:* Thin, even, noodle-like strips.
3. Pound garlic with the salt, mix into the pork and skin, add the rinsed sticky rice and knead thoroughly; rest 30 minutes. *What to look for:* Mixture becomes tacky and holds together when squeezed. *Tip:* Knead well (Pim's related หมูส้ม method kneads about 5 min) so salt is evenly distributed.
4. Wrap tightly in banana leaves (tucking whole chillies in) or pack into bags, pressing out ALL air; tie firmly. *What to look for:* Parcels are hard and compact with no air pockets. *Tip:* krua.co stresses squeezing out air; air pockets lead to spoilage.
5. Leave at room temperature 3-4 days until it turns sour, then refrigerate. *What to look for:* Pleasant clean sour smell; colour shifts from red-pink to paler pink; texture firms. Off, putrid or slimy = discard. *Tip:* Refrigerate once sour to slow further souring.

**Taste:** Lactic sourness, salty and garlicky; new jin som is mild, old jin som sharply sour. Texture: Firm, springy meat with chewy strands of skin. (sour 4, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 3 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Locals mostly cook it: wrapped in banana leaf and roasted (jin som mok), with egg, or in kaeng phak plang (Malabar-spinach curry). Eaten with sticky rice, fresh chillies and raw vegetables. Shared plate; pick a piece with your sticky-rice ball.

**Safety:** Fermented pork is eaten raw by some people, but Thai health authorities specifically warn that raw แหนม carries Streptococcus suis risk, and a Department of Medical Sciences survey (2018, Northeast) found about 20% of community fermented products (naem, pla som) failed standards for food-poisoning bacteria. For visitors: eat it COOKED (grilled, mok, fried). At home: use very fresh inspected pork, clean hands and tools, correct salt, airtight packing, and refrigerate once sour.

**Common mistakes:** Assuming 'fermented = safe raw'; Leaving air in the parcel; Cutting the salt; Fermenting in the fridge from day one (it will not sour properly).

**Phrases:**

- จิ๊นส้มนี้ส้มแล้วกา *jin som ni som laeo ka*: "Is this jin som sour (ready) yet?" (Central Thai: แหนมนี้เปรี้ยวแล้วหรือยัง; confidence low)
- เอาจิ๊นส้มหมก *ao jin som mok*: "I'll have the leaf-roasted sour pork" (Central Thai: ขอแหนมหมก; confidence low)

**Culture:** A preservation technique to stop meat going to waste; it also appears as an ingredient in curries (e.g. kaeng phak plang jin som). Industrial versions exist; krua.co mentions modern factory processing aimed at safety.

## Sour pork roasted in banana leaf (optionally with egg)
**แหนมหมก / จิ๊นส้มหมก (หมกไข่)**, Kham Mueang **จิ๊นส้มหมก**, said *jin som mok*: จิ๊น *jin* (45) · ส้ม *som* (44ʔ) · หมก *mok* (21)

'mok' is short and low with a stopped 'k'. หมก = to bury/roast wrapped in hot ashes.

*From:* whole Lanna region. *Occasion:* everyday dish, drinking snack. *Serves:* 4. *Confidence:* high.

Jin som wrapped in several layers of banana leaf and cooked slowly on ash-covered embers or low charcoal until fragrant; a popular variation cracks an egg over it first.

**Ingredients**

- 180 g jin som with pork skin (แหนมผสมหนังหมู). Buy at: Warorot (Kad Luang) market, Northern-food souvenir stalls (e.g. the long-running sai ua / khaep mu / nam phrik shops reviewed on Wongnai) or naem stalls in any market. Abroad, use: Vietnamese nem chua or homemade jin som
- 1 per parcel egg (optional) (ไข่ไก่ (ไม่ใส่ก็ได้)). Buy at: egg stalls. Abroad, use: Egg
- 4-inch strips, several layers banana leaves (ใบตอง). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Frozen banana leaves; foil outside as last resort
- several bamboo pins (ไม้กลัด). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Toothpicks
- to serve small garden chillies (พริกขี้หนูสวน). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Thai chillies
- to serve coriander (ผักชี). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Coriander

**Prep ahead:** Cut and wipe banana leaves; soften over a flame if they crack.

**Method**

1. Wash hands, break up the jin som onto a plate.
2. Tear banana leaves into ~4-inch strips, layer them crosswise, add jin som (crack an egg over it if using), fold into a rectangle and pin. *Tip:* Multiple layers stop the leaf burning through.
3. Cook on low charcoal or on embers covered with ash until fragrant, turning occasionally. *What to look for:* Outer leaf is charred brown, inner leaf olive-green; smell of toasted leaf and sour pork; egg is set.
4. Unwrap, serve with chillies and coriander.

**Taste:** Sour and salty with a smoky toasted-banana-leaf aroma. Texture: Warm, softened sour pork; egg custardy. (sour 4, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 3 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Eat hot with sticky rice straight from the opened leaf. The opened parcel goes in the middle; everyone pinches a bit with their rice.

**Safety:** Cook until the pork is hot throughout and any egg is set; this is the safer way for visitors to enjoy jin som.

**Common mistakes:** Using a single leaf layer (burns); High heat; Eating the leaf.

**Phrases:**

- หมกเลยเจ้า *mok loei jao*: "Let's roast it (in leaves) now" (Central Thai: หมกเลยค่ะ; confidence low)

**Culture:** 'Mok' uses the residual heat of a cooking fire's embers - a thrifty village technique.

## Herb-pasted fish grilled flat in banana leaf
**แอ๊บปลา (แอ๊บปลานิล)**, Kham Mueang **แอ๊บป๋า**, said *aep pa*: แอ๊บ *aep* (45) · ปลา *pa* (24)

'aep' high, short, ends in unreleased 'p'. Northern speakers say ปลา as 'pa' (no l) - Wongnai's list writes ป๋า for fish. Spellings แอ๊บ / แอ็บ both occur.

*From:* whole Lanna region. *Occasion:* everyday home dish. *Serves:* 4. *Confidence:* high.

Aep means ingredients mixed with a pounded herb paste, folded flat in several layers of banana leaf, pinned with bamboo, and slowly grilled. Fish (tilapia, snakehead) is the most common.

**Ingredients**

- 500 g tilapia fillet (เนื้อปลานิล). Buy at: fish stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); ask them to fillet. Abroad, use: Any firm white fish (cod, basa, snapper)
- 1 tsp makrut lime zest (ผิวมะกรูด). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Makrut lime leaf, very finely shredded
- 3 heads garlic (กระเทียม). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Garlic
- 10 shallots (หอมแดง). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Shallots
- 7 stalks lemongrass (ตะไคร้). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Lemongrass
- 10 bird's eye chillies (พริกขี้หนู). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Thai chillies
- 1 tsp shrimp paste (กะปิ). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Shrimp paste
- 1 tbsp turmeric (ขมิ้น). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Fresh or 1 tsp ground
- 1 tsp fish sauce (น้ำปลา). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Fish sauce
- 1 tsp salt (เกลือ). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Salt
- a handful spring onion and coriander (ต้นหอม ผักชี). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- as needed banana leaves, bamboo pins (ใบตอง ไม้กลัด). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Frozen banana leaves, toothpicks

**Prep ahead:** Paste can be pounded a day ahead and chilled.

**Method**

1. Rinse the fish and cut into pieces.
2. Pound makrut zest and turmeric fine, then add lemongrass, chilli, shallot, garlic, shrimp paste and salt and pound to a paste. *What to look for:* Yellow-orange, fragrant paste.
3. Mix the paste with the fish, add spring onion, coriander and fish sauce.
4. Test: wrap 1 tbsp in a scrap of leaf, grill, taste, adjust seasoning. *Tip:* Matichon's cook recommends this test parcel.
5. Wrap flat in several leaf layers, pin, and grill over low heat until cooked, turning. *What to look for:* Leaf browns and blisters, juices bubble at the edges, strong lemongrass smell; fish is opaque and flakes. *Tip:* Charcoal gives the best aroma.

**Taste:** Turmeric- and lemongrass-scented, spicy, rounded saltiness from shrimp paste, smoky leaf aroma. Texture: Moist, flaky fish bound in soft herb paste. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 3 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Open the parcel at the table and eat with sticky rice and raw vegetables. One parcel is shared; pinch pieces with rice.

**Safety:** Cook until fish is opaque throughout. Freshwater fish in Northern Thailand can carry liver-fluke larvae if eaten raw - never eat under-cooked freshwater fish.

**Common mistakes:** Too few leaf layers; Fire too hot; Skipping the test parcel.

**Phrases:**

- แอ๊บป๋า มีกา *aep pa mi ka*: "Do you have aep pa?" (Central Thai: มีแอ๊บปลาไหม; confidence low)

**Culture:** Silpa-Wattanatham defines aep as folding food flat in multiple banana-leaf layers pinned with wood; aep is made with fish, pork, shrimp, pig brain and even flowers.

## Pig brain with herb paste grilled in banana leaf
**แอ๊บอ่องออ (แอ๊บสมองหมู)**, Kham Mueang **แอ๊บอ่องออ**, said *aep ong o*: แอ๊บ *aep* (45) · อ่อง *ong* (21) · ออ *o* (24)

'ong' low, then 'o' rising. อ่องออ = animal brain.

*From:* whole Lanna region. *Occasion:* everyday / market food; an adventurous dish for visitors. *Serves:* 2-3. *Confidence:* medium.

Fresh pig brain mixed with chilli, shallot, lemongrass, turmeric, galangal and shrimp paste, wrapped in banana leaf and grilled slowly; some cooks add egg to enrich it and reduce oiliness. Pig brain is preferred over beef brain for cost and availability.

**Ingredients**

- 2-3 brains (quantity not given in sources) pig brain (สมองหมู). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning; order a day ahead. Abroad, use: Hard to find abroad; soft tofu is a vegetarian stand-in (a 'แอ็บอ่องออเจ' made with tofu exists)
- to taste chilli, shallot, lemongrass, turmeric, galangal (พริก หอมแดง ตะไคร้ ขมิ้น ข่า). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- a little shrimp paste (กะปิ). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Shrimp paste
- 1 egg (optional) (ไข่ (ไม่ใส่ก็ได้)). Buy at: egg stall. Abroad, use: Egg
- several banana leaves (ใบตอง). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Frozen banana leaves

**Prep ahead:** Rinse brains gently in cold water and remove blood spots just before cooking.

**Method**

1. Pound chilli, shallot, lemongrass, turmeric, galangal and shrimp paste into a paste.
2. Gently fold the brain with the paste (and egg if using). *Tip:* Brain is very delicate - fold, don't mash.
3. Wrap in several layers of banana leaf, pin and grill over low heat until set. *What to look for:* Parcel firms up; contents set like a custard with no pink liquid. *Tip:* Cooking time is not stated in sources; cook until fully set.

**Taste:** Rich and creamy with a spicy herbal paste. Texture: Soft, creamy, custard-like. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 4 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Eaten hot with rice (Silpa-mag mentions steamed rice; sticky rice is also common). If offered, it is polite to try a small bit; declining politely is fine.

**Safety:** Offal must be very fresh and fully cooked.

**Common mistakes:** Mistaking it for tofu or fish custard - ask first if you avoid offal; Stirring hard and breaking the brain.

**Phrases:**

- อ่องออ แม่นอะหยัง *ong o maen a-yang*: "What is 'ong o'?" (Central Thai: อ่องออ คืออะไร; confidence low)

**Culture:** Silpa-Wattanatham calls it a Northern dish 'with a cute name'; it surprises many Thais from other regions too.

## Northern steamed curry parcel (no coconut milk)
**ห่อนึ่ง (ไก่ / ปลา)**, Kham Mueang **ห่อนึ่ง**, said *ho nueng*: ห่อ *ho* (21) · นึ่ง *nueng* (42)

'ho' low, 'nueng' high-falling. ห่อ = wrap, นึ่ง = steam. Central Thai ห่อหมก uses coconut milk; Northern ห่อนึ่ง does not.

*From:* whole Lanna region. *Occasion:* home cooking; temple offerings at big Buddhist festivals (Songkran, Buddhist Lent) according to Kham Phaka. *Serves:* 4-6. *Confidence:* medium.

Meat or fish mixed with a Northern chilli paste, the kind of mixed greens used for kaeng khae, glass noodles and toasted rice (or soaked raw rice pounded into the paste as a binder), laid on noni (ยอ) leaves, wrapped in banana leaf and steamed.

**Ingredients**

- about 500 g (quantities not given in sources) free-range chicken chopped on the bone, or catfish/snakehead pieces (ไก่บ้านสับทั้งกระดูก หรือปลาดุก/ปลาช่อนหั่นชิ้น). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning; chicken stalls will chop for you ('สับ'). Abroad, use: Chicken thighs on the bone; any firm fish
- paste for 500 g dried chilli, garlic, shallot, shrimp paste, salt (fish version adds galangal and lemongrass) (พริกแห้ง กระเทียม หอมแดง กะปิ เกลือ (ปลา: ข่า ตะไคร้)). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Same
- 2-3 tbsp soaked raw rice pounded into the paste, or toasted ground rice (ข้าวสารแช่ (ข้าวเบือ) หรือข้าวคั่ว). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Rice flour (for binding) or homemade toasted rice powder
- 2 cups mixed tender greens (ivy-gourd tips etc.) (ผักรวม (ยอดตำลึง ผักอ่อนตามสวน)). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles; ask for 'ผักแกงแค' bundles. Abroad, use: Spinach, pea shoots, baby kale
- 1 small bundle glass noodles, soaked (วุ้นเส้นแช่น้ำ). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Mung-bean glass noodles
- to line each parcel noni leaves (ใบยอ). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Hard to find abroad; young chard or cabbage leaf (flavour will differ)
- a handful dill and spring onion (ผักชีลาว ต้นหอม). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Dill, spring onion
- to taste fish sauce (น้ำปลา). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Fish sauce
- as needed banana leaves, pins (ใบตอง ไม้กลัด). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Frozen banana leaves; ramekins covered with foil

**Prep ahead:** Soak rice (for ข้าวเบือ) and glass noodles; wash and pick greens; soften banana leaves.

**Method**

1. Pound dried chilli, garlic and salt fine; add shallot and shrimp paste (and lemongrass, galangal, soaked rice for the fish version); pound smooth. *What to look for:* Thick, dark-red, fragrant paste.
2. Mix meat/fish with the paste, greens, noodles, toasted rice, dill, spring onion and fish sauce. *What to look for:* Everything is evenly coated red. *Tip:* Kham Phaka briefly stir-fries paste and chicken first until fragrant but not cooked; the Cookpad fish version mixes raw.
3. Line banana leaf with noni leaves, spoon in the mixture, fold like a sticky-rice-custard parcel and pin.
4. Steam until cooked (about 45-60 min for fish per Cookpad; chicken on the bone similar). *What to look for:* Parcels feel firm; opened, juices are clear-red, meat pulls from bone, fish flakes.

**Taste:** Gentle and rounded: toasted-rice aroma, vegetable sweetness, moderate chilli, slight bitterness from the noni leaf. Texture: Moist, dense, noodles and greens soaked in spicy juices. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 1, bitter 1, umami 3 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Open and eat with sticky rice. Kham Phaka suggests re-frying leftovers in lard with lots of garlic and herbs. At temple festivals, food is offered to monks first; lay people eat afterwards.

**Safety:** Chicken must be fully cooked (no pink at the bone). Steam long enough for bone-in pieces.

**Common mistakes:** Adding coconut milk (that makes Central ho mok); Skipping the toasted rice/rice binder (watery parcels); Under-steaming bone-in chicken.

**Phrases:**

- ห่อนึ่งไก่ มีกา *ho nueng kai mi ka*: "Do you have ho nueng kai?" (Central Thai: มีห่อนึ่งไก่ไหม; confidence low)

**Culture:** Writer Kham Phaka (The 101 World) describes ho nueng as a festival and temple-offering dish rarely on restaurant menus; leftovers from merit-making feasts go into kaeng ho (mixed re-fried curry).

Sources: https://www.the101.world/kab-khoa-kab-khaek-19/, https://cookpad.com/th/recipes/10919012, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_language

## Shan-style steamed rice with pork and pork blood in banana leaf
**ข้าวกั๊นจิ๊น (ข้าวเงี้ยว)**, Kham Mueang **ข้าวกั๊นจิ๊น**, said *khao kan jin*: ข้าว *khao* (44ʔ) · กั๊น *kan* (45) · จิ๊น *jin* (45)

Three short syllables; 'kan' and 'jin' both high. กั๊น = to knead/squeeze, จิ๊น = meat.

*From:* Mae Hong Son, Chiang Mai, Shan (Tai Yai) communities across the North. *Occasion:* lunch (ข้าวตอน) or snack; market food. *Serves:* 3-4. *Confidence:* high.

A Tai Yai (Shan) dish adopted across the North: cooked jasmine (non-glutinous) rice kneaded with pork blood and minced pork, seasoned, wrapped in banana leaf and steamed; served drizzled with garlic oil and topped with fried garlic, fried dried chillies, shallot and cucumber.

**Ingredients**

- 3 cups (or 1 L raw rice per KPRU) cooked jasmine rice (ข้าวสวย (ข้าวเจ้า)). Buy at: cook it yourself. Abroad, use: Jasmine rice
- 1 cup / 200 g minced pork (หมูบด). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning. Abroad, use: Pork mince
- 1 cup / 200 g fresh pork blood (เลือดหมูสด). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning; sold in bags at pork stalls - ask 'เลือดหมูสด'. Abroad, use: Fresh pork blood from Asian butchers; otherwise this dish can't be made authentically
- a handful lemongrass leaves (ใบตะไคร้). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Lemongrass stalks, bruised
- optional boiled pork skin, small sour tomatoes (some versions) (หนังหมูต้ม, มะเขือส้ม (บางสูตร)). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning. Abroad, use: Cherry tomatoes
- 1 head + 4 tbsp oil garlic fried in oil (keep the oil) (กระเทียม + น้ำมัน (น้ำมันกระเทียมเจียว)). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- 1 tsp each salt, sugar (light soy) (เกลือ น้ำตาล (ซีอิ๊วขาว)). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Same
- to serve fried dried chillies, sliced shallot, cucumber, coriander, spring onion (พริกแห้งทอด หอมแดงซอย แตงกวา ผักชี ต้นหอม). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- as needed banana leaves (ใบตอง). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Frozen banana leaves

**Prep ahead:** Cook the rice; fry garlic and reserve the oil; strain blood with lemongrass.

**Method**

1. Squeeze the blood together with lemongrass leaves, then strain. *What to look for:* Smooth dark-red liquid, less metallic smell. *Tip:* Removes clots and the 'fishy' blood smell.
2. Fry garlic golden; keep the oil. *What to look for:* Golden, nutty-smelling, not brown.
3. Knead (kan) the rice with the pork and blood until evenly red-brown; season with salt, sugar and garlic oil. (Wongnai's version stir-fries pork and blood first, then mixes in rice.) *What to look for:* Each grain is coated deep red; mix holds when squeezed.
4. Wrap tightly in banana leaf and steam until cooked - about 30 minutes (KPRU; only 10 minutes if the mix was pre-fried). *What to look for:* Parcels firm; inside turns uniformly brown with no red liquid.
5. Unwrap, drizzle garlic oil, top with fried garlic; serve with fried chillies, shallot, cucumber and coriander.

**Taste:** Savoury and garlicky with the deep mineral note of blood; heat comes from the fried chillies you add. Texture: Soft, slightly sticky rice with a rich, iron-savoury depth; crunchy toppings. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 1, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 4 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** A lunch dish: eat from the leaf with fried garlic and chillies, bites of raw garlic or shallot, and cucumber to refresh. Usually one parcel per person.

**Safety:** Contains pork blood: it must be fresh, from an inspected source, and fully steamed (no red liquid). Never eat raw-blood dishes.

**Common mistakes:** Using sticky rice (the Shan version uses jasmine rice); Under-steaming; Skipping the garlic oil - it's essential.

**Phrases:**

- ข้าวกั๊นจิ๊น ใส่เลือดกา *khao kan jin sai lueat ka*: "Does the khao kan jin have blood in it?" (Central Thai: ข้าวกั๊นจิ๊นใส่เลือดไหม; confidence low)

**Culture:** Khaosod notes 'เงี้ยว (ngiao)' was the Lanna name for Shan people, hence the alternative name khao ngiao. Shan people call themselves Tai (ไต); ngiao can be felt as a dated or derogatory exonym, so prefer 'khao kan jin' or 'Tai Yai food'. Khaosod also notes the Shan preference for non-glutinous rice at midday.

Sources: https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/how-to/tips/article_43764, https://arit.kpru.ac.th/ap2/local/?nu=pages&page_id=847&code_db=610008&code_type=TK001, https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/79663cd892224ce59502ca0a52a24bdc, https://www.wongnai.com/food-tips/top-35-must-try-northern-thai-dishes, https://www.ddc.moph.go.th/brc/news.php?news=22526&deptcode=brc, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_language

## Grilled and pounded beef
**เนื้อทุบ (จิ้นตุ๊บ)**, Kham Mueang **จิ๊นตุ๊บ**, said *jin tup*: จิ๊น *jin* (45) · ตุ๊บ *tup* (45)

Both syllables high and short; 'tup' ends in an unreleased p. ตุ๊บ = to pound/bash (Central ทุบ). Spellings vary: จิ้นตุ๊บ, จิ๊นตุ๊บ, จิ้นตุ้.

*From:* whole Lanna region. *Occasion:* กับแกล้ม (drinking snack); side dish. *Serves:* 2. *Confidence:* medium.

Beef marinated with coriander root, garlic and pepper, grilled, then pounded until it shreds into fluffy fibres; eaten with a chilli dip.

**Ingredients**

- 500 g beef (เนื้อวัว). Buy at: beef stalls in fresh markets. Abroad, use: Flank or topside
- 2 roots, 4-5 cloves, 1 tsp coriander root, garlic, black pepper (รากผักชี กระเทียม พริกไทยดำป่น). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- 2 stalks, 15 g lemongrass, galangal (sliced) (ตะไคร้ ข่า). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- 2 tsp, 1 tbsp, 2 tsp, 1/2 cup salt, light soy, sugar, water (เกลือ ซีอิ๊วขาว น้ำตาล น้ำ). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Same

**Prep ahead:** Marinate 4 hours.

**Method**

1. Pound coriander root, garlic and pepper; mix with beef and remaining seasonings; marinate 4 hours.
2. Grill over medium heat until cooked, then cool. *What to look for:* Browned outside, cooked through, firm.
3. Pound the meat until it shreds apart. *What to look for:* Meat splits into fluffy threads. *Tip:* The more you pound, the better the texture (Wongnai).

**Taste:** Savoury, peppery, smoky; very moreish. Texture: Shredded, fluffy yet chewy fibres. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 1, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 4 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Drinking snack or with sticky rice, dipped in a chilli sauce (Wongnai: jaew; Krua Khun Toi pairs it with nam phrik kha). Shared plate; in drinking circles, food is communal.

**Safety:** Grill fully before pounding. Raw beef dishes (e.g. ลาบดิบ) carry tapeworm risk per Thai science agencies.

**Common mistakes:** Pounding raw meat (it's grilled first); Using a very tender steak cut - it doesn't shred as well.

**Phrases:**

- จิ๊นตุ๊บ จ๊าดลำ *jin tup jaat lam*: "The pounded beef is really tasty" (Central Thai: เนื้อทุบอร่อยมาก; confidence low)

**Culture:** A classic kap klaem (food eaten with drinks).

Sources: https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/smashed-roasted-beef, https://www.kruakhuntoi.com/retrospective/%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%B7%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B8%E0%B8%9A%E0%B8%99%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%9E%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%82%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%B2/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_language

## Eggs grilled in a banana-leaf cup
**ไข่ป่าม**, Kham Mueang **ไข่ป่าม**, said *khai pam*: ไข่ *khai* (21) · ป่าม *pam* (21)

Both low. ป่าม = cooking liquid food in a leaf cup over low heat without oil (Pim.in.th). Careful: ไข่ (egg) vs ไก่ (chicken) vs ไก (river weed) differ only by aspiration/tone.

*From:* whole Lanna region, Phrae (community learning example). *Occasion:* breakfast, market snack, family cooking. *Serves:* 2-4. *Confidence:* high.

Seasoned beaten egg with spring onion and chilli (sometimes minced pork or shrimp) poured into banana-leaf cups and cooked over gentle charcoal until puffed and set - no oil, smoky banana-leaf aroma.

**Ingredients**

- 2 (Pim) to 15 (Wongnai batch) eggs (ไข่ไก่). Buy at: egg stalls. Abroad, use: Eggs
- 2 tbsp each minced pork / shrimp (optional) (หมูสับ / กุ้ง (ไม่ใส่ก็ได้)). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning. Abroad, use: Same
- 1-3 stalks spring onion (ต้นหอม). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- 4-5 chillies (พริกขี้หนู/พริกแดง). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- 1/4 tsp each (per 2 eggs) salt, pepper (เกลือ พริกไทย). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Same
- for cups banana leaf, pins (ใบตอง ไม้กลัด). Buy at: banana-leaf sellers in any fresh market (sold in folded bundles). Abroad, use: Frozen banana leaves; foil cups

**Prep ahead:** Make the leaf cups: wipe leaves, cut rectangles, place two with undersides together, fold up the sides and pin both ends.

**Method**

1. Lightly beat eggs (not frothy) with salt, pepper, spring onion and chilli.
2. Set leaf cups over a low charcoal fire.
3. Pour egg to 1/3-1/2 of the cup height (it rises); add pork/shrimp.
4. Grill gently 10-30 min until puffed and set. *What to look for:* Egg rises and firms; pressed lightly it feels dry and taut; a skewer comes out clean; leaf underneath browns but doesn't burn through. *Tip:* The CCF community version adds a little water in the lower leaf layers to stop burning.

**Taste:** Mild and soft with a toasted-leaf fragrance. Texture: Soft, puffy, custardy-omelette. (sour 0, salty 2, spicy 1, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 2 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Eat hot from the cup with sticky rice. One cup each, or shared from the cup with rice.

**Safety:** Cook until fully set if serving children, elderly or pregnant people.

**Common mistakes:** Filling cups to the brim (overflows as it puffs); High heat (leaf burns, egg raw).

**Phrases:**

- ไข่ป่าม สองกระทง *khai pam song kathong*: "Two khai pam, please" (Central Thai: ไข่ป่ามสองกระทง; confidence low)

**Culture:** A Children and Youth Development (CCF) project in Phrae used khai pam to pass elders' kitchen knowledge to children - it is a nostalgic, homely dish.

Sources: https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/grilled-eggs-in-banana-leaves, https://www.pim.in.th/side-dish-by-chicken/552-2012-03-01-11-20-57, https://www.ccfthai.or.th/news/view/b1KP05PYQ6lX/, https://www.wongnai.com/food-tips/top-35-must-try-northern-thai-dishes, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_language

## Nan river weed (freshwater algae), fried sheets or toasted crumble
**ไกทอด / ไกยี (สาหร่ายน้ำจืดน่าน)**, Kham Mueang **ไก**, said *kai*: ไก *kai* (24)

Rising tone, unaspirated k. Do not confuse with ไก่ kai (chicken, low tone) or ไข่ khai (egg).

*From:* Nan (Nan River - Tha Wang Pha, Phu Phiang), Chiang Rai (Mekong, Chiang Khong). *Occasion:* OTOP souvenir from Nan; breakfast with sticky rice; drinking snack. *Serves:* 4 as a snack. *Confidence:* medium.

Kai is a long green freshwater alga (Cladophora glomerata) growing on rocks in clean, sunlit, flowing rivers - an indicator of clean water. It is washed, dried into sheets (kai phaen) and fried crisp, or toasted and crumbled into kai yi with salt, toasted sesame and fried garlic. High in protein, fibre and B vitamins.

**Ingredients**

- 2-3 sheets dried river-weed sheets (ไกแผ่นอบแห้ง). Buy at: Nan OTOP/souvenir shops; some Chiang Mai souvenir shops. Abroad, use: Lao 'kaipen' sheets from Asian grocers; nori is NOT equivalent
- for shallow frying vegetable oil (น้ำมันพืช). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Same
- to taste toasted sesame, fried garlic, salt (for kai yi) (งาคั่ว กระเทียมเจียว เกลือ (สำหรับไกยี)). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Same

**Prep ahead:** Cut sheets into palm-size pieces (Lao guide: 7x14 cm).

**Method**

1. Cut the sheets into pieces.
2. Flash-fry in hot oil a few seconds per side. *What to look for:* Turns bright green and crisp instantly; stop before it darkens. *Tip:* The Lao Pha Khao Lao guide warns: 3-5 seconds per side - longer turns it bitter and burnt. (Frying time is from a Lao source; Nan practice presumably similar.)
3. Kai yi: toast dry kai on low heat until crisp, crush while hot, season with salt, toasted sesame, fried garlic (optional sesame oil). *What to look for:* Crumbly, nutty, smells of the river and sesame.

**Taste:** Crisp, salty-savoury, nutty sesame and garlic, faint green/grassy taste. Texture: Shatteringly crisp (fried) or dry crumble (kai yi). (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 0, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 3 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Kai yi with sticky rice and chilli dip (Thai PBS: nam phrik makhuea som, a tomato dip). Fried sheets as a beer snack. Fresh kai is made into ho nueng kai (steamed parcel), kaeng kai (curry) and khua kai (stir-fry). Shared plate.

**Safety:** Buy sealed commercial sheets; only riverside communities harvest fresh kai. Kai grows only in clean water, so wild-harvest quality depends on river condition.

**Common mistakes:** Over-frying (bitter); Confusing ไก (weed) with ไก่ (chicken) when ordering; Thinking it's seaweed/nori - it's freshwater.

**Phrases:**

- ไกแผ่น มีกา *kai phaen mi ka*: "Do you have river-weed sheets?" (Central Thai: มีไกแผ่นไหม; confidence low)

**Culture:** A community resource (e.g. Ban Nong Bua women's farmer group, Tha Wang Pha) and a symbol of clean rivers; conservation groups in Chiang Khong link falling kai to changes in the Mekong.

Sources: https://krua.co/food_story/kai-freshwater-algae/, https://mgronline.com/smes/detail/9550000112384, https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/BanTung/episodes/99707, https://www.seub.or.th/bloging/knowledge/2023-248/, https://phakhaolao.la/en/story/fried-rock-algae-river-weed-chips-kai-paen-juen/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_language

## Northern-style whole chicken steamed in curry paste
**ไก่นึ่งเมือง**, Kham Mueang **ไก่นึ่งเมือง**, said *kai nueng mueang*: ไก่ *kai* (21) · นึ่ง *nueng* (42) · เมือง *mueang* (33)

'kai' low (chicken), 'nueng' falling, 'mueang' mid. 'Mueang' here means 'local/Northern style'.

*From:* Lanna region. *Occasion:* family meals. *Serves:* 4-6. *Confidence:* medium.

Whole native chicken rubbed inside and out with a Northern paste and steamed; the paste from the cavity plus steaming juices becomes the dipping sauce. (Included as the sourced alternative to 'kai yang mueang', for which no Thai source was found.)

**Ingredients**

- 1 whole native (free-range) chicken (ไก่บ้าน). Buy at: chicken stalls in fresh markets (ask for ไก่บ้าน). Abroad, use: Free-range or poussin
- 8 dried chillies (พริกแห้ง). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Same
- 1 tsp salt (เกลือ). Buy at: grocery. Abroad, use: Same
- 2 tsp coriander seed (เมล็ดผักชี). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Same
- as listed lemongrass 2 tbsp, galangal 1 tbsp, turmeric 2 tbsp (ตะไคร้ ข่า ขมิ้น). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- as listed coriander root 3, garlic 10 g, shallot 10 g (รากผักชี กระเทียม หอมแดง). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- as listed shrimp paste 1 tsp, fish sauce 1/2 tbsp (กะปิ น้ำปลา). Buy at: dry-goods and chilli-paste stalls around Warorot / Ton Lamyai (ready-made Northern curry pastes are sold by weight). Abroad, use: Same
- 15 makrut lime leaves (ใบมะกรูด). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Frozen makrut leaves

**Prep ahead:** Pound paste; marinate chicken 30 min.

**Method**

1. Pound dry chilli with salt, then add coriander seed, lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, coriander root, garlic, shallot (driest first); season with shrimp paste and fish sauce; add torn makrut leaves. *What to look for:* Deep orange, very aromatic paste.
2. Rub the paste all over and inside the chicken; marinate 30 min.
3. Steam, covered, 45 minutes. *What to look for:* Juices clear; thigh meat pulls away easily.
4. Scoop paste from the cavity, mix with coriander, fish sauce and steaming juices for a dip.

**Taste:** Herbal, spicy, clean-tasting. Texture: Tender, firm-fleshed native chicken. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 3 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Tear the chicken by hand, dip in the paste sauce, eat with sticky rice. Whole bird in the middle, everyone tears pieces.

**Safety:** Steam until juices run clear at the thigh bone.

**Common mistakes:** Using a large broiler chicken (needs longer steaming, softer texture).

**Culture:** 'Mueang' as an adjective marks something as local Northern (khon mueang = Northern people, kham mueang = Northern speech).

## Herb-stuffed charcoal-grilled fish (Phrae style)
**ปลาปิ้งสมุนไพร (แบบแพร่)**, Kham Mueang **ป๋าปิ้ง**, said *pa ping*: ป๋า *pa* (24) · ปิ้ง *ping* (44ʔ)

'pa' rising, 'ping' high with glottal catch.

*From:* Phrae (Ban Phra That Phra Lo). *Occasion:* everyday, gatherings. *Serves:* 2-3. *Confidence:* medium.

Tilapia or red tilapia scored and salted, stuffed with chilli, shallot, garlic, lemongrass, turmeric and a little minced pork, grilled over charcoal; eaten with nam phrik num or jaew and raw vegetables. Community recipe shared on Lemon8 (social media) - medium confidence.

**Ingredients**

- 600-800 g (6-8 ขีด) tilapia / red tilapia (ปลานิล/ปลาทับทิม). Buy at: fish stalls; ask them to gut and scale. Abroad, use: Sea bream, snapper
- to taste chilli, shallot, garlic, lemongrass, turmeric, salt (พริก หอมแดง กระเทียม ตะไคร้ ขมิ้น เกลือ). Buy at: herb and vegetable stalls in fresh markets (Muang Mai, Ton Lamyai); herbs are sold in small bundles. Abroad, use: Same
- small amount minced pork (หมูสับ). Buy at: pork butcher stalls in a fresh market (Muang Mai market, Ton Lamyai market, or any neighbourhood kad) - go early morning. Abroad, use: Same

**Prep ahead:** Pound stuffing coarsely; mix lightly with pork.

**Method**

1. Score the fish 2-3 times per side, rub lightly with salt.
2. Stuff the belly with the herb-pork mixture. *Tip:* Mix lightly so the stuffing doesn't go soggy.
3. Grill over medium charcoal, turning often at first, then raise the heat to crisp the skin. *What to look for:* Skin blisters and crisps, flesh at the thickest point is opaque and flakes; stuffing hot. *Tip:* Brush with a little garlic oil near the end (optional).

**Taste:** Smoky, herby, salty skin with sweet flesh. Texture: Crisp salty skin, moist flesh. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 3 out of 5)

**How to eat it:** Pick flesh off with fingers or spoon, dip in nam phrik num or jaew, eat with sticky rice, cucumber, long beans, cabbage. Whole fish shared; take from your side of the fish.

**Safety:** Cook freshwater fish thoroughly (liver-fluke risk when raw/under-cooked); cook the pork stuffing through.

**Common mistakes:** Starting on very high heat (burnt skin, raw inside).

**Culture:** Grilled river fish with a chilli dip and sticky rice is one of the most everyday Northern meals.
