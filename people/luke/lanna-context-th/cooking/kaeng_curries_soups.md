# Northern Thai Curries & Soups (แก๋ง) — a step-by-step guide for foreigners

*Retrieved 2026-09-26 from mostly Thai-language sources (Krua.co, Matichon, Mae Fah Luang Univ., CMU Lanna database, Dept. of Medical Sciences reports, Wongnai, Kapook, Mae Noi, Gotoknow). Tones are **derived** and need a native-speaker check.*

## Before you start

**Northern curries have no coconut milk.** They are water-based, and their depth comes from pork bones and fat, fermented seasonings (shrimp paste กะปิ, fermented fish ปลาร้า, fermented soybean discs ถั่วเน่า, crab paste น้ำปู๋) and slow simmering.

### Pounding a paste in a mortar (ครก)

- Use a heavy granite mortar; slice everything small/thin first.
- Soak dried chillies until soft (slit and de-seed for less heat); pound them with salt FIRST — salt acts as an abrasive and absorbs moisture.
- Next add low-moisture aromatics: galangal, lemongrass, turmeric, coriander root.
- Then juicy ingredients — shallots, garlic — later, so the paste doesn't splash into your eyes.
- Shrimp paste / pla ra go in LAST; added early they make pounding harder.
- Pound until smooth and uniform; some Northern curries (phak wan) use a deliberately coarse paste.
- Northern curries are water-based — no coconut milk. Richness comes from pork fat, bones, fermented seasonings (kapi, pla ra, thua nao, nam pu) and long simmering.

### Reading a Northern menu: the soup words

| Word | Say | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| แกง (แก๋ง) | kaeng | Wet dish where the liquid is about equal to the solids. e.g. แกงอ่อมไก่ |
| ต้ม | tom | Brothy dish, can be mild, like Central Thai kaeng jued.  |
| จอ | jo | Curry-like broth WITHOUT a finely pounded paste. e.g. ผักกาดจอ |
| อ่อม | om | Premium Lanna meat curry, simmered low and long. e.g. แกงอ่อมไก่, แกงอ่อมปลา |
| อุ๊ก | uk | Same method as 'hum' (braise in little liquid). e.g. อุ๊กไก่ |
| อ็อก | ok | Lampang term; like uk with a thick, scant sauce. e.g. อ็อกหมู, อ็อกไก่ |
| มอก | mok | Specific name; only known example is mok pu (crab). e.g. มอกปู |

### Where to shop in Chiang Mai

Where-to-buy entries are general guidance (not sourced per item): Warorot/Kad Luang for dried spices, hang lay powder and ferments; Ton Lamyai (across the road) and Muang Mai (wholesale, early morning) for fresh herbs, veg and meat; every neighbourhood morning market (กาดเช้า) has a curry-paste seller who pounds fresh pastes to order.

### Handy Kham Mueang food words

- **กิ๋น** (kin) = eat — Central Thai กิน
- **ยะ** (ya) = make/do/cook — Central Thai ทำ
- **ลำ** (lam) = delicious — Central Thai อร่อย
- **จ๊าดลำ** (chat lam) = very delicious — Central Thai อร่อยมาก
- **แก๋ง** (kaeng) = curry — Central Thai แกง
- **จิ๊น** (jin) = meat — Central Thai เนื้อ(สัตว์)
- **ใบบ่ะขูด** (bai ba-khut) = kaffir lime leaf — Central Thai ใบมะกรูด
- **ไส้ปุ๋มอุมอาม** (sai pum um am) = assorted offal — Central Thai เครื่องใน
- **ข้าวนึ่ง** (khao nueng) = sticky rice (lit. 'steamed rice') — Central Thai ข้าวเหนียว

## Hang lay — Northern Burmese-influenced pork curry

**Thai:** แกงฮังเล · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งฮังเล (อ่าน แก๋งฮังแล) · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · ฮัง *hang* (33) · เล/แล *le / lae* (33)  
*Northerners say 'kaeng' with a rising tone and often end with 'lae' (as in 'lair' without the r) rather than 'lay'. OpenBase lists the reading as แก๋งฮังแล.*

**From:** เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, ลำปาง Lampang, แม่ฮ่องสอน Mae Hong Son, เชียงราย Chiang Rai (เชียงแสน Chiang Saen style), พะเยา Phayao, น่าน Nan  
**Season:** All year  
**Occasion:** Merit-making (งานบุญ), funerals and other big gatherings; khan tok dinners; also everyday market curry  
**Serves:** 5–6 (with sticky rice)  
**Confidence:** high

A slow-braised pork curry (no coconut milk) that is sour, sweet and salty in balance, perfumed with hang lay spice powder, shredded ginger, whole garlic and tamarind. The name is thought to come from Burmese 'hin le'/'hing le' — 'hin' meaning curry — and Thai sources describe it as Burmese- and Shan (Tai Yai)-influenced. Chiang Mai versions lean sweeter; Chiang Rai, Phayao and Nan often add peanuts; the Chiang Saen style adds long beans, fresh chillies and pickled bamboo shoot.

**Shopping list**

- Large dried red chillies (soaked, seeds out for less heat) — พริกแห้งเม็ดใหญ่ / พริกแห้ง — 5–10 pods (10–15 g) — swap abroad: Any mild dried red chilli (e.g. guajillo) + a few dried bird's-eye for heat.
- Galangal, sliced — ข่า — 1 Tbsp (~5 g) — swap abroad: Frozen galangal; ginger is NOT a good swap here.
- Salt (coarse/sea salt) — เกลือ — 1 tsp — swap abroad: Any coarse salt.
- Lemongrass (lower stalk, thinly sliced) — ตะไคร้ — 1–2 stalks — swap abroad: Frozen lemongrass from Asian grocers.
- Thai shallots — หอมแดง — 3–5 bulbs — swap abroad: Small French shallots.
- Thai garlic (small cloves) — กระเทียม — 5–10 cloves — swap abroad: Regular garlic, use ~2/3 amount.
- Shrimp paste — กะปิ — 1 tsp — swap abroad: Any Thai/Malay belacan-type shrimp paste.
- Pork belly, 2x2 in (5 cm) cubes — หมูสามชั้น — 500 g — swap abroad: Any pork belly.
- Pork neck or loin, cubed — หมูสันคอ / สันนอก / จิ๊นหมู — 500 g – 1 kg — swap abroad: Pork shoulder.
- Hang lay spice powder (masala-style blend) — ผงฮังเล — 10 g (1–2 sachets) — swap abroad: Mild Indian curry powder or garam masala + a pinch of turmeric (Mae Fah Luang Univ. recipe allows ผงกะหรี่).
- Young ginger, julienned — ขิงอ่อนซอย / ขิง — 50 g (~1/2 cup) — swap abroad: Regular ginger, cut finer.
- Whole peeled garlic cloves (or pickled garlic) — กระเทียมแกะเปลือก (ทั้งกลีบ) / กระเทียม — 50 g — swap abroad: Any garlic; pickled garlic adds tang.
- Tamarind water — น้ำมะขามเปียก / น้ำบ่ะขาม — 3 Tbsp (~45 g) — swap abroad: Tamarind concentrate (use less).
- Dark sweet soy sauce — ซีอิ๊วดำ — 1 Tbsp — swap abroad: Chinese dark soy + a little sugar.
- Cane or palm sugar — น้ำตาลอ้อย / น้ำตาลปี๊บ / น้ำอ้อย — to taste (some cooks use much more) — swap abroad: Brown sugar.
- Roasted peanuts (optional, Chiang Rai/Nan style) — ถั่วลิสงคั่ว (ถ้าชอบ) / ถั่วดิน — 2 Tbsp — swap abroad: Any unsalted roasted peanuts.

**Prep ahead:** Marinate the pork in paste + hang lay powder for at least 1 hour (overnight in fridge is fine). Hang lay tastes better the next day.

**Method**

1. Pound salt with soaked dried chillies until fine; add galangal and lemongrass, then shallots and garlic, and shrimp paste last. Pound to a smooth paste. (~15 min). *Look/smell for:* Paste turns brick-red and glossy with no visible fibres; smell is sharp and sweet. *Tip:* Dry/hard ingredients first, wet ones later so the paste does not splash (Krua.co, Wongnai tips).
2. Cut the pork into ~5 cm cubes, toss with dark soy, then rub in the paste and hang lay powder. Marinate at least 1 hour. (~60 min). *Look/smell for:* Meat is evenly stained red-brown.
3. Stir the marinated pork in a hot pot until it firms up and smells toasty; add water just to cover. (~10 min). *Look/smell for:* Fat starts to render; spices smell roasted, not raw. *Tip:* Northern curries are water-based — no coconut milk.
4. Simmer gently 1–2 hours until the pork is fork-tender, topping up with a little water if needed. (~90 min). *Look/smell for:* Surface shows a layer of red oil; pork belly fat is translucent and wobbly.
5. Add ginger and whole garlic; season with tamarind, sugar (and fish sauce if needed). Simmer ~15 min more; add peanuts if using. (~15 min). *Look/smell for:* Sauce is thick, glossy, dark red-brown; taste is sour-sweet-salty with no single flavour dominating. *Tip:* Taste and adjust gradually; Chiang Mai families like it sweeter.

**Taste:** Rich, mellow sour-sweet-salty, only mildly hot, warm-spiced with fresh ginger bite. (sour 3/5, salty 3, spicy 1, sweet 3, bitter 0, umami 4). Texture: Very tender pork in a thick, oily, clinging sauce; crunchy ginger.

**How to eat it:** Roll a small ball of sticky rice with your fingers and dip it in the sauce or use it to pick up pork. On a khan tok tray it sits with nam phrik num, nam phrik ong, pork rinds and steamed vegetables. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand; pork crackling (แคบหมู); steamed/fresh veg (ผักนึ่ง ผักสด). Etiquette: Curry is served in a shared bowl in the middle; take a little at a time with your own spoon or sticky rice, and don't pick through the bowl for the best pieces.

**Safety:** Cooked well; reheat thoroughly. Keeps 2–3 days refrigerated.

**Common mistakes:** Adding coconut milk (it's a water-based curry); Using lean meat only — it dries out; belly is essential; Rushing the braise; Substituting galangal with ginger in the paste; Dumping all the sugar at once

**Say it at the market:**

- มีแก๋งฮังเลก่อ (*mi kaeng hang lae ko*) — Do you have hang lay curry? [Central: มีแกงฮังเลไหม] — At a market curry stall
- ลำขนาด (*lam kha-naat*) — Very delicious! [Central: อร่อยมาก] — Complimenting the cook
- เอาข้าวนึ่งถุงนึ่ง (*ao khao nueng thung nueng*) — One bag of sticky rice, please [Central: เอาข้าวเหนียวถุงหนึ่ง] — Buying sticky rice

**Culture:** Traditionally a festival and funeral curry cooked in huge pots after slaughtering a pig, requiring many hands and long simmering. Its Burmese/Shan roots reflect centuries of exchange between Lanna, Burma and Shan State.

## Kaeng khae — Northern mixed forest-vegetable curry

**Thai:** แกงแค · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งแค · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · แค *khae* (33)  
*'khae' with an aspirated k and a flat mid tone.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All Northern provinces, แพร่ Phrae, เชียงราย Chiang Rai, เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai  
**Season:** All year; the vegetable mix changes with the season  
**Occasion:** Everyday home curry  
**Serves:** 2–4  
**Confidence:** high

A thin, herbal, peppery curry crammed with many local vegetables — the leaf of cha phlu (wild betel) and 'phak khae' lead the aroma. No coconut milk and not much water. Protein can be chicken, grilled frog or dried fish. The paste uses makhwaen (Northern prickly ash) and pla ra. Elders regard it as a 'balancing' dish.

**Shopping list**

- Dried chillies — พริกแห้ง — 4–6 pods — swap abroad: Dried red chillies.
- Garlic — กระเทียม — 7–10 cloves — swap abroad: Garlic.
- Lemongrass, sliced — ตะไคร้ซอย / ตะไคร้ — 1 stalk — swap abroad: Frozen lemongrass.
- Galangal — ข่า — 3 slices — swap abroad: Frozen galangal.
- Makhwaen (Zanthoxylum) seeds, toasted — มะแขว่น / บ่ะแขว่น — 1–2 tsp — swap abroad: Sichuan pepper (use less).
- Salt — เกลือ — 1 tsp — swap abroad: Sea salt.
- Fermented fish, chopped — ปลาร้าสับ / ปลาฮ้า — 1 Tbsp — swap abroad: Extra fish sauce + a little anchovy paste.
- Chicken on the bone (village chicken ideal), chopped — ไก่ (สับชิ้น) / จิ๊นไก่ — 300–400 g — swap abroad: Chicken thighs.
- Wild betel leaf (Piper sarmentosum) — ใบชะพลู / ใบชะพลู (บางแหล่งเรียกรวมกับผักแค — ต้องตรวจสอบ) — 1 handful — swap abroad: Betel leaf from Vietnamese grocers (lá lốt).
- Thai round eggplant; pea eggplant — มะเขือเปราะ, มะเขือพวง / บ่ะเขือ — 10 + 10–20 — swap abroad: Small eggplants.
- Long beans, 4 cm pieces — ถั่วฝักยาว — 2–3 pods — swap abroad: Green beans.
- Mixed greens: chilli leaves, sawtooth coriander, cha-om (acacia), ivy gourd tips, long-pepper shoots, para cress, sesbania flowers, wild mushrooms — whatever is in season — ใบพริก, ผักชีฝรั่ง, ชะอม, ตำลึง, ยอดดีปลี, ผักคราด, ดอกแค, เห็ดลม ฯลฯ / ผักแก๋งแค — 2–3 cups total — swap abroad: Spinach, chard, green beans, Thai basil, any peppery greens.
- Fish sauce — น้ำปลา — to taste — swap abroad: Fish sauce.

**Prep ahead:** Wash and sort vegetables into 'hard' (eggplants, beans), 'medium' and 'soft leaves/herbs' piles before cooking.

**Method**

1. Pound salt and dried chillies first, then galangal, lemongrass, garlic and makhwaen; finally mix in the pla ra. (~12 min). *Look/smell for:* Fragrant, slightly coarse paste; makhwaen gives a citrus-pine smell. *Tip:* Too much makhwaen overwhelms (Krua.co).
2. Stir-fry the paste with the chicken in the pot (no oil needed — chicken fat is enough) until fragrant. (~5 min). *Look/smell for:* Chicken turns opaque; paste smells toasted.
3. Add ~600 ml water and simmer until the chicken is cooked. (~15 min). *Look/smell for:* Clear, reddish broth. *Tip:* Keep broth scant — it's not a soup.
4. Add hard veg first (eggplants), then beans, and finally the leafy greens and herbs; cook 1–2 minutes more. (~8 min). *Look/smell for:* Leaves just wilted and bright green.
5. Taste, adjust with fish sauce, and take off the heat with the lid ajar so the greens don't overcook. (~1 min).

**Taste:** Peppery, salty, herbal and slightly bitter, with a numbing citrus note from makhwaen. (sour 0/5, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 2, umami 3). Texture: Thin, herbal broth packed with vegetables of different textures.

**How to eat it:** Eat with sticky rice; sip the broth. Often paired with a nam phrik and something fried/grilled. With: Sticky rice; grilled fish or pork; nam phrik. Etiquette: Shared bowl in the centre; take a spoonful to your own plate or dip sticky rice.

**Safety:** If using wild mushrooms (เห็ดลม etc.), buy from established market sellers only. Use cooked/bottled pla ra if unsure of the source.

**Common mistakes:** Too much water — it becomes a bland soup; Adding all vegetables at once; Leaving out cha phlu leaf; Over-using makhwaen

**Say it at the market:**

- ผักแก๋งแคมีก่อ (*phak kaeng khae mi ko*) — Do you have kaeng khae vegetables? [Central: มีผักแกงแคไหม] — At a vegetable stall

**Culture:** Each household uses whatever grows around the house that season; variety is the point. Elders treat it as food that keeps the body in balance.

**Sources:** https://www.matichon.co.th/weekly/column/article_184162, https://krua.co/food_story/kang-care-kai, https://sites.google.com/mfu.ac.th/chiangrai/traditional/foods/gang-khae, https://www.wongnai.com/food-tips/top-35-must-try-northern-thai-dishes

## Kaeng ho — 'combined' curry stir-fried from leftover curries

**Thai:** แกงโฮะ · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งโฮ๊ะ · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · โฮ๊ะ *ho* (45)  
*'ho' is short and high, cut off sharply — like a quick 'hoh!'.*

**From:** เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North  
**Season:** After festivals and temple feasts; all year  
**Occasion:** Day after merit-making at the temple (แกงโฮะวัด), weddings, funerals; now also cooked fresh  
**Serves:** Flexible  
**Confidence:** medium

'Ho' means 'to combine'. Kaeng ho takes leftover festival curries — hang lay, glass-noodle curry, steamed curries — and cooks them down together in a wok with fresh vegetables, lemongrass and shredded kaffir lime leaf until almost dry. A no-waste dish that became a favourite in its own right.

**Shopping list**

- Leftover hang lay curry — แกงฮังเลเหลือ / แก๋งฮังเล — 2 cups — swap abroad: Your own leftover hang lay
- Leftover glass-noodle curry or soaked glass noodles — แกงวุ้นเส้น / วุ้นเส้นแช่น้ำ / แก๋งวุ้นเส้น — 1 cup — swap abroad: Mung-bean glass noodles
- Pickled bamboo shoot — หน่อไม้ดอง / หน่อส้ม — 1/2 cup — swap abroad: Jarred pickled bamboo shoot
- Long beans, pea & round eggplant, cha-om, ivy gourd leaves, croton-type 'bai koson' leaves — ถั่วฝักยาว มะเขือพวง มะเขือเปราะ ชะอม ตำลึง ใบโกศล — 2–3 cups — swap abroad: Green beans, eggplant, spinach
- Lemongrass and finely shredded kaffir lime leaf — ตะไคร้ ใบมะกรูดซอยฝอย / ตะไคร้ ใบบ่ะขูด — 2 stalks, 5 leaves — swap abroad: Frozen lime leaves
- Fried dried chillies or fresh chillies — พริกแห้งทอด / พริกสด / พริก — to taste

**Prep ahead:** Collect leftover curries (must have been kept cold and be fresh — 1 day old max).

**Method**

1. Heat a large wok and tip in all the leftover curries; bring to a bubbling simmer. (~5 min). *Look/smell for:* Everything boiling hot throughout. *Tip:* Reboiling well is the food-safety point of this dish.
2. Add pickled bamboo and glass noodles; stir. (~3 min).
3. Add hard vegetables then leafy ones; stir-simmer until the liquid is mostly absorbed, veg cooked and noodles soft. (~10 min). *Look/smell for:* Almost dry, glossy mixture; noodles translucent.
4. Scatter lemongrass, shredded lime leaf and chillies; toss and serve. (~1 min). *Look/smell for:* A burst of citrus aroma over the stewed curry. *Tip:* Krua.co notes lemongrass and lime leaf mask any 'stale' smell.

**Taste:** Dense, sour-sweet-salty, perfumed with lemongrass and lime leaf. (sour 2/5, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 2, bitter 1, umami 4). Texture: Dry-ish stir-fried stew with noodles and mixed veg.

**How to eat it:** With sticky rice, picked up with the fingers. With: Sticky rice; fresh vegetables. Etiquette: Temple kaeng ho is shared among helpers — accepting some is accepting a share in the merit.

**Safety:** Only combine leftovers that were refrigerated and are no more than a day old; bring everything to a full boil. In hot weather, unrefrigerated party leftovers can spoil fast.

**Common mistakes:** Thinking it's a 'soup' — it should be almost dry; Using spoiled leftovers; Skipping the lemongrass/lime leaf finish

**Say it at the market:**

- โฮ๊ะกั๋น (*ho kan*) — Mix/combine together [Central: รวมกัน] — Describing the dish

**Culture:** 'Temple kaeng ho' is called a curry of togetherness: food offered to monks is redistributed back to the community in a new form. It embodies Lanna thrift.

**Sources:** https://krua.co/food_story/kangho

## Young jackfruit curry (auspicious New Year curry)

**Thai:** แกงขนุน · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งบ่าหนุน / แก๋งบะหนุน · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · บ่า *ba* (21) · หนุน *nun* (24)  
*'Ba-nun' — in Kham Mueang the fruit is บ่าหนุน; 'nun' (หนุน) also means 'to support', hence the good-luck pun.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North, เชียงราย Chiang Rai, เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai  
**Season:** Especially on วันปากปี๋ / วันพญาวัน during Pi Mai Mueang (Songkran, mid-April); young jackfruit available much of the year  
**Occasion:** Lanna New Year, weddings (blessing), family gatherings  
**Serves:** 4  
**Confidence:** medium

Young (green) jackfruit simmered with pork ribs or dried fish in a garlicky chilli-shrimp-paste broth, finished with small sour tomatoes, cha-om and wild betel leaf. Many Northerners eat it on the Lanna New Year 'head of the year' day because 'nun' (from ba-nun, jackfruit) also means 'to prop up/support' — so the year ahead is supported.

**Shopping list**

- Young green jackfruit, peeled, cut in chunks — ขนุนอ่อน / บ่าหนุนอ่อน — 300 g — swap abroad: Canned young green jackfruit in brine (rinse well).
- Pork soft-bone ribs — กระดูกหมูอ่อน / ซี่โครงหมู / จิ๊นหมู — 300 g — swap abroad: Pork spare ribs.
- Large dried red chillies (soaked, seeds out for less heat) — พริกแห้งเม็ดใหญ่ / พริกแห้ง — 5–10 pods (10–15 g) — swap abroad: Any mild dried red chilli (e.g. guajillo) + a few dried bird's-eye for heat.
- Salt (coarse/sea salt) — เกลือ — 1 tsp — swap abroad: Any coarse salt.
- Thai shallots — หอมแดง — 3–5 bulbs — swap abroad: Small French shallots.
- Thai garlic (small cloves) — กระเทียม — 5–10 cloves — swap abroad: Regular garlic, use ~2/3 amount.
- Shrimp paste — กะปิ — 1 tsp — swap abroad: Any Thai/Malay belacan-type shrimp paste.
- Small sour tomatoes — มะเขือเทศลูกเล็ก / มะเขือส้ม / บ่ะเขือส้ม — 50 g — swap abroad: Cherry tomatoes + a squeeze of lime.
- Cha-om (Acacia pennata) shoots — ชะอม / ผักหละ — 1 bunch — swap abroad: Omit, or a few basil leaves.
- Wild betel leaf — ใบชะพลู — 1 bunch — swap abroad: Lá lốt from Vietnamese grocers.

**Prep ahead:** If cutting jackfruit yourself, oil your knife and hands — the sap is extremely sticky. Keep cut pieces in water.

**Method**

1. Pound salt, dried chillies, garlic, shallots and shrimp paste to a paste. (~10 min). *Look/smell for:* Smooth brick-red paste.
2. Boil ~800 ml water, dissolve the paste, add pork ribs and simmer until tender. (~40 min). *Look/smell for:* Ribs meat pulls from bone.
3. Add the jackfruit and cook until tender. (~15 min). *Look/smell for:* Jackfruit pierces easily; texture like artichoke heart.
4. Add tomatoes, cha-om and cha phlu; season; turn off the heat and leave uncovered so herbs don't stew. (~3 min). *Look/smell for:* Leaves just wilted. *Tip:* iKasalong tip: don't cover right away.

**Taste:** Savoury, gently hot, tangy from small tomatoes, with cha-om's distinctive aroma. (sour 2/5, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 3). Texture: Meaty, fibrous jackfruit in thin brothy curry.

**How to eat it:** Eaten with sticky rice, ideally with family gathered together on New Year's 'head of the year' day. With: Sticky rice. Etiquette: Wishing elders well and eating together is part of the point; offer the first serving to elders.

**Safety:** Cha-om has an unusual sulphurous smell — normal. Wash jackfruit sap off skin with oil, not water.

**Common mistakes:** Using ripe (sweet) jackfruit; Getting jackfruit sap on the knife and board without oiling; Covering the pot after adding herbs

**Say it at the market:**

- กิ๋นแก๋งบ่าหนุน ปี๋นี้จะมีคนหนุน (*kin kaeng ba-nun pi ni ja mi khon nun*) — Eat jackfruit curry and this year someone will support you [Central: กินแกงขนุน ปีนี้จะมีคนค้ำจุน] — Explaining the New Year custom (paraphrase of the belief — low confidence wording)

**Culture:** The pun ba-nun / nun ('support') makes it an auspicious dish for Lanna New Year and weddings. This is a widely shared custom but practice varies by family.

## Wild sweet-leaf curry, often with red-ant eggs

**Thai:** แกงผักหวาน (ไข่มดแดง) · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งผักหวาน · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · ผัก *phak* (21) · หวาน *wan* (24)  
*'Phak' is short and low; 'wan' rises. 'Phak wan' literally 'sweet vegetable'.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North (also Isan)  
**Season:** Hot season (roughly Feb–Apr) when ผักหวานป่า shoots and red-ant eggs appear  
**Occasion:** Seasonal home cooking  
**Serves:** 3–4  
**Confidence:** medium

Young shoots of phak wan pa (Melientha suavis, a wild forest tree — NOT sweet-potato leaf) cooked in a light chilli broth with red-ant eggs, tomato and sometimes mushrooms. The paste is coarsely pounded. Some Northern homes use dried/grilled fish or glass noodles instead of ant eggs.

**Shopping list**

- Phak wan pa shoots (Melientha suavis) — ยอดผักหวานป่า / ผักหวาน — 200–300 g — swap abroad: Young spinach or pea shoots (different flavour).
- Red weaver-ant eggs/larvae — ไข่มดแดง / ไข่มดส้ม — 100 g — swap abroad: Omit, or use dried fish.
- Fresh or dried chillies — พริกสดหรือพริกแห้ง / พริก — 5–8
- Lemongrass, young galangal — ตะไคร้, ข่าอ่อน / ตะไคร้, ข่า — 1 stalk, 3 slices
- Shallots, garlic — หอมแดง กระเทียม — 3 + 5
- Shrimp paste or fermented fish — กะปิ หรือ ปลาร้า / กะปิ / ปลาฮ้า — 1 tsp–1 Tbsp — swap abroad: Anchovy paste
- Tomatoes; mushrooms (optional) — มะเขือเทศ, เห็ด (ถ้าชอบ) / บ่ะเขือส้ม — 2; handful
- Fish sauce — น้ำปลา — to taste

**Prep ahead:** Pick over phak wan, discarding tough stems. Rinse ant eggs gently in a sieve and pick out adult ants.

**Method**

1. Pound the paste ingredients coarsely. (~5 min). *Look/smell for:* Rough paste with visible bits.
2. Boil water, add the paste, return to the boil. (~5 min). *Look/smell for:* Reddish, fragrant broth.
3. Add phak wan and cook until tender. (~4 min). *Look/smell for:* Leaves deep green and soft.
4. Add ant eggs, mushrooms and tomato; season with fish sauce. (~3 min). *Look/smell for:* Eggs turn opaque white. *Tip:* Don't boil the eggs long or they toughen.

**Taste:** Naturally sweet greens, a mild sour tang from ant eggs, gentle heat. (sour 2/5, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 2, bitter 0, umami 3). Texture: Soft greens, eggs that pop, light broth.

**How to eat it:** With sticky rice; sip the hot broth. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand. Etiquette: Shared bowl.

**Safety:** Buy phak wan pa from market sellers rather than foraging — misidentifying wild plants is a general risk (no specific look-alike verified in this research round). Anyone with shellfish/insect allergy should avoid ant eggs.

**Common mistakes:** Confusing phak wan pa with phak wan ban (Sauropus) or sweet-potato leaves; Over-cooking ant eggs

**Say it at the market:**

- ผักหวานมีก่อเจ้า (*phak wan mi ko jao*) — Do you have phak wan? [Central: มีผักหวานไหมคะ] — Market (เจ้า = polite particle, female & increasingly general)

**Culture:** A marker of the hot season, when phak wan pa sprouts and weaver ants lay eggs; foraged foods are prized and pricey.

**Sources:** https://cooking.kapook.com/view289376.html

## Jor phak kad — mustard-green soup with fermented soybean and tamarind

**Thai:** จอผักกาด · **Kham Mueang:** จอผักกาด / ผักกาดจอ · **Say it:** จอ *jo* (24) · ผัก *phak* (21) · กาด *kat* (21)  
*'Jo' rhymes with 'jaw' (long o), rising tone.*

**From:** เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, ลำปาง Lampang, ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North  
**Season:** Cool season (Nov–Feb) when flowering mustard greens (ผักกาดจ้อน/ผักกาดดอก) are plentiful  
**Occasion:** Everyday  
**Serves:** 2–3  
**Confidence:** medium

'Jo' is a Lanna category: a brothy dish like a curry but without a finely pounded paste (CMU's Lanna food-naming database gives ผักกาดจอ as its example). Flowering mustard greens simmered with pork ribs, seasoned with shrimp paste, toasted fermented-soybean discs (thua nao) and tamarind, topped with fried garlic and fried dried chillies.

**Shopping list**

- Flowering mustard / choy sum with flower buds, 5 cm lengths — ผักกาดจ้อน / ผักกาดกวางตุ้งมีดอก / ผักกาดจอ — 300 g (2 big cups) — swap abroad: Choy sum or rapini.
- Pork baby ribs (or belly) — ซี่โครงหมูอ่อน / จิ๊นหมู — 300–500 g — swap abroad: Pork ribs.
- Shallots and garlic — หอมแดง กระเทียม — 3 Tbsp + 3 Tbsp
- Shrimp paste — กะปิ — 1 Tbsp — swap abroad: Belacan
- Dried fermented-soybean discs, toasted and crumbled — ถั่วเน่าแผ่น (ปิ้ง) / ถั่วเน่าแข็บ — 2 discs — swap abroad: 1 tsp miso or doenjang.
- Tamarind water — น้ำมะขามเปียก / น้ำบ่ะขาม — 3 Tbsp — swap abroad: Tamarind concentrate
- Fried garlic, fried dried chillies — กระเทียมเจียว, พริกแห้งทอด — garnish

**Prep ahead:** Toast thua nao discs over a flame/in a dry pan; fry garlic and chillies for garnish.

**Method**

1. Pound shallots, garlic and shrimp paste (and toasted thua nao) together. (~5 min). *Look/smell for:* Grey-brown pungent paste.
2. Boil water and simmer the ribs until tender. (~30 min). *Look/smell for:* Clear broth; meat tender.
3. Stir in the paste, add the mustard greens, season with tamarind; cook just until greens soften. (~5 min). *Look/smell for:* Greens bright and just tender; broth murky and sour-savoury. *Tip:* Don't overcook — the greens turn bitter and khaki.
4. Ladle into bowls; top with fried garlic and fried dried chillies. (~1 min).

**Taste:** Salty, sour and deeply savoury with a funky fermented-soy/shrimp note. (sour 3/5, salty 3, spicy 1, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 4). Texture: Soupy with tender-crisp greens.

**How to eat it:** Sip hot with sticky rice, a nam phrik and pork rinds. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand; pork rinds (แคบหมู). Etiquette: Shared bowl; everyone spoons their own.

**Safety:** No special issues. Thua nao is fermented but dried and toasted.

**Common mistakes:** Pounding a full curry paste — jo uses only a light shallot/garlic/kapi mix; Overcooking the greens; Skipping the sour tamarind

**Say it at the market:**

- ผักกาดจอ ซดน้ำฮ้อนๆ ลำแต๊ (*phak kat jo sot nam hon hon lam tae*) — Hot jo phak kad soup is really tasty [Central: จอผักกาด ซดน้ำร้อนๆ อร่อยจริง] — Small talk

**Culture:** Jo is one of the Lanna soup categories (kaeng, tom, jo, om, uk, ok, mok) listed in CMU's Lanna food-naming database — knowing these words helps read a Northern menu.

**Sources:** https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/baby-pork-ribs-with-chinese-broccoli-soup, https://lannakadee.cmu.ac.th/area1/food/type.php, https://www.wongnai.com/food-tips/top-35-must-try-northern-thai-dishes

## Wild earthstar-mushroom curry with young tamarind leaves

**Thai:** แกงเห็ดเผาะ (แกงเห็ดถอบ) ใส่ยอดมะขาม · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งเห็ดถอบ · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · เห็ด *het* (21) · ถอบ *thop* (21)  
*Northerners call the mushroom เห็ดถอบ (het thop); Central Thais say เห็ดเผาะ (het pho — 'pop' mushroom, for the crunch).*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North (and Isan)  
**Season:** Early rainy season, about May–July only (once a year)  
**Occasion:** Seasonal treat; pricey in the market  
**Serves:** 3–4  
**Confidence:** high

Het thop are round, button-like wild earthstar mushrooms (Astraeus) that appear under large trees for a few weeks at the start of the rains. The Lanna version is a clear chilli-paste curry (no coconut) soured with young tamarind leaves; the mushrooms pop crunchily between the teeth.

**Shopping list**

- Earthstar mushrooms (fresh or brined) — เห็ดถอบ/เห็ดเผาะ / เห็ดถอบ — 300 g fresh / 100 g brined — swap abroad: Canned het pho from Asian grocers.
- Young tamarind leaves (and flowers) — ยอดมะขามอ่อน (และดอก) / ยอดบ่ะขาม — 2 cups — swap abroad: Sorrel or a squeeze of lime at the end.
- Dried chillies — พริกแห้ง — 5
- Galangal (or fresh turmeric) — ข่าซอย / ขมิ้นสด / ข่า / ขมิ้น — 1 Tbsp
- Lemongrass — ตะไคร้ซอย / ตะไคร้ — 1 Tbsp
- Garlic — กระเทียม — 4 cloves
- Shrimp paste, salt — กะปิ, เกลือ — 1 Tbsp, 1 tsp

**Prep ahead:** Wash thoroughly: put in water in a lidded container and shake repeatedly, changing water, until no soil remains (Kapook). Check EVERY mushroom (see safety). Some cooks pre-boil with salt.

**Method**

1. Pound dried chilli, galangal/turmeric, lemongrass, garlic, shrimp paste and salt to a paste. (~8 min).
2. Boil water, add paste and mushrooms; simmer about 15 minutes. (~15 min). *Look/smell for:* Mushrooms darken; broth turns earthy.
3. Add young tamarind leaves and turn off the heat. (~1 min). *Look/smell for:* Fresh sour smell.

**Taste:** Hot, salty, bright-sour; mushrooms crunch then yield. (sour 3/5, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 4). Texture: Crunchy-popping mushroom shells with a soft centre in thin broth.

**How to eat it:** With sticky rice. Bite the mushroom to hear the 'pop'. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand. Etiquette: Expensive — don't hog the mushrooms from the shared bowl.

**Safety:** WILD MUSHROOM WARNING. Thai Department of Medical Sciences and provincial PR offices warn of a toxic look-alike, เห็ดก้อนฝุ่น / เห็ดไข่หงส์ (Scleroderma, e.g. S. citrinum). Signs of the poisonous one: a root-like stalk/base, rough scaly skin, may change colour when cut; edible het thop has no root and smooth skin (sometimes with fine white fuzz). Symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, dizziness, breathing difficulty) can start 1–3 h after eating. Folk tests (silver spoon, cooking with rice, onion colour) do NOT work (HRDI, Dailynews expert). If poisoned: go to hospital immediately, bring a sample; Thai emergency number 1669. Only buy from established market sellers; never forage yourself.

**Common mistakes:** Not washing out all the grit; Buying from roadside foragers without checking; Adding coconut milk (that's a Central/other style)

**Say it at the market:**

- เห็ดถอบกิโลเต้าใด (*het thop kilo tao dai*) — How much per kilo for het thop? [Central: เห็ดเผาะกิโลละเท่าไร] — Market — prices spike early in the season

**Culture:** A prized seasonal forest food and cash income for villagers. The practice of burning forest floor to make mushrooms easier to find is linked to the North's haze debate (context note; not from this round's cited sources).

**Sources:** https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/featured/article_284384, https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/earthstar-mushroom-soup, https://cooking.kapook.com/view281312.html, https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/341528, https://uttaradit.prd.go.th/th/content/page/index/id/189954, https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/3504781/, https://www.hrdi.or.th/articles/Detail/20

## Fresh bamboo-shoot curry, Northern style (often with nam pu crab paste)

**Thai:** แกงหน่อไม้สด (ใส่น้ำปู๋) · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งหน่อ / แก๋งหน่อไม้ · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · หน่อ *no* (21) · ไม้ *mai* (45)  
*Northerners often just say แก๋งหน่อ 'kaeng no'.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North, น่าน Nan (nam pu), พะเยา Phayao, เชียงราย Chiang Rai  
**Season:** Rainy season (about Jun–Sep) when fresh shoots come up  
**Occasion:** Everyday  
**Serves:** 4  
**Confidence:** medium

A rainy-season curry of fresh bamboo shoots in chilli broth. Many Northern cooks add nam pu (น้ำปู๋) — a black, sticky paste made by pounding rice-field crabs, straining and boiling the juice down for hours — which gives an earthy, briny depth. Pork, chicken or fish and local greens complete it.

**Shopping list**

- Fresh bamboo shoots, peeled, sliced, PRE-BOILED — หน่อไม้สด (ต้มแล้ว) / หน่อ — 400 g — swap abroad: Canned/vacuum bamboo shoots (rinse & boil).
- Nam pu (rice-field crab paste) — น้ำปู๋ — 1–2 Tbsp (to taste) — swap abroad: Omit; add a little shrimp paste + fish sauce.
- Large dried red chillies (soaked, seeds out for less heat) — พริกแห้งเม็ดใหญ่ / พริกแห้ง — 5–10 pods (10–15 g) — swap abroad: Any mild dried red chilli (e.g. guajillo) + a few dried bird's-eye for heat.
- Salt (coarse/sea salt) — เกลือ — 1 tsp — swap abroad: Any coarse salt.
- Lemongrass (lower stalk, thinly sliced) — ตะไคร้ — 1–2 stalks — swap abroad: Frozen lemongrass from Asian grocers.
- Thai shallots — หอมแดง — 3–5 bulbs — swap abroad: Small French shallots.
- Thai garlic (small cloves) — กระเทียม — 5–10 cloves — swap abroad: Regular garlic, use ~2/3 amount.
- Pork or chicken — หมู/ไก่ / จิ๊นหมู/จิ๊นไก่ — 300 g
- Local greens (cha-om, lemon basil etc.) — ผักพื้นบ้าน (ชะอม ใบแมงลัก ฯลฯ) / ผักหละ ฯลฯ — 1 cup — swap abroad: Basil

**Prep ahead:** Boil sliced fresh bamboo shoots in plenty of water, lid off, and discard the water (repeat if bitter). For bottled/tinned (ปี๊บ) shoots, boil at least 30 minutes before use (botulism advice).

**Method**

1. Boil the bamboo shoots and drain (see prep). (~20 min). *Look/smell for:* Bitterness and sharp smell reduced. *Tip:* Lid off lets volatile cyanogenic compounds escape.
2. Pound the paste. (~8 min).
3. Boil water, dissolve paste, cook the meat. (~15 min).
4. Add bamboo shoots and nam pu; simmer to blend. (~10 min). *Look/smell for:* Broth turns dark and thick. *Tip:* Add nam pu gradually — it's strong.
5. Add greens, adjust seasoning, serve. (~2 min).

**Taste:** Hot, salty, very savoury, with nam pu's earthy, rice-paddy aroma. (sour 0/5, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 1, bitter 1, umami 5). Texture: Crunchy shoots in a thick dark broth.

**How to eat it:** With sticky rice. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand. Etiquette: Shared bowl.

**Safety:** Fresh bamboo shoots contain cyanogenic glycosides — always boil (lid off) and discard water. Tinned/bottled bamboo (หน่อไม้ปี๊บ) has caused botulism outbreaks in Thailand; boil at least 30 minutes before eating (Doctor.or.th / health authorities). Nam pu is boiled for hours; buy from reputable sellers.

**Common mistakes:** Using raw bamboo shoots unboiled; Too much nam pu; Expecting it to taste like Isan แกงหน่อไม้ใบย่านาง (green, with yanang leaf)

**Say it at the market:**

- ใส่น้ำปู๋ก่อ (*sai nam pu ko*) — Is there nam pu in it? [Central: ใส่น้ำปูไหม] — Asking at a curry stall

**Culture:** Nam pu is a rice-farming-season preserve made from crabs caught in paddies — food knowledge tied to the agricultural calendar.

**Sources:** https://food.trueid.net/detail/1aPyAMJR9m7X, https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/458338, https://www.doctor.or.th/article/detail/1439

## Young fig-leaf (phak hueat) curry

**Thai:** แกงผักเฮือด · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งผักเฮือด (บางถิ่น ผักฮี้ / ผักเฮียด) · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · ผัก *phak* (21) · เฮือด *hueat* (42)  
*'hueat' has the Thai 'uea' diphthong (like 'oo-uh') and a falling tone.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North  
**Season:** Late cool season into hot season when the deciduous tree pushes new shoots (sources: ~Oct–Jan / late cool season)  
**Occasion:** Seasonal everyday  
**Serves:** 3–4  
**Confidence:** medium

Phak hueat is the young shoot of a deciduous fig-family tree (Ficus sp.; local names include pho sai, sai liap, phak krai). The tips are slightly sour-astringent and are simmered with pork or pork bones in a simple paste of chilli, shallot, garlic, shrimp paste and pla ra, with tomatoes. Other regions have the tree but, per one Thai source, only the North cooks it this way.

**Shopping list**

- Young phak hueat shoots (Ficus) — ยอดผักเฮือด / ผักเฮือด — 300 g — swap abroad: No real substitute; young fig or sorrel leaves as a rough idea.
- Pork bones or pork — กระดูกหมู / หมู / จิ๊นหมู — 300 g
- Dried chillies — พริกแห้ง — 10
- Garlic 5 cloves; shallot 1 — กระเทียม, หอมแดง — 5 + 1
- Shrimp paste; fermented fish — กะปิ, ปลาร้า / กะปิ, ปลาฮ้า — 1 Tbsp each
- Tomatoes — มะเขือเทศ / บ่ะเขือส้ม — 2–3

**Prep ahead:** Pick the tender tips; some cooks steam them first to reduce astringency (Mae Noi).

**Method**

1. Pound chillies, garlic, shallot, shrimp paste and pla ra. (~8 min).
2. Boil water with the paste and pork until cooked. (~20 min).
3. Add phak hueat and tomatoes; cook until tender; season. (~8 min). *Look/smell for:* Leaves turn olive and soft.

**Taste:** Sour-astringent, hot and salty — a 'green' taste. (sour 3/5, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 3). Texture: Soft leaves in a thin broth.

**How to eat it:** With sticky rice. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand. Etiquette: Shared bowl.

**Safety:** Buy from market sellers who know the plant.

**Common mistakes:** Using mature leaves (too tough and astringent)

**Say it at the market:**

- ผักเฮือดออกแล้วก่อ (*phak hueat ok laeo ko*) — Is phak hueat in season yet? [Central: ผักเฮือดออกแล้วหรือยัง] — Market

**Culture:** A seasonal marker vegetable; its name varies by district (ผักฮี้, ผักเฮียด).

**Sources:** https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/506041, https://www.maenoicurry.com/en/article/98/%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%9C%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%81%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%AE%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%94, https://cookpad.com/th/recipes/6617421

## Kaeng om — Northern slow-simmered offal & meat curry

**Thai:** แกงอ่อม (แบบเหนือ) · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งอ่อม / อ่อมจิ๊น · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · อ่อม *om* (21)  
*'om' is short-ish and low — not the yoga 'om'.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North, แพร่ Phrae  
**Season:** All year; cooler months popular  
**Occasion:** Weekend cooking, after a pig/cow slaughter, drinking food (กับแกล้ม)  
**Serves:** 4–6  
**Confidence:** medium

'Om' means simmering meat over a low flame for a long time until it falls apart; CMU's Lanna food-naming database calls it a premium Lanna dish. The Northern version is meat- and offal-led (tendon, liver, intestine, cartilage) with blood, galangal, lemongrass, coriander root and kaffir lime leaf, using a spiced paste similar to larb spice — unlike the vegetable-heavy Isan kaeng om.

**Shopping list**

- Pork or beef meat and offal (tendon, liver, intestines, cartilage), cleaned — เนื้อและเครื่องในหมูหรือวัว (เอ็น ตับ ไส้ กระดูกอ่อน) / จิ๊น ไส้ปุ๋มอุมอาม — 1 kg — swap abroad: Beef shin + tendon + a little liver.
- Pork blood (optional) — เลือดหมู / เลือด — 1/2 cup — swap abroad: Omit.
- Om curry paste (dried chilli, shallot, garlic, lemongrass, galangal, kapi) plus warm spices/prik laab — พริกแกงอ่อม / พริกลาบ + พริกแกงพื้นฐาน / พริกแก๋ง — 3–4 Tbsp — swap abroad: Make base paste + 1 tsp larb spice.
- Galangal slices, lemongrass lengths, coriander root — ข่า (แว่น), ตะไคร้ (ท่อน), รากผักชี / ข่า ตะไคร้ รากผักชี — 1 head, 3 stalks, 3 roots
- Kaffir lime leaves, torn — ใบมะกรูด / ใบบ่ะขูด — 5 — swap abroad: Frozen lime leaves
- Coriander, spring onion, sawtooth coriander to finish — ผักชี ต้นหอม ผักชีฝรั่ง / ผักหอมป้อม (ผักชี), ผักหอมเป (ผักชีฝรั่ง) — ต้องตรวจสอบ — garnish

**Prep ahead:** Clean offal thoroughly (salt/flour rub, rinse); par-boil intestines. Budget 2+ hours for simmering tendon.

**Method**

1. Fry the paste in a little oil until fragrant; add meat/offal, galangal, lemongrass and coriander root; stir until cooked on the outside. (~10 min). *Look/smell for:* Paste smells toasted; meat coated red.
2. Add water, bring to the boil, add blood, then simmer on LOW until the tendon is soft. (~120 min). *Look/smell for:* Broth darkens and thickens; tendon translucent and yielding. *Tip:* This low, long simmer is what 'om' means.
3. Season with salt, add lime leaves and chillies; finish with herbs. (~3 min).

**Taste:** Hot, deeply meaty and spiced, with a slight bitterness from offal/bile in some versions. (sour 0/5, salty 3, spicy 4, sweet 0, bitter 2, umami 5). Texture: Soft tendon, chewy offal in a dark, thick broth.

**How to eat it:** With sticky rice; Mae Noi notes it also works over rice noodles. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand; fresh herbs. Etiquette: Shared bowl; it's polite to try a bit of the offal if offered.

**Safety:** Offal and blood must be very fresh and cooked thoroughly. Streptococcus suis infection from undercooked pork/blood is a known risk in Northern Thailand — never eat raw blood versions. (General public-health knowledge; not from this round's sources.)

**Common mistakes:** Boiling hard instead of gentle simmering; Confusing with Isan kaeng om (dill, veg-heavy); Poorly cleaned intestines

**Say it at the market:**

- อ่อมจิ๊น (*om jin*) — Meat 'om' curry [Central: แกงอ่อมเนื้อ] — Ordering

**Culture:** In Kham Mueang, 'om' is also used figuratively for dawdling or dragging something out — a nice example of cooking vocabulary in daily speech.

**Sources:** https://www.maenoicurry.com/en/article/105/%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%AD%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B9-%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD, https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/421377, https://lannakadee.cmu.ac.th/area1/food/type.php

## Sour fish soup with young tamarind and tomato

**Thai:** ต้มส้มปลา · **Kham Mueang:** ต้มส้มป๋า · **Say it:** ต้ม *tom* (44ʔ) · ส้ม *som* (44ʔ) · ปลา/ป๋า *pa* (24)  
*Northerners often drop the l: ปลา → 'pa'. 'Som' in Kham Mueang is simply 'sour'.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North (sour fish soups are pan-Thai; Northern attribution low confidence)  
**Season:** All year; young tamarind in cool season  
**Occasion:** Everyday  
**Serves:** 3–4  
**Confidence:** low

In Lanna naming, 'tom' is a brothy dish that may be mild like Central 'kaeng jued'. This sour fish soup simmers freshwater fish with young tamarind pods, sour tomatoes, whole shallots, dried chilli and lemongrass, seasoned with salt, fish sauce and a little turmeric, and finished with spring onion and lemon basil. NOTE: recipe from a user-contributed Thai source; a specifically Lanna 'tom som' tradition was not confirmed this round.

**Shopping list**

- Freshwater fish (tilapia, snakehead...), cut in pieces — ปลาน้ำจืด (ปลานิล ปลาช่อน ฯลฯ) / ป๋า — 500 g — swap abroad: Any firm white fish.
- Young green tamarind pods, cracked — มะขามอ่อน / บ่ะขามอ่อน — 1–2 pods — swap abroad: Tamarind water.
- Small sour tomatoes, halved — มะเขือเทศลูกเล็ก / บ่ะเขือส้ม — 4–5 — swap abroad: Cherry tomatoes.
- Whole shallots, dried chillies, bruised lemongrass — หอมแดง พริกแห้ง ตะไคร้ — 3–4, 4–5, 1
- Salt 1 tsp, fish sauce 1 Tbsp, turmeric 1/2 tsp — เกลือ น้ำปลา ขมิ้นผง — 
- Spring onion, lemon basil — ต้นหอม ใบแมงลัก — garnish — swap abroad: Thai basil.

**Prep ahead:** Clean and cut fish; small fish can stay whole.

**Method**

1. Boil water with lemongrass, tamarind, tomatoes and whole shallots until the tomatoes colour the broth and the tamarind softens. (~10 min). *Look/smell for:* Pinkish, tart-smelling broth.
2. Add fish; season with salt, fish sauce and turmeric. (~8 min). *Look/smell for:* Fish flakes when pressed. *Tip:* Don't stir hard or the fish breaks.
3. Taste; finish with spring onion and basil. (~1 min).

**Taste:** Sour-led, lightly salty, fresh. (sour 4/5, salty 3, spicy 1, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 3). Texture: Clear sour broth, flaky fish.

**How to eat it:** Sip hot with sticky or steamed rice. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand. Etiquette: Shared bowl; watch for bones.

**Safety:** Freshwater fish must be fully cooked (liver fluke risk in raw freshwater fish).

**Common mistakes:** Boiling fish too long; Stirring vigorously

**Say it at the market:**

- ส้มแต๊ (*som tae*) — Really sour! [Central: เปรี้ยวจริง] — Tasting
**Sources:** https://cookpad.com/th/recipes/15964706, https://lannakadee.cmu.ac.th/area1/food/type.php

## Kaeng kradang — jellied pork-trotter curry set by the winter cold

**Thai:** แกงกระด้าง · **Kham Mueang:** แก๋งกะด้าง / หมูกระด้าง / หมูหนาว (แกงตุ๋ง) · **Say it:** แก๋ง *kaeng* (24) · กะ *ka* (21) · ด้าง *dang* (44ʔ)  
*Northerners say 'ka-dang' (not 'kra-').*

**From:** เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, เชียงราย Chiang Rai, แม่ฮ่องสอน Mae Hong Son, ลำพูน Lamphun, ลำปาง Lampang, แพร่ Phrae, น่าน Nan  
**Season:** Cold season only (Dec–Jan) — the cold night air is the 'setting agent'  
**Occasion:** Winter breakfast; sold in slices at morning markets  
**Serves:** 6–8  
**Confidence:** high

Pork trotters and legs (some kitchens use the pig's face meat) are singed, scraped, boned and simmered for hours, then left out overnight in the winter cold until the broth sets into a natural jelly. Krua.co calls cold air the key 'ingredient'. Chiang Mai style seasons with white pepper, garlic and coriander root; Chiang Rai style uses a paste of dried chilli, galangal, lemongrass and shrimp paste.

**Shopping list**

- Pork legs & trotters (or face/mask meat) — ขาหมู ตีนหมู (หรือหน้ากากหมู) / ขาหมู ตี๋นหมู — 1.5–2 kg — swap abroad: Pig's feet + pork hock; add unflavoured gelatin if your climate is warm.
- White pepper, garlic, coriander root (Chiang Mai style) — พริกไทยขาว กระเทียม รากผักชี (แบบเชียงใหม่) — 1 tsp, 1 head, 3 roots
- Dried chilli, galangal, lemongrass, shrimp paste (Chiang Rai style) — พริกแห้ง ข่า ตะไคร้ กะปิ (แบบเชียงราย) — paste ~3 Tbsp
- Salt, fish sauce — เกลือ น้ำปลา — to taste
- Spring onion, coriander — ต้นหอม ผักชี — garnish

**Prep ahead:** Singe off hairs over flame and scrape the skin clean; this takes time.

**Method**

1. Singe and scrape the trotters/legs clean, debone and cut into pieces. (~30 min). *Look/smell for:* Skin smooth and pale-gold, no hairs.
2. Pound the seasoning for your chosen style (CM: pepper-garlic-coriander root; CR: chilli paste). (~8 min).
3. Simmer the pork with the seasoning on low for several hours until the skin is very soft. (~180 min). *Look/smell for:* Broth becomes sticky — fingers stick together when you touch a drop. *Tip:* Stickiness = enough gelatin to set.
4. Season, pour into trays/bowls and leave overnight in the cold air. (~480 min). *Look/smell for:* By morning it is set into a firm, amber jelly. *Tip:* In warm weather use the fridge.
5. Cut into slices; top with herbs. (~2 min).

**Taste:** Savoury and peppery (or chilli-spiced), a cool jelly that melts on hot rice. (sour 0/5, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 4). Texture: Cold, wobbly jelly with soft skin and meat — melts on hot sticky rice.

**How to eat it:** With piping-hot sticky rice as a winter breakfast; the heat of the rice melts the jelly. With: Hot sticky rice. Etiquette: Bought by the slice at the market and shared at breakfast.

**Safety:** Keep cold; once set, it should stay chilled and be eaten within a couple of days. Do not leave outside overnight in warm weather.

**Common mistakes:** Expecting it to set in warm weather without a fridge; Removing too much skin (the gelatin source)

**Say it at the market:**

- หนาวแล้ว มีแก๋งกะด้างก่อ (*nao laeo mi kaeng ka-dang ko*) — It's cold now — any kaeng kradang? [Central: หนาวแล้ว มีแกงกระด้างไหม] — Market, winter mornings

**Culture:** Truly seasonal: without the cold it doesn't exist traditionally. Krua.co compares it with Vietnamese and Chinese jellied-pork dishes.

**Sources:** https://krua.co/food_story/kangkradang/, https://www.wongnai.com/food-tips/top-35-must-try-northern-thai-dishes

## Yam jin kai — Northern spiced chicken soup (a 'yam' that is not a salad)

**Thai:** ยำจิ๊นไก่ · **Kham Mueang:** ยำจิ๊นไก่ · **Say it:** ยำ *yam* (33) · จิ๊น *jin* (45) · ไก่ *kai* (21)  
*จิ๊น (jin) is Kham Mueang for 'meat'.*

**From:** ทั่วภาคเหนือ All North  
**Season:** All year  
**Occasion:** After a สู่ขวัญ (khwan-calling) ceremony — the ritual chicken becomes this meal  
**Serves:** 4  
**Confidence:** medium

Despite the name 'yam', it's a soupy, dark-red, curry-like dish. A whole village chicken — often the one used in a khwan-calling (สู่ขวัญ) ceremony — is boiled, shredded and mixed with larb spice (prik laab), makhwaen and the broth, then finished with spring onion, coriander and mint. This is the closest Northern analogue to a 'tom yam'.

**Shopping list**

- Whole free-range village chicken — ไก่บ้านทั้งตัว / จิ๊นไก่ — 1 bird (~1.2 kg) — swap abroad: Free-range chicken.
- Northern larb spice powder — พริกลาบ — 2–3 Tbsp — swap abroad: See larb dataset for recipe.
- Makhwaen, toasted and ground — มะแขว่น / บ่ะแขว่น — 1 tsp — swap abroad: Sichuan pepper.
- Spring onion, coriander, mint — ต้นหอม ผักชี สะระแหน่ / ผักหอม — 1 cup
- Salt, fish sauce — เกลือ น้ำปลา — to taste

**Prep ahead:** Boil the chicken whole until cooked; reserve the broth.

**Method**

1. Boil the whole chicken until cooked; keep the broth. (~45 min). *Look/smell for:* Juices run clear; broth golden.
2. Shred the meat and mix with prik laab and makhwaen. (~10 min). *Look/smell for:* Meat evenly coated red.
3. Add broth, bring to the boil, season. (~5 min). *Look/smell for:* Dark red, fragrant, soupy.
4. Add herbs and serve. (~1 min).

**Taste:** Hot, warm-spiced, tingling from makhwaen. (sour 0/5, salty 3, spicy 4, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 4). Texture: Shredded chicken in spiced broth.

**How to eat it:** Eaten sitting together in a circle with elders after the ceremony, with sticky rice. With: Sticky rice (ข้าวนึ่ง) by hand. Etiquette: Let elders start; the meal is part of the blessing.

**Safety:** Cook chicken fully.

**Common mistakes:** Expecting a salad from the word 'yam'

**Say it at the market:**

- ยำจิ๊นไก่ (*yam jin kai*) — Northern spiced chicken soup [Central: ต้มไก่ใส่พริกลาบ (โดยประมาณ)] — Ordering

**Culture:** Su khwan is a ceremony to call back a person's khwan (life essence), believed by many to wander after fright or travel; the ceremonial chicken becomes shared food.

**Sources:** https://krua.co/food_story/northern-thai-style-spicy-chicken-soup
