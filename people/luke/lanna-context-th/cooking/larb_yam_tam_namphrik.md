# Northern Thai larb, yam, tam, sa & nam phrik: a cook-along guide for foreigners

*Built from Thai-language sources (krua.co, Mahidol INMU, CMU Faculty of Medicine, HRDI, Matichon, Mae Noi, Wongnai/aroifin, The101) on 2026-09-26. Kham Mueang = Northern Thai (คำเมือง).*

## Before you start

- **Northern larb isn't Isan larb.** Northern (ลาบเมือง): dry spice powder (พริกลาบ) with warm spices and มะแขว่น, often offal/blood, no lime, no toasted rice. Isan/Lao larb: lime juice, fish sauce, toasted rice (ข้าวคั่ว), simpler; krua.co notes Lao-style larb seasoning is 'less complex than Northern Thailand'.
- **Where to shop:** Chiang Mai: Warorot (Kad Luang) for spices, dried chillies, ready-made พริกลาบ and dried goods; Ton Lamyai (opposite Warorot, across Wichayanon Rd) for fresh vegetables, herbs and meat.
- **How to eat:** everything here is eaten with sticky rice (ข้าวเหนียว), rolled into a small ball with your right hand, plus a basket of fresh or blanched vegetables. The bitter, astringent leaves are part of the balance.
- **Saying thanks:** *ลำขนาดเจ้า* (lam kha-naat jao) means "really delicious" (polite). *เจ้า* is the Northern polite particle and anyone can use it.
- **Pronunciation note:** All tones are DERIVED (not from a recorded pronunciation dictionary) using the Chiang Mai tone-box mapping: high/mid class live unmarked=24 low-rising; low class live unmarked=33 mid; high/mid class + mai ek or dead syllable=21 low-falling; low class + mai ek or dead long syllable=42 high-falling; high/mid class + mai tho=44ʔ high-level-glottal; low class + mai tho or dead short=45 high-rising. Class is taken from the etymological (Tai) initial, so Northern spellings with ๊/๋ (e.g. กิ๋น, จ๊าด) are read as sound-hints. Confidence low; needs native-speaker check. Other Northern provinces (Nan, Phrae, Lampang, Chiang Rai) differ in tone contours.

### Vegetables eaten with larb (ผักกับลาบ)

- **ผักไผ่**: Vietnamese coriander. Peppery; shredded into larb and yam
- **ยอดมะกอก**: Young hog-plum leaves. Sour-astringent
- **ใบพลูคาว**: Fish mint (Houttuynia). Fishy-herbal
- **ใบเล็บครุฑ**: Polyscias leaf. 
- **แตงกวา กะหล่ำปลี ถั่วฝักยาว มะเขือเปราะ มะระขี้นก ใบบัวบก**: Cucumber, cabbage, long bean, Thai eggplant, bitter gourd, gotu kola. Cooling/bitter counterpoints; 'food as medicine' framing

## Northern larb spice mix (dry chilli-spice powder)
**พริกลาบ (เครื่องลาบ)** · Kham Mueang: *พริกลาบ* · `phrik laap (Northern: pik laap)` · confidence: medium

Say it: พริก (phrik / pik, tone 45) · ลาบ (laap, tone 42)

Prik laab is a dry powder of charcoal-roasted dried chillies pounded with toasted spices. It is the soul of Northern larb and very different from Isan larb (which uses toasted rice and lime). Chiang Mai/Lampang styles use many warm spices; Nan/Phrae and eastern Lanna styles use fewer spices but lean on มะแขว่น (makhwaen, a Zanthoxylum related to Sichuan pepper) for a numbing, cool-citrus aroma. Three stages: roast, pound, then mix into the meat.

*Where from:* เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, ลำปาง Lampang, น่าน Nan, แพร่ Phrae, เชียงราย Chiang Rai, พะเยา Phayao. *Season:* All year; fresh-crop มะแขว่น is harvested roughly Oct–Dec (HRDI). *Occasion:* Base for all Northern larb, also yam and some curries. *Serves:* Makes enough for about 1 kg meat (4–5 Tbsp per kg per iKasalong).

**Prep ahead:** Make a batch and keep airtight in a dry, dark place; aroma fades over weeks. Ready-made packs (roughly 20–80 THB per krua.co) are used by many households.

**Steps**
1. Roast the dried chillies over charcoal until fragrant and crisp; do not burn. (เผาพริกแห้งบนเตาถ่านจนหอมกรอบ ระวังไหม้). ~5 min. *Look/smell for:* Chillies darken slightly, become brittle and smell smoky-sweet; sharp fumes mean it's close to burning. *Tip:* Open a window — roasting chillies makes everyone cough.
2. Dry-toast the hard spices (cinnamon, star anise, cloves, cardamom) first on low heat. (คั่วเครื่องเทศเม็ดแข็ง (อบเชย โป๊ยกั๊ก กานพลู กระวาน) ไฟอ่อนก่อน). ~4 min. *Look/smell for:* Warm, sweet smell rises; no smoke. *Tip:* Different spices burn at different speeds — the สล่าลาบ (larb master) roasts them in sequence.
3. Add the softer spices (coriander, cumin, fennel-type seed, makhwaen, long pepper, makhlaep) and toast until fragrant. (ใส่เครื่องเทศที่เหลือ (ลูกผักชี ยี่หร่า เทียนข้าวเปลือก มะแขว่น ดีปลี มะแหลบ) คั่วต่อจนหอม). ~3 min. *Look/smell for:* Seeds start to pop lightly; makhwaen smells citrus-pine. *Tip:* Shake the pan constantly.
4. Pound all the spices finely. (โขลกเครื่องเทศทั้งหมดให้ละเอียด). ~10 min. *Look/smell for:* A fine, fragrant brown powder. *Tip:* Pound in a circular grinding motion rather than hard strikes.
5. Add the roasted chillies and salt; pound together until evenly mixed. (ใส่พริกเผาและเกลือ โขลกรวมให้เข้ากัน). ~5 min. *Look/smell for:* Dark red-brown, dry, very aromatic powder. *Tip:* Taste a pinch: should be smoky-hot, salty, with a tingle from makhwaen.

**Taste:** Smoky, fiercely hot, warm-spiced, with a numbing tingle from makhwaen. (sour 0, salty 2, spicy 5, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 1). Texture: Dry, fine powder.
Taste words: *เผ็ด* (phet) = spicy, *เด้าลิ้น (ชาลิ้น)* (dao lin) = tongue-numbing (makhwaen effect, per krua.co)

**How to eat:** Not eaten alone; mixed into larb, yam jin kai, some curries. Being given a family's homemade prik laab is a real gesture — each master's blend is personal.

**Food safety:** Store dry; discard if mouldy. Roasting chillies releases irritating smoke.
**Common mistakes:** Using Isan larb seasoning (toasted rice + lime) and calling it Northern larb.; Burning spices — bitterness ruins the batch.; Using pre-ground spices that have lost aroma.
**Phrases:** *มีพริกลาบก่อ* (mee pik laap ko) = Do you have larb spice?
**Culture:** The สล่าลาบ (sa-la laap, 'larb master') controls everything from choosing meat to roasting spices; each master's recipe defines local style. krua.co notes some Chiang Mai cooks call sparse eastern styles 'บ่เติง' (not reaching the mark) — an insider in-joke, i.e. regional rivalry, not a fact about quality.

## Northern cooked pork larb
**ลาบหมูคั่ว (ลาบเมือง แบบสุก)** · Kham Mueang: *ลาบคั่ว* · `laap khua (moo)` · confidence: high

Say it: ลาบ (laap, tone 42) · คั่ว (khua, tone 42)

Northern-style cooked larb: pork hand-minced until sticky, mixed with offal and prik laab, then quickly stir-cooked in offal stock until done, finished with Vietnamese coriander (ผักไผ่), spring onion, coriander, fried garlic and shallots. Dry, dark red-brown, smoky and aromatic — no lime, no toasted rice.

*Where from:* All of Lanna; Chiang Mai, Lampang, Nan, Phrae styles differ in spice mix. *Season:* All year; historically festival/feast food. *Occasion:* Celebrations, merit-making, housewarming, gatherings with drinks, welcoming guests; eating larb is said to bring โชคลาภ (luck) because ลาบ sounds like ลาภ. *Serves:* 4–5

|---|---|---|---|
| Lean pork (jin moo) | เนื้อหมูแดง / จิ๊นหมู | 500 g | Pork stall at Ton Lamyai or a local morning market (buy early); abroad: Pork shoulder or loin. |
| Pork offal (liver, small intestine, heart, spleen) (khrueang nai) | เครื่องในหมู (ตับ ไส้อ่อน หัวใจ เซี่ยงจี๊) / เครื่องใน | 150–200 g | Pork stall at Ton Lamyai or a local morning market (buy early); abroad: Pork liver + heart only, or skip offal. |
| Boiled pork skin (nang moo) | หนังหมูต้ม / หนังหมู | 50 g | Pork stall at Ton Lamyai or a local morning market (buy early); abroad: Omit or use crispy pork rind on the side. |

| Spring onion, coriander, sawtooth coriander, mint (ton hom, phak chee) | ต้นหอม ผักชี ผักชีฝรั่ง สะระแหน่ / ต้นหอม, ผักจี | 1 handful total | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Fried garlic and fried shallot (kra-thiam jiao) | กระเทียมเจียว หอมเจียว / กระเทียมเจียว | 2 Tbsp each | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Asian-grocery fried shallots. |

**Prep ahead:** Boil offal and pork skin in advance (liver and spleen only briefly — they toughen); keep the stock. Shred herbs just before serving.

**Steps**
1. Blanch sliced liver and spleen just until cooked; boil the other offal and skin until tender, then slice thinly. Keep the stock. (ลวกตับและเซี่ยงจี๊ที่ซอยแล้วพอสุก ส่วนเครื่องในอื่นและหนังหมูต้มจนสุก ซอยบาง เก็บน้ำต้มไว้). ~30 min. *Look/smell for:* Liver cut surface no longer red; intestine and skin tender when pinched. *Tip:* Over-boiled liver goes hard (Mae Noi tip).
2. Mince the pork by hand with a cleaver until very fine and sticky. (สับหมูด้วยมีดจนละเอียดและเหนียว). ~15 min. *Look/smell for:* Rhythmic 'tok-tok' chopping sound; meat turns tacky and clings to the blade. *Tip:* Traditional larb is never machine-ground; hand-mincing gives the right texture.
3. Mix the minced pork, offal and prik laab thoroughly (if using blood, add now — it must be fully cooked in the next step). (คลุกหมูสับ เครื่องใน และพริกลาบให้เข้ากัน (ถ้าใช้เลือด ให้ผสมตอนนี้และต้องคั่วให้สุกทั่ว)). ~3 min. *Look/smell for:* Uniform red-brown colour; strong spice aroma. *Tip:* Wear gloves — chilli burns.
4. Bring a little offal stock to a boil in the wok, add the larb and stir-cook until fully cooked and just moist. (ตั้งกระทะใส่น้ำต้มเครื่องในเล็กน้อยจนเดือด ใส่ลาบลงคั่วจนสุกทั่ว พอขลุกขลิก). ~6 min. *Look/smell for:* No pink anywhere; blood (if used) turns dark brown; juices bubble; kitchen smells of roasted spice. *Tip:* Cook fast over high heat so the meat stays tender, but make sure it is cooked through.
5. Off the heat, taste and adjust salt, fold in the shredded herbs and top with fried garlic and shallots. (ปิดไฟ ชิมปรุงรส ใส่ผักไผ่ ต้นหอม ผักชี สะระแหน่ โรยกระเทียมเจียว หอมเจียว). ~2 min. *Look/smell for:* Herbs wilt slightly and release a fresh peppery smell. *Tip:* Do not add lime — that's Isan style.

**Taste:** Hot, smoky, warm-spiced and savoury, with a slight bitterness from offal/herbs — not sour, not sweet. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 4, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 4). Texture: Fine, moist-dry mince with chewy offal and crunchy fried garlic.
Taste words: *ลำ* (lam) = delicious, *จ๊าดลำ* (jaat lam) = very delicious, *เผ็ด* (phet) = spicy

**How to eat:** Eat with sticky rice (ข้าวเหนียว) rolled into small balls with the right hand; pinch some larb with the rice. Alternate with ผักกับลาบ (larb vegetables): cucumber, cabbage, young hog-plum leaves (ยอดมะกอก), fish-mint leaf (ใบพลูคาว), Polyscias leaf (ใบเล็บครุฑ), long beans, Thai eggplant — plus pork rind. Served in one central dish; take small amounts with rice using your right hand or a spoon; bitter/astringent vegetables are meant to balance the richness.

**Food safety:** Cook pork, offal and especially blood completely (no pink, blood fully browned). Raw pork and raw blood in Northern dishes are linked to Streptococcus suis (ไข้หูดับ — can cause deafness, sepsis, death), trichinosis and pork tapeworm per CMU Faculty of Medicine. Buy fresh, refrigerate, eat the same day.
**Common mistakes:** Adding lime juice and toasted rice (Isan style).; Using a food processor — texture turns mushy.; Undercooking blood or offal.; Skipping the herbs and vegetables, which are part of the dish.
**Phrases:** *ลำขนาดเจ้า* (lam kha-naat jao) = It's really delicious (polite); *ลำแต้ๆ* (lam tae-tae) = Truly tasty; *กิ๋นข้าวกั๋นเจ้า* (kin khao kan jao) = Let's eat (polite); *เอาลาบคั่วเจ้า บ่เอาดิบ* (ao laap khua jao, bo ao dip) = Cooked larb please, not raw
**Culture:** Larb is feast food; some Northerners say eating it brings โชคลาภ (luck) — a pun on ลาบ/ลาภ — and being treated to larb is an honour. Historically, larb for big occasions (slaughtering a pig/buffalo, a hunt) was made by men; krua.co notes larb made by women was called 'ลาบแม่'. Many larb shops in Chiang Mai also sell ลาบควาย (buffalo) and ลาบเนื้อ.

## Raw larb and luu (raw blood dish) — cultural description only, NO recipe
**ลาบดิบ และ หลู้ (ข้อมูลเชิงวัฒนธรรมเท่านั้น)** · Kham Mueang: *ลาบดิบ, หลู้* · `laap dip, luu` · confidence: high

Say it: ลาบ (laap, tone 42) · ดิบ (dip, tone 21) · หลู้ (luu, tone 44ʔ)

Raw larb (ลาบดิบ) uses raw minced meat, offal and blood. หลู้ (luu) is centred on fresh raw pig (sometimes cattle/buffalo) blood mixed with minced meat, prik laab, herbs and crispy fried bits, often served with มันแผว (a lacy sheet of caul fat). They belong with ส้า to the family of uncooked Northern dishes. This entry is cultural only — we deliberately give no preparation steps.

*Where from:* Widespread in Lanna. *Season:* All year; associated with festivals and Songkran gatherings. *Occasion:* Traditional male-led feast food; drinking sessions. *Serves:* n/a

**Prep ahead:** Not applicable — we do not provide instructions for raw pork/blood dishes.

**Taste:** Spice-heavy, iron-rich, with bitter herbs; prik laab is said to mask the 'gamey' smell. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 4, sweet 0, bitter 2, umami 4). Texture: Soft, wet, with crunchy fried garnish (as described in sources).

**How to eat:** Eaten with local vegetables, pickles and sticky rice. If offered raw larb or luu, it is fine to politely decline: 'ขอลาบคั่วเจ้า' (cooked larb please). Hosts are used to guests preferring cooked.

**Food safety:** STRONG WARNING: Thai doctors (CMU Faculty of Medicine, Ramathibodi, provincial health reports in Thairath) repeatedly warn that raw pork, raw pork blood and raw beef in ลาบดิบ, หลู้, ส้า and ซอยจุ๊ can transmit Streptococcus suis (ไข้หูดับ: meningitis, sepsis, permanent deafness, death — deaths are reported most years, often around Songkran), trichinosis, pork and beef tapeworm (cysts can reach the brain). Alcohol and chilli do not make it safe. Do not eat; seek medical care promptly for fever, headache or hearing loss after eating raw pork.
**Common mistakes:** Assuming spice or alcohol 'kills' pathogens.; Feeling obliged to eat it to be polite — declining is normal.
**Phrases:** *บ่กิ๋นดิบเจ้า* (bo kin dip jao) = I don't eat raw (polite)
**Culture:** These raw dishes are tied historically to men's hunting and feast culture. They remain valued by some Northerners as heritage, while many — including cooks and shops — now prefer or recommend the cooked versions because of health risks. Present them respectfully, without sensationalising.

## Northern-style chicken larb (cooked)
**ลาบไก่เมือง (ลาบไก่คั่ว)** · Kham Mueang: *ลาบไก่* · `laap kai (mueang)` · confidence: low

Say it: ลาบ (laap, tone 42) · ไก่ (kai, tone 21)

A Northern chicken larb follows the same method as cooked pork larb: hand-minced chicken (with liver/gizzard if liked) mixed with prik laab, cooked in chicken stock, finished with Vietnamese coriander and fried garlic. This is an adaptation of the documented pork method — distinct from Isan larb kai (toasted rice + lime).

*Where from:* Lanna-wide. *Season:* All year *Occasion:* Everyday and festive; ไก่บ้าน (free-range chicken) preferred. *Serves:* 3–4

**Prep ahead:** Simmer bones for stock; boil gizzard until tender.

**Steps**
1. Boil gizzards until tender; blanch liver just until cooked; slice thinly. (ต้มกึ๋นให้นุ่ม ลวกตับพอสุก ซอยบาง). ~25 min. *Look/smell for:* Liver firm and brown inside. *Tip:* Same logic as pork: don't overboil liver.
2. Hand-mince the chicken finely. (สับไก่ด้วยมีดจนละเอียด). ~10 min. *Look/smell for:* Sticky, fine mince. *Tip:* Include a little skin for richness.
3. Mix mince with prik laab. (คลุกไก่สับกับพริกลาบ). ~2 min. *Look/smell for:* Even red-brown colour.
4. Stir-cook in simmering stock until fully cooked; add offal. (คั่วกับน้ำต้มไก่จนสุกทั่ว ใส่เครื่องใน). ~6 min. *Look/smell for:* No pink; meat turns opaque and crumbly; juices mostly absorbed. *Tip:* Poultry must be cooked through (74°C).
5. Off heat, season, fold in herbs and top with fried garlic. (ปิดไฟ ปรุงรส ใส่ผักและกระเทียมเจียว). ~2 min. *Look/smell for:* Fresh herb aroma. *Tip:* No lime.

**Taste:** Lighter than pork larb, spicy and aromatic. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 4, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 3). Texture: Fine, moist mince.
Taste words: *ลำ* (lam) = delicious

**How to eat:** With sticky rice and fresh vegetables. Shared central plate.

**Food safety:** Never serve chicken larb raw or pink; cook to 74°C.
**Common mistakes:** Confusing with Isan larb gai (lime + khao khua).
**Phrases:** *ลำขนาดเจ้า* (lam kha-naat jao) = It's really delicious (polite); *ลำแต้ๆ* (lam tae-tae) = Truly tasty; *กิ๋นข้าวกั๋นเจ้า* (kin khao kan jao) = Let's eat (polite)
**Culture:** Adaptation note: exact Northern chicken-larb recipe not verified from a Thai source this round; method follows documented cooked pork larb. Needs cook review.

## Pounded young jackfruit with chilli paste and pork
**ตำขนุน** · Kham Mueang: *ตำบ่าหนุน* · `tam khanun / tam ba-nun` · confidence: medium

Say it: ตำ (tam, tone 24) · บ่า (ba, tone 21) · หนุน (nun, tone 24)

Tam khanun is a Lanna home dish: boiled young (green) jackfruit pounded with a chilli paste of dried chillies, garlic, shallot, galangal, lemongrass and fermented fish, then stir-fried with pork and tomatoes and topped with fried kaffir-lime leaves and garlic. Savoury, rich, mildly hot — more like a warm pounded relish than a salad.

*Where from:* Lanna-wide. *Season:* Young jackfruit is available most of the year, especially hot/rainy season. *Occasion:* Everyday; also served at merit-making and gatherings (CMU art-culture has an article on jackfruit in Lanna belief — not accessed; see gaps). *Serves:* 4–6

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Young green jackfruit (ba-nun on) | ขนุนอ่อน / บ่าหนุนอ่อน | 500–800 g (whole, before peeling) | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Canned young green jackfruit in brine (rinse well). |
| Pork belly or minced pork (jin moo) | หมูสามชั้น/หมูสับ / จิ๊นหมู | 150 g | Pork stall at Ton Lamyai or a local morning market (buy early); abroad: Same; vegetarian versions exist. |
| Small sour tomatoes (ba-khuea som) | มะเขือเทศลูกเล็ก / บะเขือส้ม | 80–150 g | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Cherry tomatoes. |
| Dried chillies (phrik haeng) | พริกแห้ง / พริกแห้ง | 5–10 | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Same. |
| Garlic, shallots (kra-thiam, hom daeng) | กระเทียม หอมแดง / กระเทียม, บะหอม(?) | 1 head / 4–6 bulbs | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Galangal, lemongrass (kha, ta-khrai) | ข่า ตะไคร้ / ข่า, ตะไคร้ | 3 slices / 1 stalk | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Chopped fermented fish (or shrimp paste) (pla-ra) | ปลาร้าสับ (หรือกะปิ) / ฮ้า (ปลาร้า — Northern term unverified) | 1 Tbsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Anchovy paste or fish sauce. |
| Kaffir lime leaves (bai ma-krut) | ใบมะกรูด / ใบมะกรูด | 3–5, shredded & fried | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Frozen lime leaves. |
| Oil, fried garlic (nam man) | น้ำมันพืช กระเทียมเจียว / — | 3–5 Tbsp | —; abroad: Same. |

**Prep ahead:** Peel (oil your knife — jackfruit sap is very sticky), cut and boil jackfruit until tender, then cool. Mahidol INMU's recipe boils then pounds it.

**Steps**
1. Peel young jackfruit, cut into chunks and boil until tender. (ปอกขนุนอ่อน หั่นชิ้น ต้มจนนุ่ม). ~30 min. *Look/smell for:* A knife slides in easily; flesh turns pale and stringy. *Tip:* Oil hands and knife against the sticky latex.
2. Pound dried chillies, garlic, shallots, galangal and lemongrass to a paste; add the fermented fish. (โขลกพริกแห้ง กระเทียม หอมแดง ข่า ตะไคร้ ให้ละเอียด ใส่ปลาร้าโขลกรวม). ~10 min. *Look/smell for:* Thick, dark-red paste, pungent. *Tip:* Roasting chillies/shallots/garlic first deepens flavour.
3. Pound the boiled jackfruit in the mortar until broken into shreds. (โขลกขนุนต้มในครกพอแหลก). ~5 min. *Look/smell for:* Stringy, 'pulled' texture, like shredded meat. *Tip:* Don't make it a purée.
4. Fry garlic in oil, add pork and cook through, add the paste and fry until fragrant, then tomatoes. (เจียวกระเทียม ใส่หมูผัดจนสุก ใส่น้ำพริกผัดจนหอม ใส่มะเขือเทศ). ~8 min. *Look/smell for:* Oil turns red; strong chilli-garlic aroma; tomatoes soften.
5. Add jackfruit, toss well; top with fried kaffir-lime leaves, fried garlic, coriander, spring onion. (ใส่ขนุนคลุกให้เข้ากัน โรยใบมะกรูดทอด กระเทียมเจียว ผักชี ต้นหอม). ~3 min. *Look/smell for:* Evenly coated, glossy, not wet.

**Taste:** Salty, rich, mildly hot, with lime-leaf aroma (Mae Noi: 'เค็มๆ มันๆ ตามด้วยเผ็ดนิดๆ'). (sour 1, salty 3, spicy 2, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 4). Texture: Shredded, fibrous, moist.
Taste words: *ลำ* (lam) = delicious

**How to eat:** With sticky rice and pork cracklings (แคบหมู) or salty stir-fried pork belly. Shared dish in the middle.

**Food safety:** Jackfruit latex can irritate skin. Fermented fish should be cooked/roasted.
**Common mistakes:** Using ripe (sweet yellow) jackfruit.; Puréeing instead of shredding.
**Phrases:** *บ่าหนุนอ่อนมีก่อ* (ba-nun on mee ko) = Do you have young jackfruit?
**Culture:** Jackfruit is บ่าหนุน in Kham Mueang. A common Thai association links ขนุน with 'หนุน' (support) as auspicious; CMU's Lanna art-culture office has an article 'ขนุนในความเชื่อของคนล้านนา' that could not be accessed — treat the belief link as unverified.

## Northern chicken 'yam' (spiced shredded chicken in broth)
**ยำจิ๊นไก่** · Kham Mueang: *ยำจิ๊นไก่* · `yam jin kai` · confidence: medium

Say it: ยำ (yam, tone 33) · จิ๊น (jin, tone 45) · ไก่ (kai, tone 21)

Not a Central-Thai style salad: it's a brothy, curry-like dish. Chicken boiled for a ritual offering is shredded; prik laab is fried until fragrant, chicken broth is added, then the chicken and herbs. Result: dark-red, aromatic, spicy broth with tender chicken.

*Where from:* Lanna-wide. *Season:* All year; especially after household rituals. *Occasion:* Made from the boiled chicken offered in สู่ขวัญ (soul-calling) or household spirit offerings; eaten communally after the ceremony. *Serves:* 4–5

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole free-range chicken, boiled with galangal, lemongrass, lime leaves (kai) | ไก่บ้านทั้งตัว (ต้มกับข่า ตะไคร้ ใบมะกรูด) / ไก่ | 1 small bird (~1–1.2 kg) | Pork stall at Ton Lamyai or a local morning market (buy early); abroad: Whole chicken. |
| Prik laab (phrik laap) | พริกลาบ (คั่ว) / พริกลาบ | ~20 g (2 Tbsp) | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Homemade. |
| Shallots (hom daeng) | หอมแดง / บะหอม(?) | 5 | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Vietnamese coriander, spring onion, coriander (phak phai) | ผักไผ่ ต้นหอม ผักชี / ผักไผ่ | 1 handful | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Oil (nam man) | น้ำมัน / — | 1 Tbsp | —; abroad: Same. |
| Salt/fish sauce (kluea) | เกลือ/น้ำปลา / — | to taste | —; abroad: Same. |

**Prep ahead:** Simmer the chicken with galangal, lemongrass and lime leaves (Maggi recipe: 1–2 h low heat); keep the broth. Shred meat by hand.

**Steps**
1. Simmer chicken with galangal, lemongrass and lime leaves until tender. (ต้มไก่กับข่า ตะไคร้ ใบมะกรูด ไฟอ่อนจนเปื่อย). ~60 min. *Look/smell for:* Meat pulls from bone; broth fragrant. *Tip:* Traditionally this is the offering chicken.
2. Shred the chicken into bite-size pieces. (ฉีกไก่เป็นชิ้นพอดีคำ). ~10 min. *Tip:* Keep skin for flavour.
3. Fry prik laab in a little oil on low heat until fragrant, add broth and bring to a boil. (ใส่น้ำมันในหม้อไฟอ่อน คั่วพริกลาบจนหอม เติมน้ำต้มไก่ เร่งไฟ). ~5 min. *Look/smell for:* Spice smell blooms; broth turns dark red. *Tip:* Low heat — spices burn quickly.
4. Add chicken and shallots, season, return to the boil. (ใส่ไก่ หอมแดง ปรุงรส รอเดือด). ~5 min. 
5. Stir in herbs and turn off heat. (ใส่ผัก คนให้เข้ากัน ปิดไฟ). ~1 min. *Look/smell for:* Herbs just wilt.

**Taste:** Warm-spiced, savoury, moderately hot broth. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 4). Texture: Brothy with tender shreds.
Taste words: *ลำ* (lam) = delicious

**How to eat:** Eaten sitting in a circle together after the ritual, with sticky rice; often with drinks. Communal; guests are welcomed to join after a ceremony.

**Food safety:** Offering chicken may sit out during a ritual — reheat to boiling before eating.
**Common mistakes:** Expecting a lime-dressed salad because of the word ยำ.
**Phrases:** *ลำขนาดเจ้า* (lam kha-naat jao) = It's really delicious (polite); *ลำแต้ๆ* (lam tae-tae) = Truly tasty; *กิ๋นข้าวกั๋นเจ้า* (kin khao kan jao) = Let's eat (polite)
**Culture:** A by-product of Lanna ritual life: offerings in สู่ขวัญ (soul-calling) and household spirit worship commonly include a boiled chicken, which is then turned into this dish and shared. Practices vary by family and village.

## Northern wild-greens 'sa' with fish dressing
**ส้าผัก** · Kham Mueang: *ส้าผัก* · `saa phak` · confidence: medium

Say it: ส้า (saa, tone 44ʔ) · ผัก (phak, tone 21)

ส้า is a Northern 'mixed/tossed' dish, perhaps related to Central Thai พล่า. Sa phak tosses at least five kinds of young leaves/shoots (e.g. mango, cassava, forest shoots) with a dressing made like nam phrik pla: charred green chillies, shallots, garlic and fish boiled with lemongrass and shrimp paste, pounded and loosened with the fish broth. Tastes: sour, bitter, rich, sweet, astringent — refreshing.

*Where from:* Lanna-wide. *Season:* Late dry season to early rains when young shoots (ยอดผัก) appear. *Occasion:* Everyday. *Serves:* 3–4

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed young shoots/leaves (mango, cassava, forest greens) (yot phak) | ยอดผักอ่อนรวม (ยอดมะม่วง ยอดมันสำปะหลัง ฯลฯ) / ยอดผัก | 200 g, ≥5 kinds | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Mix of watercress, young mustard, rocket, sorrel, dandelion. |
| Freshwater fish (snakehead/tilapia) (pla) | ปลา (ปลาช่อน/ปลานิล) / ป๋า | 1 fish (~300 g) | Pork stall at Ton Lamyai or a local morning market (buy early); abroad: Any white fish. |
| Long green Northern chillies (phrik num) | พริกหนุ่ม / พริกหนุ่ม | 4–5 | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Anaheim + a few bird's-eye. |
| Shallot, garlic (hom daeng, kra-thiam) | หอมแดง กระเทียม / — | 3 / 1 head | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Lemongrass, shrimp paste (ta-khrai, ka-pi) | ตะไคร้ กะปิ / — | 1 stalk / 1 tsp | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Spring onion, coriander (ton hom, phak chee) | ต้นหอม ผักชี / — | garnish | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |

**Prep ahead:** Poach fish with lemongrass and shrimp paste; flake flesh, keep broth.

**Steps**
1. Poach fish with lemongrass and shrimp paste; flake the flesh, keep the broth. (ต้มปลากับตะไคร้และกะปิ แกะเนื้อ เก็บน้ำต้ม). ~15 min. *Look/smell for:* Flesh white and flaky. *Tip:* Remove all bones.
2. Char green chillies, shallots, garlic; peel and pound with the fish. (เผาพริกหนุ่ม หอมแดง กระเทียม ลอกเปลือก โขลกรวมกับเนื้อปลา). ~10 min. *Look/smell for:* Fluffy, pale-green paste.
3. Loosen with fish broth to a dressing. (เติมน้ำต้มปลาให้เหลว). ~1 min. *Look/smell for:* Pourable, like thick soup.
4. Toss with shoots; garnish with spring onion and coriander. (คลุกกับยอดผัก โรยต้นหอม ผักชี). ~2 min. *Tip:* Toss just before eating.

**Taste:** Sour, bitter, rich, sweetish and astringent — fresh and green. (sour 2, salty 2, spicy 2, sweet 1, bitter 3, umami 3). Texture: Crisp-tender leaves in creamy fish dressing.
Taste words: *ขมแก๊ก* (khom kaek) = very bitter, *ฝาดหยั่งก้นตุ๊* (faat yang kon tu) = extremely astringent (idiom), *ส้มโจ๊ะโล๊ะ* (som jo-lo) = very sour

**How to eat:** With sticky rice, alongside a grilled or meat dish. Shared.

**Food safety:** Only eat wild shoots identified by locals; cassava leaves must be young and should be cooked/blanched in many practices (cyanogenic) — verify local practice. Cook fish fully. Note: some ส้า variants use raw meat (CMU Medicine warns) — this version is fish-cooked.
**Common mistakes:** Foraging unknown leaves.; Confusing with raw-meat ส้า variants.
**Phrases:** *ผักนี้กิ๋นได้ก่อ* (phak nii kin dai ko) = Is this vegetable edible?
**Culture:** The101.world author speculates ส้า may be the same word as Central Thai พล่า — a hypothesis, not established etymology.

## Sliced raw Thai eggplant tossed with chilli, crab paste and fermented fish
**ส้ามะเขือ (ส้ามะเขือแจ้)** · Kham Mueang: *ส้าบ่าเขือ(?)* · `saa makhuea (jae)` · confidence: medium

Say it: ส้า (saa, tone 44ʔ) · มะ (ma, tone 45) · เขือ (khuea, tone 24) · แจ้ (jae, tone 44ʔ)

Thinly sliced small local eggplants, soaked in salt water to remove bitterness, tossed with a pounded paste of charred green chillies, shallots, garlic and shrimp paste plus nam puu, fermented-fish sauce and sliced lemongrass.

*Where from:* Lanna-wide. *Season:* All year *Occasion:* Everyday side dish. *Serves:* 3

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small round local eggplant (ma-khuea jae) | มะเขือแจ้ / บ่าเขือแจ้(?) | 5 | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Thai apple eggplant. |
| Charred green chillies (phrik num) | พริกหนุ่มเผา / พริกหนุ่ม | 3–4 | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Anaheim/jalapeño. |
| Crab paste (nam puu) | น้ำปู๋ / น้ำปู๋ | 2 Tbsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Shrimp paste. |
| Sliced lemongrass (ta-khrai) | ตะไคร้ซอย / — | 1 Tbsp | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Shallot, garlic (hom daeng, kra-thiam) | หอมแดง กระเทียม / — | 2 / 1 head | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Shrimp paste (ka-pi) | กะปิ / — | 1/2 tsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Same. |
| Fermented fish sauce (nam pla-ra) | น้ำปลาร้า / — | 1 Tbsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Fish sauce. |
| Salt flakes (dok kluea) | ดอกเกลือ / — | pinch | —; abroad: Sea salt. |

**Prep ahead:** Slice eggplant thinly into salted water just before mixing.

**Steps**
1. Char chillies, shallots, garlic; pound with shrimp paste. (เผาพริก หอม กระเทียม โขลกกับกะปิให้ละเอียด). ~12 min. 
2. Slice eggplant thinly, soak in salt water, squeeze dry. (ฝานมะเขือบาง แช่น้ำเกลือ บีบให้แห้ง). ~10 min. *Look/smell for:* Slices stay pale, not brown.
3. Toss eggplant, lemongrass, paste, nam puu and fermented-fish sauce. (คลุกมะเขือ ตะไคร้ เครื่องโขลก น้ำปู๋ น้ำปลาร้า). ~3 min. *Look/smell for:* Slices coated grey-green.

**Taste:** Salty, funky, hot, crunchy. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 4). Texture: Crunchy raw slices.

**How to eat:** With pork dishes and sticky rice. Shared.

**Food safety:** Use cooked (boiled) fermented-fish sauce; raw pla-ra carries liver-fluke risk in some regions.
**Common mistakes:** Skipping the salt-water soak.
**Culture:** Kham Mueang name for eggplant (บ่าเขือ) is derived from the บะ/บ่า fruit-prefix pattern (cf. บะเขือส้ม = tomato) — verify.

## Galangal chilli dip
**น้ำพริกข่า** · Kham Mueang: *น้ำพริกข่า* · `nam phrik kha (Northern: nam pik kha)` · confidence: medium

Say it: น้ำ (nam, tone 45) · พริก (phrik/pik, tone 45) · ข่า (kha, tone 21)

A dry, fiery galangal dip: roasted dried chillies pounded with old galangal, garlic, lemongrass, salt (and in some recipes มะแขว่น), then dry-fried. Classic with steamed beef (จิ๊นนึ่ง) and steamed wild mushrooms like เห็ดถอบ/เห็ดเผาะ.

*Where from:* Lanna-wide; nearly every province has its own version (Greenery). *Season:* Rainy season classic with เห็ดถอบ/เห็ดเผาะ (wild earthstar mushrooms). *Occasion:* Everyday. *Serves:* 2

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large dried chillies, charred (phrik haeng) | พริกแห้งเม็ดใหญ่เผา / พริกแห้ง | 5 | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Dried chillies. |
| Mature galangal (kha) | ข่าแก่ / ข่า | 3 pieces (~1 inch) | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Galangal (Asian grocery). |
| Garlic (kra-thiam) | กระเทียม / — | 15 small cloves | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Lemongrass (ta-khrai) | ตะไคร้ / — | 1 stalk | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Makhwaen (ma-khwaen) | มะแขว่น / มะแขว่น | ~1 tsp (reading of krua recipe; verify) | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Sichuan pepper. |
| Sea salt (kluea) | เกลือสมุทร / — | 1/2 tsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Same. |

**Prep ahead:** Roast chillies; some cooks also roast galangal slices until golden (Greenery).

**Steps**
1. Pound chillies until cracked, add galangal and pound coarsely. (โขลกพริกแห้งพอแหลก ใส่ข่าโขลกพอหยาบ). ~5 min. *Tip:* Pound salt with chillies to help grind.
2. Add garlic, lemongrass and makhwaen; pound fine. (ใส่กระเทียม ตะไคร้ มะแขว่น โขลกต่อจนละเอียด). ~8 min. *Look/smell for:* Coarse, fragrant, reddish-brown paste.
3. Dry-fry over low heat until dry; salt to taste. (คั่วในกระทะไฟอ่อนจนแห้ง ปรุงเกลือ). ~5 min. *Look/smell for:* Crumbly, dry; strong galangal perfume. *Tip:* Stir constantly; it scorches fast.

**Taste:** Salty, sharply hot, perfumed with galangal. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 5, sweet 0, bitter 0, umami 1). Texture: Dry, crumbly.
Taste words: *เผ็ด* (phet) = spicy

**How to eat:** Dab onto steamed mushrooms, steamed beef or vegetables with sticky rice. Small bowl shared; dip with rice ball or vegetable.

**Food safety:** WILD MUSHROOMS: only buy from trusted market vendors; poisonous look-alikes cause deaths in Northern/NE Thailand most rainy seasons. Never forage yourself.
**Common mistakes:** Using young (pink) galangal — too mild.; Eating it by the spoonful — it's a condiment.
**Phrases:** *เผ็ดขนาด* (phet kha-naat) = Very spicy!
**Culture:** krua.co: 'คนเมืองมักกินคู่กับเนื้อวัวนึ่ง เห็ดนึ่ง'.

## 'Red-eye' dried-chilli dip
**น้ำพริกตาแดง** · Kham Mueang: *น้ำพริกตาแดง* · `nam phrik ta daeng` · confidence: high

Say it: น้ำ (nam, tone 45) · พริก (pik, tone 45) · ตา (ta, tone 24) · แดง (daeng, tone 24)

Salty, hot, smoky dip of charcoal-roasted dried chillies, shallots and garlic pounded with salt, banana-leaf-roasted fermented fish and grilled dried fish.

*Where from:* Lanna-wide. *Season:* All year *Occasion:* Everyday pantry dip. *Serves:* 4

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large dried chillies (phrik haeng) | พริกแห้งเม็ดใหญ่ / พริกแห้ง | 7 | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Same. |
| Shallots (hom daeng) | หอมแดง / — | 7 | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Thai garlic (kra-thiam) | กระเทียมไทย / — | 2 heads | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Chopped fermented fish (pla-ra) | ปลาร้าสับ / — | 2 Tbsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Anchovy paste. |
| Grilled dried fish, flaked & pounded (pla haeng) | ปลาแห้งย่างแกะเนื้อ / ป๋าแห้ง(?) | 3 Tbsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Dried anchovy/bonito flakes. |
| Sea salt (kluea) | เกลือสมุทร / — | 1 tsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Same. |

**Prep ahead:** Wrap pla-ra in several layers of banana leaf and roast until fragrant.

**Steps**
1. Roast chillies on low charcoal until crisp; roast shallots and garlic, peel burnt skins; roast banana-leaf-wrapped pla-ra. (เผาพริกแห้งไฟอ่อนจนหอมกรอบ เผาหอมกระเทียมจนหอม ลอกเปลือกไหม้ ห่อปลาร้าใบตองหมกไฟ). ~15 min. *Look/smell for:* Smoky-sweet; pla-ra parcel smells savoury. *Tip:* Don't burn chillies.
2. Pound chillies with salt, add shallot and garlic, then pla-ra and dried fish; pound to combine. (โขลกพริกกับเกลือให้ละเอียด ใส่หอมกระเทียมโขลก ใส่ปลาร้า ปลาแห้งโขลกให้เข้ากัน). ~10 min. *Look/smell for:* Thick, dark-red, fluffy paste.

**Taste:** Salty, hot, smoky, fishy-savoury. (sour 0, salty 4, spicy 4, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 4). Texture: Thick, slightly flaky.

**How to eat:** With fresh and blanched vegetables, mushrooms and pork rind. Shared bowl.

**Food safety:** Roast pla-ra before use.
**Common mistakes:** Burnt chillies make it bitter.
**Culture:** Commercial versions sold at Warorot are often sweeter; home Northern versions are 'not sweet'.

Sources: https://krua.co/recipe/red-eye-chili-paste, https://krua.co/food_story/northen-chilli-paste-recipe

## Northern fish chilli dip (green chilli base)
**น้ำพริกปลา** · Kham Mueang: *น้ำพริกป๋า(?)* · `nam phrik pla` · confidence: high

Say it: น้ำ (nam, tone 45) · พริก (pik, tone 45) · ปลา (pa, tone 24)

Built on the nam phrik num (roasted green chilli) base, seasoned hot and salty with pla-ra and shrimp paste, with steamed fish flesh pounded until fluffy.

*Where from:* Lanna-wide. *Season:* All year *Occasion:* Everyday *Serves:* 4

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Snakehead fish (grilled or steamed) (pla chon) | ปลาช่อน / ป๋าช่อน(?) | 1 fish | Pork stall at Ton Lamyai or a local morning market (buy early); abroad: Any firm white fish. |
| Green chillies (phrik num) | พริกหนุ่ม / พริกหนุ่ม | 5 | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Anaheim + bird's-eye. |
| Shallots (hom daeng) | หอมแดง / — | 3 | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Garlic (kra-thiam) | กระเทียม / — | 1 head | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Shrimp paste (ka-pi) | กะปิ / — | 1 tsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Same. |
| Chopped pla-ra (pla-ra) | ปลาร้าสับ / — | 1 Tbsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Anchovy. |
| Salt, spring onion, coriander (kluea) | เกลือ ต้นหอม ผักชี / — | to taste | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |

**Prep ahead:** Steam the gutted fish; flake.

**Steps**
1. Scale, gut and steam fish until done; flake flesh. (ขอดเกล็ด ควักไส้ นึ่งปลาจนสุก แกะเนื้อ). ~20 min. *Look/smell for:* Flesh opaque, flakes.
2. Roast chillies, shallots, garlic in embers; peel. Roast shrimp paste and pla-ra in separate banana-leaf parcels. (หมกพริกหนุ่ม หอม กระเทียม ในเตาถ่าน ลอกเปลือก ห่อกะปิ ปลาร้าใบตองหมกพอหอม). ~15 min. 
3. Pound chillies, shallots, garlic coarsely; add pastes and fish; season with salt. (โขลกพริก หอม กระเทียมพอหยาบ ใส่กะปิ ปลาร้า เนื้อปลา ปรุงเกลือ). ~8 min. *Look/smell for:* Pale-green, fluffy.
4. Top with spring onion and coriander. (โรยต้นหอม ผักชี). ~1 min. 

**Taste:** Hot, salty, smoky, fishy. (sour 0, salty 3, spicy 4, sweet 1, bitter 0, umami 4). Texture: Fluffy, coarse.

**How to eat:** With fresh, boiled and steamed vegetables (long beans, bamboo, pumpkin, cucumber, eggplant, young กระถิน). Shared.

**Food safety:** Cook fish fully (freshwater fish can carry liver fluke if raw).
**Common mistakes:** Leaving small bones in.
**Culture:** Also used as the base dressing for ส้าผัก.

Sources: https://krua.co/recipe/namprikpra, https://krua.co/food_story/northen-chilli-paste-recipe, https://www.the101.world/kab-khoa-kab-khaek-18/

## Fermented mustard-green chilli dip
**น้ำพริกน้ำผัก** · Kham Mueang: *น้ำพริกน้ำผัก* · `nam phrik nam phak` · confidence: medium

Say it: น้ำ (nam, tone 45) · พริก (pik, tone 45) · น้ำ (nam, tone 45) · ผัก (phak, tone 21)

A very old-fashioned Northern dish (krua.co: many non-Northerners have never heard of it). Mustard greens are fermented with sticky rice, salt and rice-rinsing water until sour, then boiled down to a thick 'nam phak', and mixed with roasted chilli, garlic, salt and makhwaen.

*Where from:* Lanna (old-style); regional spread unverified. *Season:* Cool season when mustard greens are plentiful (derived; verify) *Occasion:* Everyday, old-style *Serves:* 6

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Qty | Buy / swap abroad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mustard greens (phak kaat) | ผักกาดเขียว / ผักกาด | 1 kg | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Mustard greens. |
| Steamed sticky rice (khao niao) | ข้าวเหนียวนึ่ง / ข้าวนึ่ง (common Northern term; not verified this round) | 1/4 cup | —; abroad: Cooked glutinous rice. |
| Salt (kluea) | เกลือสมุทร / — | 2 tsp + 1/2 tsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Same. |
| Rice-rinsing water (nam sao khao) | น้ำซาวข้าว / — | 1/2 cup | —; abroad: Same. |
| Toasted ground dried chillies (phrik pon) | พริกขี้หนูแห้งคั่วป่น / พริกป่น | 1.5 Tbsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Chilli flakes. |
| Garlic, pounded (kra-thiam) | กระเทียม / — | 5 cloves | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |
| Roasted pounded makhwaen (ma-khwaen) | มะแขว่นคั่วโขลก / มะแขว่น | 1 tsp | Warorot (Kad Luang) spice & dried-goods stalls; abroad: Sichuan pepper. |
| Spring onion, coriander (ton hom, phak chee) | ต้นหอม ผักชี / — | 1 each | Ton Lamyai market or any neighbourhood kad (fresh market) vegetable stall; abroad: Same. |

**Prep ahead:** Ferment 24 h in sun (krua) to ~3 days salted (Prachatai blog version).

**Steps**
1. Wash and finely chop mustard greens; mix with sticky rice and salt; pack in a clay pot, add rice water, cover and leave in the sun ~24 h until sour. (ล้างผักกาด สับละเอียด คลุกข้าวเหนียวและเกลือ ใส่หม้อดิน เทน้ำซาวข้าว ปิดฝา ตากแดด 24 ชม. จนเปรี้ยว). ~1440 min. *Look/smell for:* Pleasant sour, pickle smell; slight bubbles. *Tip:* If it smells rotten or grows fuzzy mould, discard.
2. Strain, chop solids finer, and simmer liquid and solids together on low heat until thick; cool. (กรองน้ำ สับผักให้ละเอียดขึ้น เคี่ยวรวมไฟอ่อนจนข้น พักให้เย็น). ~40 min. *Look/smell for:* Thick, olive-brown, sour aroma.
3. Mix 1 cup with chilli, garlic, salt and makhwaen; balance hot-salty-sour; add herbs. (ตวงน้ำผัก 1 ถ้วย ใส่พริกป่น กระเทียม เกลือ มะแขว่น ชิมให้เผ็ดเค็มเปรี้ยว ใส่ต้นหอมผักชี). ~5 min. 

**Taste:** Sour, salty, hot — an appetite-stimulating fermented dip. (sour 4, salty 3, spicy 3, sweet 0, bitter 1, umami 2). Texture: Thick, pulpy.
Taste words: *ส้ม* (som) = sour

**How to eat:** With fresh vegetables, boiled bamboo shoot and grilled catfish. Shared.

**Food safety:** Home fermentation: clean vessels; discard mould/off smells; boiling step makes it safer.
**Common mistakes:** Skipping the boil-down.
**Phrases:** *ของกิ๋นบะเก่า* (khong kin ba-kao) = old-time food
**Culture:** A Prachatai blogger notes younger people no longer know how to make it — a disappearing dish.
