# Northern Thai noodles, snacks, sweets and drinks: a cook-along guide for foreigners

*Built from Thai-language sources (Krua.co, Matichon, Bangkok Biz News, Thairath, Wongnai, HRDI, Agri News, Lanna Post, Chiang Mai News and others), retrieved 2026-09-26. Kham Mueang = Northern Thai (คำเมือง). The tones are derived from spelling rules (see the note at the end) and need to be checked by a native speaker. The 0-5 taste scores are editorial estimates.*

## Before you start: shopping in Chiang Mai

- **Warorot / Kad Luang (กาดหลวง):** dried chillies, fresh curry pastes by weight (ask for *phrik nam ngiao*, *phrik khao soi*), thua nao (fermented soybean discs), dried kapok flowers (ดอกงิ้ว), nam oi cane sugar, miang, khao taen.
- **Ton Lamyai (ต้นลำไย), across the road, and any morning market (กาดเช้า):** fresh *khanom sen* rice noodles, herbs, banana leaves, freshly grated coconut and coconut milk.
- **Muang Mai (เมืองใหม่):** the wholesale market, busiest before dawn. Go here for pork bones, blood, meat and bulk vegetables.
- **A word to learn first:** in the North, *nam oi* (น้ำอ้อย) usually means **solid cane sugar**, not cane juice.

## Khao soi - Chiang Mai coconut curry noodles with chicken

**ข้าวซอยไก่** · Kham Mueang: **เข้าซอยไก่** · *khao soi kai*  
Say it: เข้า *khao* (44ʔ) · ซอย *soi* (33) · ไก่ *kai* (21)  
Tip: Lanna writing and speech use short 'เข้า' for rice (Matichon Weekly, 'เข้าใหม่' column), so locals often say a clipped 'khao', not a long Central 'khâao'. 'Soi' = 'cut' (strands cut from a sheet).  
From: เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, เชียงราย Chiang Rai, แม่ฮ่องสอน Mae Hong Son · Season: All year; locals say it hits hardest in the cool season (Nov-Feb) · Occasion: Everyday lunch; a Chiang Mai 'must eat' for visitors · Serves: 4-5 · Confidence: **high**

Noodles in a spiced coconut curry (turmeric, curry powder, coriander seed), topped with a nest of crisp fried noodles and eaten with shallots, pickled mustard greens, lime and chilli fried in oil. 'Soi' comes from Shan (Tai Yai) and means 'to cut' - strands cut from a sheet of dough. Thai sources describe khao soi as a dish of the Yunnanese Muslim Chin Haw and the Shan; early versions had no coconut milk. Around 1942 a Northern Thai worker called 'Nai Pan' in a Haw khao soi shop added coconut cream, and that became the Chiang Mai style (Bangkok Biz News). Halal beef and chicken khao soi is still sold around the Ban Haw mosque off Charoen Prathet Road.

| chilli fried in oil | พริกผัดน้ำมัน (*phrik phat nam man*) | to taste | Warorot | - |

**Equipment:** granite mortar and pestle (or Blender with a splash of oil (paste will be less fragrant)); heavy pot; wok for crisp noodles; wire strainer / spider

**Prep ahead:** Soak dried chillies 20 min. Pound the paste (20-30 min) or buy fresh khao soi paste at Warorot. Paste keeps 1 week chilled.

### Method

1. Pound chillies with a pinch of salt first, then galangal, lemongrass, turmeric, zest, then shallot and garlic, and shrimp paste last. *(25 min)* **Look for:** A smooth, brick-red paste smelling of garlic and galangal. **Tip:** Salt acts as an abrasive; wet ingredients go in last so the paste doesn't splash.
2. Boil 1 cup of the thickest coconut milk until it splits and oil shows. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Bubbles turn glossy; little pools of oil appear on the surface.
3. Fry the paste in the cracked coconut cream. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Aroma shifts from raw garlic to toasty; oil turns orange-red. **Tip:** Keep heat medium; scorched paste goes bitter.
4. Stir in curry powder slurried with a little water. *(2 min)* **Look for:** Colour deepens to ochre; smells of cumin and coriander.
5. Add the chicken, coat it well, add water to cover, palm sugar and the rest of the coconut milk. Simmer until tender. *(40 min)* **Look for:** Meat pulls from the bone; broth is orange with a red oil sheen. **Tip:** Season with fish sauce or salt at the end; Muslim shops use soy sauce.
6. Deep-fry small loose nests of noodles until golden. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Loud crackle, then quiet once crisp; golden, not brown.
7. Blanch fresh noodles about 1 minute, refresh in cold water, drain. *(3 min)* **Look for:** Noodles just soft, still springy.
8. Noodles in a bowl, ladle over curry and a chicken piece, top with crisp noodles, coriander and spring onion. *(2 min)* **Look for:** Crisp nest sits on top; curry pools around it.

### Taste

Sour 1 · Salty 3 · Spicy 2 · Sweet 2 · Bitter 0 · Umami 4 (0-5, estimate). Rich and fragrant, salty-led with a sweet finish; you add sour (lime, pickles), heat (chilli oil) and crunch (shallot) yourself. *Texture:* Silky soft noodles under crunchy fried ones; rich, slightly thick broth; tender chicken.

Words to use: ลำ *lam* (delicious), ลำขนาด *lam khanat* (super tasty), เก็ม *kem* (salty (Northern unaspirated k)), ส้ม *som* (sour)

### How to eat it

Squeeze lime, add pickled greens and shallot, spoon in chilli oil, stir. Eat with chopsticks and a spoon; drink the broth from the spoon, not by lifting the bowl. **With:** Side plate of shallot, pickled mustard greens, lime; chilli fried in oil; iced tea. **When:** Breakfast to late lunch; many famous shops sell out by 2-3 pm. **Etiquette:** Single-serve bowl - not shared. Condiments on the table are communal: take with the spoon provided.

**Safety:** Cooked dish - low risk. Keep coconut curry hot or chill it quickly; coconut milk sours fast in the heat.

**Common mistakes:**
- Blending the paste raw and skipping the frying step - tastes raw and flat.
- Adding all the coconut milk at once - fry the paste in cracked coconut cream first.
- Frying noodles too dark - they go bitter.
- Forgetting the condiments; the dish is designed to be finished at the table.

**Phrases:**
- เข้าซอยไก่ ถ้วยนึ่งเจ้า (*khao soi kai thuai nueng jao*) = One chicken khao soi, please (Central: ข้าวซอยไก่ ถ้วยหนึ่งค่ะ). Ordering
- เอา...เจ้า / เอา...ครับ (*ao ... jao / khrap*) = I'll take ... (Central: เอา...ค่ะ/ครับ). Ordering at a stall; เจ้า is the Northern polite particle (mostly used by women; men usually say ครับ).
- บ่เผ็ดเน้อ (*bo phet noe*) = Not spicy, OK? (Central: ไม่เผ็ดนะ). Before the vendor adds chilli.
- ลำขนาดเจ้า (*lam khanat jao*) = It's really delicious! (Central: อร่อยมากค่ะ). Complimenting the cook - always welcome.

**Culture:** Thai writers call khao soi a bowl of many cultures - Shan noodles, Indian/Burmese spices, Thai coconut, Yunnanese technique (Bangkok Biz News). Across the border in Kengtung the Tai Khuen khao soi is a clear broth with no coconut (Silpa-Mag). Muslim shops near Ban Haw mosque sell halal versions - no pork, and be respectful around prayer times.

## Rice noodles in Northern tomato-pork broth with blood and dried kapok flowers

**ขนมจีนน้ำเงี้ยว** · Kham Mueang: **ขนมเส้นน้ำเงี้ยว (ไทใหญ่: เข้าเส้นน้ำหมากเขือส้ม)** · *khanom jin nam ngiao (Northern: khanom sen nam ngiao)*  
Say it: ขะ *kha* (21) · หนม *nom* (24) · เส้น *sen* (44ʔ) · น้ำ *nam* (45) · เงี้ยว *ngiao* (45)  
Tip: Northerners usually say 'khanom sen' for fresh rice noodles rather than Central 'khanom jin'. 'Ngiao' starts with the 'ng' of 'singer'.  
From: เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, เชียงราย Chiang Rai, แพร่ Phrae, แม่ฮ่องสอน Mae Hong Son · Season: All year; dried kapok flowers (ดอกงิ้ว) are gathered in February and stored · Occasion: Everyday breakfast/lunch; cooked in big pots for merit-making and funerals · Serves: 8-10 · Confidence: **high**

A salty, spicy, tomato-sour broth of pork bones, minced pork, cubes of blood and dried kapok (red cotton tree) flowers, built on a paste of dried chilli, shallot, garlic, turmeric, salt, shrimp paste and fermented soybean (thua nao). Ladled over fresh rice noodles with bean sprouts, pickled greens, fried garlic and pork crackling. The name refers to the Shan (Tai Yai) people, who were historically called 'Ngiao' by Northern Thais; Shan speakers themselves call the broth 'nam mak khuea som' - tomato broth.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nam ngiao paste (dried chilli, shallot, garlic, turmeric, salt, shrimp paste, fermented soybean) | พริกแกงน้ำเงี้ยว (พริกแห้ง หอม กระเทียม ขมิ้น เกลือ กะปิ ถั่วเน่า) (*phrik kaeng nam ngiao*) | 250 g ready-made, or pound your own | Warorot. Market paste sold by weight from big trays; ask for 'phrik nam ngiao' | Pound: 15 dried chillies, 10 shallots, 1 head garlic, 1 thumb turmeric, 1 tsp salt, 1 tbsp shrimp paste, 2 discs thua nao |
| dried fermented soybean discs | ถั่วเน่าแข็บ / ถั่วเน่าแข็บ (*thua nao khaep*) | 2 discs, toasted and crumbled | Warorot. Thin, dry, brown discs; toast over a flame until fragrant | 1 tbsp Korean doenjang or Japanese miso (different but close in role) |
| pork spare ribs / bones | กระดูกซี่โครงหมู (*kraduk mu*) | 400 g | Muang Mai wholesale market | - |
| minced pork with some fat | หมูสับ (ติดมัน) / จิ๊นหมู (*mu sap*) | 400 g | Muang Mai wholesale market | - |
| set pork or chicken blood | เลือดหมูหรือเลือดไก่ (ก้อน) (*lueat*) | 1 block (about 300 g), cubed | Muang Mai wholesale market. Buy same-day, firm, dark maroon; keep cold | Leave out, or use firm tofu |
| small sour local tomatoes | มะเขือส้ม / มะเขือเทศลูกเล็ก / บ่ะเขือส้ม (*makhuea som*) | 300-500 g (or 2 large tomatoes) | Ton Lamyai market. Small, very red, sour cherry-type tomatoes | Cherry tomatoes + squeeze of lime |
| dried kapok (red cotton tree) flower stamens | ดอกงิ้วแห้ง / ดอกงิ้ว (*dok ngio*) | 1/2 cup, soaked | Warorot. Brown dried bundles of stamens (the pistil is removed) | Omit, or dried day-lily buds (golden needles) - Thai home cooks sometimes use these (Wongnai UGC) |
| coriander roots | รากผักชี (*rak phak chi*) | 5, bruised | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| fish sauce / salt | น้ำปลา / เกลือ (*nam pla*) | to taste (about 2 tbsp + 1 tsp) | Supermarket | - |
| fresh fermented rice noodles | ขนมเส้น (ขนมจีน) / ขนมเส้น (*khanom sen*) | 1.5-2 kg | Ton Lamyai market. Bought fresh each morning in bundles (หัว); should smell faintly sour-clean, not off | Dried thin rice vermicelli or Vietnamese bun |
| to serve: bean sprouts, pickled mustard, shredded cabbage, lime, coriander, spring onion, fried garlic, fried dried chilli, pork crackling | ถั่วงอก ผักกาดดอง ผักกาดขาวซอย มะนาว ผักชี ต้นหอม กระเทียมเจียว พริกทอด แคบหมู (*phak kiang*) | a platter | Ton Lamyai market | - |

**Equipment:** large stockpot; wok; mortar (or Blender)

**Prep ahead:** Soak dried kapok flowers 15-30 min and snip off hard bases. Toast thua nao discs over a flame. Make or buy paste.

### Method

1. Boil water with coriander roots and ribs, skim the scum, simmer gently. *(30 min)* **Look for:** Clear broth, no grey foam.
2. In a wok fry the paste in oil, add minced pork and crumbled thua nao, fry until fragrant and the pork is cooked. *(8 min)* **Look for:** Smells deeply savoury, slightly funky from the thua nao; oil turns red.
3. Add tomatoes and fry until they collapse. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Tomatoes slump and turn jammy.
4. Tip everything into the stock and simmer low. *(30 min)* **Look for:** Broth turns orange-red with a layer of red oil.
5. Add soaked kapok flowers and blood cubes; season with fish sauce and salt; simmer 10 more minutes. *(10 min)* **Look for:** Blood firms and turns brown right through; flowers soften.
6. Taste and adjust; ladle over noodles, top with fried garlic, coriander, spring onion. *(3 min)* **Look for:** Salty-spicy with a clean tomato sourness.

### Taste

Sour 3 · Salty 4 · Spicy 3 · Sweet 1 · Bitter 0 · Umami 5 (0-5, estimate). Salty and spicy with bright tomato sourness and a deep fermented-soy savour. *Texture:* Slippery soft noodles, silky blood cubes, chewy-soft kapok flowers, crunchy sprouts and crackling.

Words to use: เก็ม *kem* (salty (Northern unaspirated k)), เผ็ด *phet* (spicy-hot), ส้ม *som* (sour), ลำ *lam* (delicious)

### How to eat it

Pile on raw bean sprouts and pickled greens, squeeze lime, crumble in fried chilli and pork crackling, then toss noodles in the broth. **With:** Raw bean sprouts, pickled mustard, shredded cabbage, lime, fried dried chilli, pork crackling (แคบหมู) or crisp buffalo skin (หนังปอง). **When:** Morning to lunch; at temple fairs and funerals from big pots. **Etiquette:** Individual bowls; at merit-making the host serves you - accept, and say 'lam khanat'.

**Safety:** Blood must be cooked through (no pink/red centre) - buy it fresh the same day and keep it cold. Thua nao is safe when bought from market vendors; toast before use. Leftover broth: reboil before eating.

**Common mistakes:**
- Thinking 'dok ngio' means dill or lily - it is the dried stamen of the red cotton (kapok) tree flower.
- Skipping the thua nao - it gives the Northern depth; shrimp paste alone tastes more Central.
- Using the exonym 'Ngiao' for Shan people in conversation - fine in the dish name, but say 'Tai Yai' (ไทใหญ่) for the people.

**Phrases:**
- ขนมเส้นน้ำเงี้ยว ใส่เลือดเน้อ / บ่ใส่เลือดเน้อ (*khanom sen nam ngiao sai lueat noe / bo sai lueat noe*) = Nam ngiao, with blood / without blood please (Central: ขนมจีนน้ำเงี้ยว ใส่เลือดนะ / ไม่ใส่เลือดนะ). Ordering
- เอา...เจ้า / เอา...ครับ (*ao ... jao / khrap*) = I'll take ... (Central: เอา...ค่ะ/ครับ). Ordering at a stall; เจ้า is the Northern polite particle (mostly used by women; men usually say ครับ).
- บ่เผ็ดเน้อ (*bo phet noe*) = Not spicy, OK? (Central: ไม่เผ็ดนะ). Before the vendor adds chilli.
- ลำขนาดเจ้า (*lam khanat jao*) = It's really delicious! (Central: อร่อยมากค่ะ). Complimenting the cook - always welcome.

**Culture:** 'Ngiao' is the old Thai exonym for the Shan (Tai Yai) people and is considered impolite when used for people today; say 'Tai Yai' or 'Tai' (their own name). The dish name 'nam ngiao' is used normally. Kapok flowers bloom in February; elders gather fallen flowers, remove the pistil (called 'khi ta') and pods, and sun-dry the brown stamens for nam ngiao, kaeng khae and kaeng bon. In Nan, the blossoms are said to signal that children working away will soon come home for Songkran.

**To verify:** Shan name เข้าเส้นน้ำหมากเขือส้ม is from one commercial site

## Khao soi noi - Shan steamed rice-flour sheets ('Tai Yai pizza')

**ข้าวซอยน้อย** · Kham Mueang: **เข้าซอยน้อย (ข้าวซอยน้อยลอยน้ำ)** · *khao soi noi*  
Say it: เข้า *khao* (44ʔ) · ซอย *soi* (33) · น้อย *noi* (45)  
Tip: 'Noi' = small.  
From: เชียงราย (แม่สาย) Chiang Rai - Mae Sai, แม่ฮ่องสอน Mae Hong Son · Season: All year · Occasion: Street snack or light meal · Serves: 3-4 (about 10 sheets) · Confidence: **medium**

A Shan (Tai Yai) dish: a thin rice-flour batter poured into a round tray, which is floated on boiling water (or steamed) until the sheet turns translucent. Plain sheets are rolled and dipped; the 'song khrueang' (loaded) version adds pickled chilli, chilli roasted in oil, fried garlic, crushed peanuts, sesame, soy sauce, minced pork, vegetables and egg, then cooks again. Nicknamed 'Tai Yai pizza'.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rice flour | แป้งข้าวเจ้า (*paeng khao jao*) | 500 g | Supermarket | Any non-glutinous rice flour (not glutinous) |
| water, room temperature | น้ำเปล่า (*nam*) | 1,400-1,500 ml | Ton Lamyai market. Add gradually - too much water = soft sheet that sticks; too little = hard | - |
| salt, a little oil | เกลือ น้ำมัน (*kluea, nam man*) | 1 tsp salt, 1 tbsp oil | Supermarket | - |
| pork lard (vendors use back fat) | น้ำมันหมู (*nam man mu*) | for greasing trays | Muang Mai wholesale market | Neutral oil |
| coriander | ผักชี (*phak chi*) | 1 bunch, chopped | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| minced pork, stir-fried | หมูสับผัด (*mu sap*) | 200 g | Muang Mai wholesale market | - |
| eggs | ไข่ไก่ (*khai*) | 3-4 | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| shredded cabbage or chopped long beans | ถั่วฝักยาว/กะหล่ำปลีซอย (*kalam pli*) | 1 cup | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| pickled chilli, chilli roasted in oil, fried garlic, crushed peanuts, crushed sesame, soy sauce | พริกส้ม พริกคั่วน้ำมัน กระเทียมเจียว ถั่วลิสงตำ งาตำ ซีอิ๊ว (*phrik som, phrik khua nam man*) | to taste | Warorot | - |

**Equipment:** shallow round aluminium trays (15-20 cm) (or Pie tins or a flat plate in a steamer); wide pan of boiling water (or Steamer); spatula to peel sheet

**Prep ahead:** Mix batter; rest 30 min. Prepare toppings.

### Method

1. Whisk flour with water added gradually until smooth; add salt and a little oil. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Thin cream consistency, no lumps.
2. Grease a tray with lard; pour in a thin layer of batter. *(1 min)* **Look for:** Batter just covers the base. **Tip:** Too little batter = sheet tears; too much = thick and undercooked.
3. Float the tray on boiling water 1-2 minutes until set. *(2 min)* **Look for:** White turns translucent and glossy, edges lift.
4. (Loaded) add seasonings, vegetables, pork and egg into the tray with the batter, float again about 2 minutes. *(3 min)* **Look for:** Egg set, toppings glued into the sheet.
5. Peel out, roll or fold, eat hot. *(1 min)* **Look for:** Soft, slightly elastic sheet.

### Taste

Sour 2 · Salty 3 · Spicy 2 · Sweet 1 · Bitter 0 · Umami 3 (0-5, estimate). Savoury-soy, tangy-hot from pickled chilli, nutty from peanut and sesame. *Texture:* Soft, slippery, slightly chewy rice sheet with nutty crunch from peanuts and sesame.

Words to use: ลำ *lam* (delicious), ส้ม *som* (sour), เผ็ด *phet* (spicy-hot)

### How to eat it

Eat immediately, hot from the tray, dipping in the sauce or pickled chilli. **With:** Pickled chilli, chilli oil; Shan-style dipping sauce with fermented soybean. **When:** Afternoon snack / breakfast at Mae Sai markets. **Etiquette:** Street food - one plate each.

**Safety:** Hot tray and steam - use tongs. Egg topping should be fully set.

**Common mistakes:**
- Using glutinous rice flour - it won't set into a clean sheet.
- Pouring batter too thick.
- Confusing it with Chiang Mai khao soi noodle curry - unrelated dish.

**Phrases:**
- เอา...เจ้า / เอา...ครับ (*ao ... jao / khrap*) = I'll take ... (Central: เอา...ค่ะ/ครับ). Ordering at a stall; เจ้า is the Northern polite particle (mostly used by women; men usually say ครับ).
- บ่เผ็ดเน้อ (*bo phet noe*) = Not spicy, OK? (Central: ไม่เผ็ดนะ). Before the vendor adds chilli.
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.

**Culture:** Krua.co describes it as a Tai Yai dish; the best-known stalls are in Mae Sai on the Myanmar border. Sources name the Shan as 'Tai Yai'; use that name.

## Khao phan phak - steamed rice-sheet rolls with egg and vegetables

**ข้าวพันผัก** · Kham Mueang: **เข้าพันผัก / ข้าวปัน (แพร่)** · *khao phan phak*  
Say it: เข้า *khao* (44ʔ) · พัน *phan* (33) · ผัก *phak* (21)  
Tip: 'Phan' = to wrap/roll. In Phrae people also say 'khao pan'.  
From: อุตรดิตถ์ (ลับแล) Uttaradit - Laplae, แพร่ Phrae (e.g. near Phra That Cho Hae) · Season: All year · Occasion: Breakfast / snack; also cooked at temple fairs · Serves: about 12 rolls · Confidence: **medium**

A rice-flour batter spread on cloth stretched over a steaming pot; egg and a mix of vegetables (cabbage, morning glory, kale, bean sprouts, coriander, carrot, glass noodles) go on while it cooks, then it is folded into a rectangle with a bamboo paddle and topped with fried garlic and pork cracklings, with a sweet-sour-spicy dipping sauce. Like Central Thai 'khao kriap pak mo' but larger and thicker. Best known from Laplae district, Uttaradit (a Lower-Northern province bordering Phrae); also sold in Phrae.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rice flour | แป้งข้าวเจ้า (*paeng khao jao*) | 300 g | Supermarket | - |
| water | น้ำ (*nam*) | about 600-700 ml | Ton Lamyai market. Batter should be like thin pouring cream | - |
| eggs | ไข่ไก่ (*khai*) | 6 | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| cabbage, morning glory, kale, bean sprouts, carrot, coriander (shredded) | กะหล่ำปลี ผักบุ้ง คะน้า ถั่วงอก แครอท ผักชี (*phak*) | 500 g mixed | Muang Mai wholesale market | - |
| glass noodles, soaked | วุ้นเส้น (*wun sen*) | 50 g | Supermarket | - |
| fried garlic and pork cracklings, with salt and pepper | กระเทียมเจียว กากหมู (*krathiam jiao, kak mu*) | 1/2 cup | Warorot | - |
| sauce: chicken stock, chilli sauce, seasoning sauce, vinegar, coconut sugar, toasted sesame | น้ำซุปไก่ ซอสพริก ซอสปรุงรส น้ำส้มสายชู น้ำตาลมะพร้าว งาคั่ว (*nam jim*) | about 1 cup | Supermarket | - |

**Equipment:** large pot or steamer; muslin cloth and string, tied taut over the pot mouth (some cloths have a 1-2 inch steam hole); flat bamboo paddle (or Thin spatula); domed lid

**Prep ahead:** Tie the cloth drum-tight over the pot and bring water to a rolling boil. Shred vegetables. Make the sauce.

### Method

1. Mix flour and water into a thin batter; rest. *(15 min)* **Look for:** Smooth, runny.
2. Ladle batter onto the cloth and spread in a thin circle; cover. *(1 min)* **Look for:** Batter clouds white within seconds.
3. Crack an egg onto the sheet, spread it, add vegetables; cover and steam about 30 s-1 min. *(1 min)* **Look for:** Egg sets, vegetables wilt slightly, sheet goes translucent.
4. Fold the sheet over the filling into a rectangle with the bamboo paddle and lift off. *(1 min)* **Look for:** Sheet releases cleanly from the cloth. **Tip:** Wet the paddle; brush the cloth with a little oil if sticking.
5. Top with garlic-crackling mix and spoon over sauce. *(1 min)* **Look for:** Glossy, garlicky.

### Taste

Sour 2 · Salty 2 · Spicy 1 · Sweet 2 · Bitter 0 · Umami 3 (0-5, estimate). Mild and soft; the sauce brings sweet-sour-light heat. *Texture:* Soft chewy sheet, tender-crisp vegetables, crunchy garlic and pork crackling.

Words to use: ลำ *lam* (delicious), หวาน *wan* (sweet), ส้ม *som* (sour)

### How to eat it

Eat hot with spoon and fork; sauce poured over or on the side. **With:** Dipping sauce; chilli flakes. **When:** Breakfast, afternoon snack. **Etiquette:** Usually ordered per plate; fine to share at the table.

**Safety:** Steam burns - keep face away when lifting the lid. Eggs should be fully set.

**Common mistakes:**
- Cloth not taut - batter pools and tears.
- Too much filling - the sheet can't fold.

**Phrases:**
- เอา...เจ้า / เอา...ครับ (*ao ... jao / khrap*) = I'll take ... (Central: เอา...ค่ะ/ครับ). Ordering at a stall; เจ้า is the Northern polite particle (mostly used by women; men usually say ครับ).
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.
- ลำขนาดเจ้า (*lam khanat jao*) = It's really delicious! (Central: อร่อยมากค่ะ). Complimenting the cook - always welcome.

**Culture:** Muang Boran Journal describes a family of Laplae steamed-sheet foods: khao khaep (sheets sun-dried on bamboo racks), khao phan phak, and mi phan (khao khaep wrapped around seasoned rice vermicelli), made by families over generations.

**To verify:** Phrae name 'ข้าวปัน' seen only in a Pantip thread title (page blocked); Batter quantity is an estimate; the A Cuisine recipe gives components but quantities were not extracted

Sources: <https://cheewajit.com/healthy-food/recipe/208958.html> · <https://www.muangboranjournal.com/post/khao-khab-Laplae> · <https://www.wongnai.com/restaurants/1216998UQ-%E0%B8%82%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%9E%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%9C%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%81-%E0%B8%8A%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%AE>

## Phrae rice noodles tossed with dry chilli-crackling relish, with clear pork broth

**ขนมจีนน้ำย้อย (ขนมจีนน้ำหมู/น้ำใส + น้ำพริกน้ำย้อย)** · Kham Mueang: **ขนมเส้นน้ำย้อย** · *khanom jin nam yoi (Phrae: khanom sen nam yoi)*  
Say it: ขะ *kha* (21) · หนม *nom* (24) · เส้น *sen* (44ʔ) · น้ำ *nam* (45) · ย้อย *yoi* (45)  
Tip: 'Yoi' = to drip. Phrae people call their town 'เมืองแป้' (Mueang Pae).  
From: แพร่ (อำเภอลอง) Phrae - Long district · Season: All year · Occasion: Breakfast/lunch; Phrae specialty · Serves: 4 · Confidence: **medium**

'Nam phrik nam yoi' is a dry relish from Long district, Phrae: chilli powder, fried garlic, fried shallot and pork cracklings. The name is said to come from the water still dripping ('yoi') off freshly blanched noodles. Phrae people toss the noodles with the relish, and drink a clear pork-bone broth ('nam mu' / 'nam sai') with small tomatoes and blood, seasoned with fish sauce - Krua.co notes Phrae's fish-sauce habit differs from the thua-nao style of the upper North.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pork back fat / fatty pork, cubed (for cracklings) | หมูติดมัน/มันแข็งหั่นชิ้น (ทำกากหมู) / ขี้แคบ / กากหมู (*kak mu*) | 1 kg (yields ~300 g crackling) | Muang Mai wholesale market | Ready-made pork scratchings, chopped |
| shallots, thinly sliced | หอมแดงซอย (*hom daeng*) | 200 g | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| Thai garlic, pounded | กระเทียมไทยทุบ (*krathiam*) | 100 g | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| chilli powder (roasted) | พริกป่น (*phrik pon*) | 2 1/2 tbsp | Warorot | - |
| sugar, salt | น้ำตาล เกลือ (*namtan, kluea*) | 2 tbsp sugar, 1/4 tsp salt | Supermarket | - |
| pork ribs and leg bones | กระดูกซี่โครง/กระดูกขาหมู (*kraduk mu*) | 800 g | Muang Mai wholesale market | - |
| galangal, lemongrass, coriander root, shallot | ข่า ตะไคร้ รากผักชี หอมแดง (*kha, takhrai*) | a handful | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| small sour tomatoes | มะเขือส้ม / บ่ะเขือส้ม (*makhuea som*) | 10-12 | Ton Lamyai market | Cherry tomatoes |
| set pork blood | เลือดหมู (*lueat mu*) | 200 g | Muang Mai wholesale market | - |
| fish sauce | น้ำปลา (*nam pla*) | to taste | Supermarket | - |
| fresh rice noodles | ขนมเส้น / ขนมเส้น (*khanom sen*) | 800 g | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| fresh and blanched vegetables, lime, pickles, spring onion, coriander, raw garlic | ผักสด ผักลวก มะนาว ผักดอง ต้นหอม ผักชี กระเทียม (*phak kiang*) | platter | Ton Lamyai market | - |

**Equipment:** wok; stockpot; paper towels

**Prep ahead:** Render cracklings (can be made days ahead; keep airtight).

### Method

1. Cut pork fat into cubes, cook in a wok with a splash of water until the fat renders and pieces puff and crisp. *(30 min)* **Look for:** Sizzle turns from wet bubbling to a dry crackle; pieces golden and airy. **Tip:** Keep lard for frying garlic and shallot.
2. Fry garlic in the lard until golden; drain. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Light gold and nutty - pull before it browns.
3. Fry shallots until golden-crisp; drain. *(8 min)* **Look for:** Stops bubbling hard when moisture is gone.
4. Toss cracklings (chopped), garlic and shallots with chilli powder, sugar and salt. *(3 min)* **Look for:** Loose, crunchy, red-flecked relish.
5. Broth: fry aromatics golden, add bones and stir, add water, boil, skim, salt, simmer 30 min. *(40 min)* **Look for:** Clear, golden broth.
6. Season with fish sauce; add tomatoes and blood cubes and simmer until blood is cooked. *(10 min)* **Look for:** Tomatoes just split; blood firm and brown throughout.
7. Put noodles on a plate still dripping, spoon relish over and toss; serve broth in a side bowl. *(2 min)* **Look for:** Relish clings to damp noodles.

### Taste

Sour 1 · Salty 3 · Spicy 3 · Sweet 2 · Bitter 0 · Umami 3 (0-5, estimate). Hot, salty, a little sweet, rich with fried garlic and shallot; the broth cleans the palate. *Texture:* Crunchy, fatty cracklings against soft noodles; light clear broth.

Words to use: เผ็ด *phet* (spicy-hot), เก็ม *kem* (salty (Northern unaspirated k)), ลำ *lam* (delicious)

### How to eat it

Toss noodles with the relish, squeeze lime, eat with fresh veg and a bite of raw garlic, sip broth between mouthfuls. **With:** Clear pork broth, fresh herbs, blanched veg, lime, pickles. **When:** Morning to lunch. **Etiquette:** Veg platter is shared; noodle plates individual.

**Safety:** Blood must be fully cooked. Hot lard spatters - dry pork before frying.

**Common mistakes:**
- Pouring broth onto the noodles - in Phrae the relish-tossed noodles and broth are eaten side by side.
- Burning the garlic - it turns bitter within seconds.

**Phrases:**
- เอา...เจ้า / เอา...ครับ (*ao ... jao / khrap*) = I'll take ... (Central: เอา...ค่ะ/ครับ). Ordering at a stall; เจ้า is the Northern polite particle (mostly used by women; men usually say ครับ).
- บ่เผ็ดเน้อ (*bo phet noe*) = Not spicy, OK? (Central: ไม่เผ็ดนะ). Before the vendor adds chilli.
- ลำขนาดเจ้า (*lam khanat jao*) = It's really delicious! (Central: อร่อยมากค่ะ). Complimenting the cook - always welcome.

**Culture:** Krua.co suggests Phrae's fish-sauce preference may reflect neighbours (Uttaradit, Sukhothai, Phitsanulok) or Lao settlers, whereas the upper North favours fermented soybean.

## Khao raem fuen - set rice/pea-flour jelly with savoury toppings

**ข้าวแรมฟืน** · Kham Mueang: **เข้าแรมฟืน / ข้าวฟืน (ไทใหญ่: ถั่วพูอุ่น/โถ่พูอุ่น สำหรับแบบอุ่น)** · *khao raem fuen*  
Say it: เข้า *khao* (44ʔ) · แรม *raem* (33) · ฟืน *fuen* (33)  
Tip: Northern speakers often say 'haem' for แรม (Northern ร often -> ฮ) - unverified for this word.  
From: เชียงราย (แม่สาย) Chiang Rai - Mae Sai, แม่ฮ่องสอน Mae Hong Son, ตาก Tak · Season: All year; the warm version is loved in the cool season · Occasion: Market snack / light meal · Serves: 6 · Confidence: **medium**

A food of the Tai Lue, Tai Khuen, Tai Yai and Yunnanese Chinese: rice or pulses (yellow pea, peanut, soy) are ground, strained and stirred with limewater over a wood fire until thick, then left overnight in trays to set. The name may come from 'khao raem khuen' ('overnight rice') or from Chinese liang fen (凉粉). Served as 'khao fuen nam' (cubes in broth), 'khao fuen yam' (tossed salad), fried, or warm ('thua phu un') - a hot, yellow, porridge-like version.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| yellow pea flour (or rice flour) | แป้งถั่วลันเตา (หรือแป้งข้าวเจ้า) (*paeng thua lanta*) | 200 g | Warorot. Pale yellow, fine | Chickpea (gram) flour gives a similar yellow jelly - not identical |
| food-grade limewater (clear liquid off slaked lime paste) | น้ำปูนใส / น้ำปูน (*nam pun sai*) | about 1 litre (with water) | Warorot. Use the clear top layer only, from edible red/white lime sold for betel or desserts | Water plus a pinch of salt; texture softer |
| roasted peanuts, crushed | ถั่วลิสงคั่วป่น (*thua lisong*) | 1/2 cup | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| chilli roasted in oil | พริกคั่วน้ำมัน (*phrik khua nam man*) | to taste | Warorot | - |
| fermented soybean (paste or disc, toasted) | ถั่วเน่า (*thua nao*) | 2 tbsp | Warorot | - |
| sweet-sour tamarind or tomato broth | น้ำมะขาม/น้ำซุปเปรี้ยวหวาน หรือน้ำมะเขือส้ม (*nam makham*) | 500 ml | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| shredded ginger, garlic, soy sauce, coriander, spring onion, bean sprouts, pickled greens | ขิงซอย กระเทียม ซีอิ๊ว ผักชี ต้นหอม ถั่วงอก ผักกาดดอง (*khing, phak chi*) | to serve | Ton Lamyai market | - |

**Equipment:** heavy pot; wooden paddle for constant stirring; tray or baking dish to set

**Prep ahead:** Must set overnight (or at least 4-6 h). Make the tamarind/tomato broth in advance.

### Method

1. Whisk the flour into limewater and water; strain. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Thin, lump-free liquid.
2. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until thick, glossy and cooked. *(20 min)* **Look for:** Goes from milky to translucent-glossy; paddle leaves a trail. **Tip:** Stop stirring and it scorches on the base.
3. Pour into a tray; leave overnight to set. *(480 min)* **Look for:** Firm, sliceable jelly.
4. Cut into strips or cubes; add broth and toppings. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Wobbly cubes glossed with red chilli oil.
5. (Warm version) serve the freshly cooked hot mixture straight into bowls with peanuts, chilli oil, soy and coriander. *(2 min)* **Look for:** Pourable, steaming yellow porridge.

### Taste

Sour 3 · Salty 2 · Spicy 3 · Sweet 2 · Bitter 0 · Umami 3 (0-5, estimate). Sour, spicy and sweet with toasted peanut and chilli oil. *Texture:* Cool, soft, wobbly jelly; nutty peanuts; slippery broth.

Words to use: ส้ม *som* (sour), เผ็ด *phet* (spicy-hot), หวาน *wan* (sweet), ลำ *lam* (delicious)

### How to eat it

Stir everything together and eat with a spoon. **With:** Bean sprouts, pickled greens, peanuts, chilli oil, fermented soybean. **When:** Morning or afternoon snack at Mae Sai / Mae Hong Son markets. **Etiquette:** Individual bowls.

**Safety:** Use only food-grade edible lime; use the clear water, never the paste. Keep set jelly refrigerated if not eaten the same day.

**Common mistakes:**
- Not stirring constantly - lumps and burning.
- Eating it expecting a sweet dessert - it's savoury-tangy.
- Using construction lime - never.

**Phrases:**
- เอา...เจ้า / เอา...ครับ (*ao ... jao / khrap*) = I'll take ... (Central: เอา...ค่ะ/ครับ). Ordering at a stall; เจ้า is the Northern polite particle (mostly used by women; men usually say ครับ).
- บ่เผ็ดเน้อ (*bo phet noe*) = Not spicy, OK? (Central: ไม่เผ็ดนะ). Before the vendor adds chilli.
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.

**Culture:** Matichon Weekly lists alternative names (khao raem khuen, khao fuen tho, tho fu) and traces the dish to Sipsong Panna (Xishuangbanna) before it became a Mae Sai signature. Krua.co notes a Mae Hong Son friend calls the warm version 'tho phu un' in Tai Yai style ('tho phu' relates to 'tofu', not winged beans).

**To verify:** Flour:liquid quantities are a working ratio (sources give method, not grams); Pronunciation note about ร->ฮ is unverified for this word

## Khanom pat - Lanna palm-sugar, coconut and rice-flour slab

**ขนมปาด** · Kham Mueang: **ข้าวหนมปาด / ขนมปาด** · *khanom pat*  
Say it: ขะ *kha* (21) · หนม *nom* (24) · ปาด *pat* (21)  
Tip: 'Pat' = to spread/smooth flat (the mixture is spread into a slab). Older Northerners say 'khao nom' (ข้าวหนม) for sweets.  
From: ลำปาง Lampang, พะเยา Phayao, เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, ทั่วล้านนา across Lanna · Season: Festivals; Songkran, ordinations, housewarmings · Occasion: Auspicious merit-making (งานบวช ขึ้นบ้านใหม่); a higher-status offering because it is laborious · Serves: a 20 x 20 cm tray (about 25 pieces) · Confidence: **medium**

A stirred Lanna sweet: glutinous rice flour with a little rice flour is cooked with coconut milk, palm or cane sugar and toasted grated coconut until thick and sticky, spread ('pat') into a thin slab, sprinkled with white sesame, cooled and cut. Made for auspicious events such as ordinations and housewarmings; it takes a lot of stirring, so relatives make it together.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| grated coconut, sun-dried a day then toasted | มะพร้าวขูด (ตากแดด 1 วัน แล้วคั่ว) (*maphrao khut*) | 700 g | Ton Lamyai market. Freshly grated mature coconut from the market coconut stall | Unsweetened desiccated coconut, toasted |
| palm sugar or Northern cane sugar blocks (nam oi) | น้ำตาลปี๊บ / น้ำอ้อย / น้ำอ้อย (*namtan pip / nam oi*) | 800-900 g | Warorot | Jaggery or panela |
| coconut cream | หัวกะทิ (*hua kathi*) | 100 ml + extra to stop sticking | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| glutinous rice flour | แป้งข้าวเหนียว (*paeng khao niao*) | 550 g | Supermarket | - |
| rice flour | แป้งข้าวเจ้า (*paeng khao jao*) | 50 g | Supermarket | - |
| white sesame | งาขาว (*nga khao*) | 2 tbsp, toasted | Warorot | - |

**Equipment:** brass pan or large heavy wok; long wooden paddle; tray lined with banana leaf (or Baking paper)

**Prep ahead:** Toast coconut. Steam the flours until cooked (Lampang method) or mix to a batter.

### Method

1. Steam the two flours (spread thin on a cloth) until cooked. *(20 min)* **Look for:** Flour clumps and turns slightly translucent.
2. Reduce coconut cream until oily, add palm sugar and toasted coconut. *(15 min)* **Look for:** Dark caramel colour, smells of toffee and coconut.
3. Add the steamed flour and stir continuously, adding splashes of coconut cream so it doesn't stick. *(40 min)* **Look for:** Mass becomes glossy, pulls away from the pan in one lump. **Tip:** This is a two-person job - swap stirrers.
4. Spread into the tray in a thin even layer, scatter sesame, cool completely. *(60 min)* **Look for:** Firm but tender slab.
5. Cut into squares or diamonds. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Clean cuts with an oiled knife.

### Taste

Sour 0 · Salty 1 · Spicy 0 · Sweet 4 · Bitter 0 · Umami 0 (0-5, estimate). Sweet and rich, toasty coconut and caramel. *Texture:* Dense, soft-chewy, with toasted coconut bits.

Words to use: หวาน *wan* (sweet), ลำ *lam* (delicious), ลำขนาด *lam khanat* (super tasty)

### How to eat it

Eat as a snack with tea; offered to monks at merit-making. **With:** Hot tea or plain water. **When:** Festivals, ceremonies. **Etiquette:** Offered on trays; take one piece at a time. Offerings to monks come before guests eat.

**Safety:** Molten sugar mixture burns badly - long sleeves, long paddle. Keeps about 2 weeks unrefrigerated per the community recipe (glutinous flour helps).

**Common mistakes:**
- Stopping stirring - scorches in seconds.
- Cutting while warm - it smears.

**Phrases:**
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.
- ลำขนาดเจ้า (*lam khanat jao*) = It's really delicious! (Central: อร่อยมากค่ะ). Complimenting the cook - always welcome.

**Culture:** A community learning-centre recipe (NFE Wang Phang subdistrict) sun-dries and toasts the coconut; glutinous flour lets it keep ~2 weeks. Chiang Mai News writes that the real taste of Lanna sweets is 'the cooperation of relatives' who make them together.

**To verify:** Quantities scaled 1/10 from a commercial Lampang community recipe; Province of the Wang Phang recipe: page summary said Lampang, but Wang Phang subdistrict is in Lamphun (Wiang Nong Long) - verify

## Khanom jok - pyramid banana-leaf parcels of sticky dough with coconut filling

**ขนมเทียน** · Kham Mueang: **ขนมจ๊อก / ข้าวหนมจ็อก (บางถิ่นเรียกขนมนมสาว)** · *khanom jok*  
Say it: ขะ *kha* (21) · หนม *nom* (24) · จ๊อก *jok* (45)  
Tip: Written both จ๊อก and จ็อก; 'jok' refers to a pointed, gathered shape.  
From: ทั่วล้านนา across Lanna · Season: Songkran (Pi Mai Mueang, mid-April) and merit-making · Occasion: Songkran offerings, temple merit-making · Serves: about 25 parcels · Confidence: **medium**

Known in Central Thai as khanom thian; in the North it is khanom jok. Glutinous rice flour kneaded with water (sometimes with sugar), wrapped around a filling of grated coconut cooked with cane sugar (sometimes peanut or sesame), folded into a pointed banana-leaf pyramid and steamed. Traditional for Songkran and merit-making.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| glutinous rice flour | แป้งข้าวเหนียว (*paeng khao niao*) | 500 g | Supermarket | - |
| coconut or cane sugar (dough) | น้ำตาลมะพร้าว / น้ำอ้อย / น้ำอ้อย (*namtan*) | optional, up to 350 g for a sweet dough | Warorot | - |
| water | น้ำ (*nam*) | 300-400 ml | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| grated coconut | มะพร้าวขูด (*maphrao khut*) | 300 g | Ton Lamyai market | - |

## Khao taen - Lampang crispy sticky-rice crackers with watermelon juice and cane-sugar drizzle

**ข้าวแต๋น (ข้าวแต๋นน้ำแตงโม)** · Kham Mueang: **เข้าแต๋น** · *khao taen*  
Say it: เข้า *khao* (44ʔ) · แต๋น *taen* (24)  
Tip: The ๋ on แต๋น signals the Northern rising tone.  
From: ลำปาง Lampang (GI registered 17 June 2013) · Season: Hot, dry season is best for sun-drying; sold all year · Occasion: Once for housewarmings, weddings, Songkran; now a souvenir · Serves: about 30 crackers · Confidence: **high**

Steamed sticky rice mixed with watermelon juice and black sesame, pressed into discs, sun-dried two days, deep-fried until puffed and crisp, then drizzled with boiled cane sugar (nam oi). Lampang's version is a registered Geographical Indication; the province's hot, dry climate helps drying and even puffing.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |

| black sesame | งาดำ (*nga dam*) | 2 tbsp | Warorot | - |
| salt | เกลือ (*kluea*) | 1 tsp | Supermarket | - |
| Northern cane sugar blocks | น้ำอ้อย (น้ำตาลอ้อยก้อน) / น้ำอ้อย (*nam oi*) | 300 g + 100 ml water | Warorot | Jaggery or light muscovado |

**Prep ahead:** Soak rice overnight. Need two hot sunny days for drying.

### Method

### How to eat it

Snack with tea; classic Lampang souvenir. **With:** Tea. **When:** Any time. **Etiquette:** Bring a bag as a gift (ของฝาก) when visiting.

**Safety:** Deep-frying hazard. Store airtight; they go stale in humid air.

**Common mistakes:**
- Frying before fully dry.
- Oil not hot enough - greasy, flat crackers.

**Phrases:**
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.

**Culture:** GI registered 17 June 2013; originally a ceremonial sweet for housewarmings, weddings and Songkran.

## Khao lam - sticky rice with coconut and black beans roasted in bamboo

**ข้าวหลาม** · Kham Mueang: **เข้าหลาม** · *khao lam*  
Say it: เข้า *khao* (44ʔ) · หลาม *lam* (24)  
Tip: Not the same word as 'lam' (ลำ, delicious).  
From: ทั่วภาคเหนือ across the North (and all Thailand) · Season: Cool season; ritually on the full moon of the 4th Northern month (เดือนสี่เป็ง, around January) · Occasion: ตานข้าวใหม่ / ตานข้าวจี่ข้าวหลาม - new-rice merit-making · Serves: 6-8 tubes · Confidence: **medium**

Sticky rice with coconut milk, sugar, salt (and black beans) packed into bamboo tubes ('phai khao lam' / 'mai pang' bamboo, 1-2 years old, with a thin inner membrane), plugged with banana leaf or coconut husk and roasted over a low fire about an hour, turning constantly. In Lanna, villagers make khao lam and khao ji (grilled sticky rice) from the new harvest and offer them to monks on the full moon of the 4th Northern month (around January) - 'tan khao mai' - and build a pile of firewood ('lua') to light as a fire offering to the Buddha ('hing fai phra jao').

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| glutinous rice (new-season rice is prized) | ข้าวเหนียว (ข้าวใหม่ยิ่งดี) / เข้าเหนียว / เข้าใหม่ (*khao niao*) | 1 kg | Ton Lamyai market. New-crop rice: white, translucent, germ attached, fragrant (Matichon) | - |
| coconut milk | กะทิ (*kathi*) | 400 ml | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| sugar | น้ำตาลทราย (*namtan*) | 50 g | Supermarket | - |
| salt | เกลือ (*kluea*) | 1 1/2 tsp (should taste slightly salty) | Supermarket | - |
| black beans, cooked | ถั่วดำต้มสุก (*thua dam*) | 50 g | Warorot | - |
| khao-lam bamboo tubes, one end closed at a node | กระบอกไผ่ข้าวหลาม (*krabok phai*) | 6-8 tubes ~30 cm | Ton Lamyai market. Green, 1-2 years old, thin inner skin | Oven: foil-wrapped ramekins at 180 C - no bamboo aroma |
| banana leaf or coconut husk for plugs | ใบตอง / กาบมะพร้าว (*bai tong*) | a few | Ton Lamyai market | - |

**Equipment:** charcoal or wood fire, low heat; machete to split the bamboo (or Heavy knife)

**Prep ahead:** Soak rice 3-4 h (aroimak) - GotoKnow soaks briefly then stands filled tubes overnight. Cook beans.

### Method

1. Mix rice with beans; season coconut milk with sugar and salt (slightly salty). *(10 min)* **Look for:** Coconut milk tastes sweet-salty.
2. Fill tubes loosely, pour coconut milk to cover, leave a quarter empty. *(10 min)*
3. Plug with rolled banana leaf. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Snug plug.
4. Roast leaning over low embers about 45-60 minutes, turning every 10-15 minutes. *(60 min)* **Look for:** Coconut milk bubbles at the mouth; outer bamboo chars evenly; sweet toasty smell. **Tip:** If it boils over, remove plug and top up.
5. Test with a skewer (comes out clean); shave off the charred outer bamboo. *(10 min)* **Look for:** Rice set in a cylinder wrapped in thin white membrane.

### Taste

Sour 0 · Salty 2 · Spicy 0 · Sweet 3 · Bitter 0 · Umami 0 (0-5, estimate). Sweet-salty, creamy, smoky. *Texture:* Dense, chewy, creamy; smoky bamboo aroma.

Words to use: หวาน *wan* (sweet), ลำ *lam* (delicious), ลำขนาด *lam khanat* (super tasty)

### How to eat it

Split or peel the bamboo strip by strip and eat the rice cylinder in its thin membrane. **With:** Nothing - or hot tea. **When:** Cool-season mornings; tan khao mai day. **Etiquette:** At tan khao mai, the first portions go to the monks and to the dead (merit dedicated to ancestors) before people eat.

**Safety:** Bamboo can split and spit hot coconut milk - roast at an angle, keep face away; let cool before peeling.

**Common mistakes:**
- Packing rice too tight - uncooked core.
- High heat - burnt outside, raw inside.

**Phrases:**
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.
- ลำขนาดเจ้า (*lam khanat jao*) = It's really delicious! (Central: อร่อยมากค่ะ). Complimenting the cook - always welcome.

**Culture:** On the full moon of the 4th Northern month (= 2nd Central month; 13 Jan 2025 per the Lampang report), people offer new unhusked and milled rice, khao lam and khao ji to monks, dedicating merit to deceased relatives and deities, and light a stupa-shaped pile of firewood at dawn to 'warm the Buddha', read as burning away defilements. Northern months run about two ahead of Central Thai months.

## Miang - Lanna fermented tea leaves for chewing ('om miang')

**เมี่ยง (ชาหมัก)** · Kham Mueang: **เหมี้ยง** · *miang (Northern: miang, written hmiang)*  
Say it: เหมี้ยง *miang* (44ʔ)  
Tip: Northern spelling เหมี้ยง (Matichon Academy, Matichon Weekly). The ห is silent: it just marks the tone. 'Om miang' (อมเหมี้ยง) = to hold/suck miang in the cheek.  
From: เชียงใหม่ (แม่กำปอง ฯลฯ) Chiang Mai, ลำปาง Lampang, แพร่ Phrae, น่าน Nan, เชียงราย Chiang Rai - highland 'ป่าเมี่ยง' villages · Season: Picked 4 times a year: early (Apr-Jun, tough, astringent), mid (Jun-Aug), 'miang choi' (Aug-Oct, best), 'miang moei' (Nov-, small leaves) · Occasion: After meals; welcoming guests; merit-making, ordinations, funerals, housewarmings · Serves: a handful of quids · Confidence: **high**

For Lanna people 'miang' means tea - Assam tea (Camellia sinensis var. assamica, 'wild tea') - not the Central Thai leaf-wrap snack. Half-mature leaves are tied in bundles, steamed 30-60 minutes until yellow and soft, and fermented in pits or tanks for 15 days to 8 months. Short fermentation gives astringent 'miang fat' (preferred by elders), long gives sour 'miang som', or a mix 'miang som fat'. Traditional way: open 2-3 leaves, put coarse salt in the middle, roll, and chew slowly held in the cheek; some add pickled ginger. 'Miang wan' adds toasted coconut, sugar and roasted peanut. A 1491 CE (BE 2034) inscription at Wat Phra That Hariphunchai mentions money for 'mak miang' (betel and miang) offerings.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fermented tea leaves (sour or astringent) | เมี่ยงหมัก (เมี่ยงส้ม/เมี่ยงฝาด) / เหมี้ยง (*miang*) | 1 bundle (แหนบ) | Warorot. Moist, olive-brown, sour-clean smell; ask for 'miang som' if you're new | Burmese laphet (fermented tea) is the closest equivalent |
| coarse sea salt | เกลือเม็ด (*kluea met*) | a pinch per quid | Supermarket | - |
| pickled ginger | ขิงดอง (*khing dong*) | a sliver | Ton Lamyai market | - |
| toasted coconut, sugar, roasted peanuts (miang wan) | มะพร้าวคั่ว น้ำตาล ถั่วลิสงคั่ว (*maphrao khua*) | a spoonful | Ton Lamyai market | - |

**Equipment:** a small tray/bowl to offer miang to guests

**Prep ahead:** None if buying ready-fermented miang. Making it takes months.

### Method

1. Peel off and stack 2-3 fermented leaves. *(0.5 min)* **Look for:** Leaves damp, supple, sour-smelling.
2. Put a few salt crystals (and ginger) in the middle. *(0.5 min)*
3. Roll into a small quid. *(0.5 min)* **Look for:** Thumb-sized.
4. Tuck in the cheek and chew slowly, sucking the juice. *(10 min)* **Look for:** Sour, salty, astringent rush; mouth feels clean; mild caffeine buzz.

### Taste

Sour 4 · Salty 3 · Spicy 0 · Sweet 0 · Bitter 2 · Umami 1 (0-5, estimate). Sour, astringent and salty; refreshing and mildly stimulating. *Texture:* Leathery, fibrous leaf.

Words to use: ส้ม *som* (sour), ฝาด *fat* (astringent), เก็ม *kem* (salty (Northern unaspirated k))

### How to eat it

After a meal or while chatting; offered to visitors from the welcome tray. Elders traditionally paired miang with a hand-rolled Northern cigarette in the afternoon. **With:** Salt, pickled ginger; nothing else needed. **When:** After meals, afternoon, at ceremonies. **Etiquette:** Hosts pass the miang around; take one, roll your own. At weddings and funerals there are people whose job is serving miang.

**Safety:** Contains caffeine. Buy from established vendors; fermented product should smell sour-clean, not putrid.

**Common mistakes:**
- Swallowing it like a snack - it's chewed slowly (modern soft versions can be swallowed).
- Confusing Northern miang with Central 'miang kham' (betel-leaf wraps).
- Drinking it as tea - fermented miang is chewed, not brewed.

**Phrases:**
- อมเหมี้ยงก่อ (*om miang ko*) = Would you like some miang? (Central: อมเมี่ยงไหม). Host offering to a guest
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.

**Culture:** Krua.co: a Lanna welcome tray for visitors includes fermented miang. Tea-growing villages are called 'ban pa miang' (miang-forest villages); pickers use a finger blade ('lep kep miang') and take only two-thirds of each leaf. HRDI notes miang is still used in funerals, weddings, housewarmings and rituals, and supports forest-intercropped highland livelihoods. Younger people know it less.

**To verify:** Tone of เหมี้ยง (derived 44ʔ); Phrase อมเหมี้ยงก่อ is constructed, not sourced

Sources: <https://krua.co/food_story/mieang-som-mieang-whan-lanna-refreshment/> · <https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/how-to/tips/article_21570> · <https://www.matichon.co.th/weekly/column/article_172905> · <https://www.matichonacademy.com/tour-story/%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B5%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%87-%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD-%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B5%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%87-%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD> · <https://hkm.hrdi.or.th/Knowledge/detail/657>

## Nam oi (Northern solid cane sugar) and nga tam oi (sesame pounded with cane sugar)

**น้ำอ้อย (น้ำตาลอ้อยก้อน) และงาตำอ้อย** · Kham Mueang: **น้ำอ้อย / งาตำอ้อย / เข้าบ่ายงาตำอ้อย** · *nam oi; nga tam oi*  
Say it: น้ำ *nam* (45) · อ้อย *oi* (44ʔ)  
Tip: WARNING for translators: in Central Thai น้ำอ้อย means sugarcane JUICE; in Lanna it usually means SOLID cane sugar made by boiling juice and setting it in blocks (Matichon Weekly).  
From: ทั่วล้านนา across Lanna · Season: Cane-boiling after harvest; nga tam oi is a cool-season treat · Occasion: Cooking staple; winter snack · Serves: 4 (nga tam oi) · Confidence: **high**

In Lanna 'nam oi' is fresh cane juice boiled down and set into blocks - used in hang lay curry, pickled mustard greens, pounded salads and sweets such as khao taen and khanom jok. 'Nga tam oi' is toasted sesame pounded with nam oi, pressed into a ball of sticky rice ('khao bai nga tam oi') - a warming cool-season snack.

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northern cane sugar block | น้ำอ้อยก้อน / น้ำอ้อย (*nam oi*) | 100 g | Warorot. Golden-brown, fragrant blocks or cakes; some are mixed with coconut | Jaggery, panela, rapadura |
| toasted black or white sesame | งาดำหรืองาขาวคั่ว / งา (*nga*) | 100 g | Warorot | - |
| salt | เกลือ (*kluea*) | pinch | Supermarket | - |
| hot steamed sticky rice | ข้าวเหนียวนึ่งร้อน / เข้านึ่ง (*khao nueng*) | 300 g | Ton Lamyai market | - |

**Equipment:** mortar; dry pan for toasting

**Prep ahead:** Steam sticky rice.

### Method

1. Dry-toast sesame on low heat. *(5 min)* **Look for:** Seeds pop and smell nutty. **Tip:** Burns fast - keep moving.
2. Pound sesame with chopped nam oi and salt to a sticky paste. *(10 min)* **Look for:** Oily, clumping paste.
3. Flatten a handful of hot sticky rice, fill with the paste, close into a ball. *(5 min)*

### Taste

Sour 0 · Salty 1 · Spicy 0 · Sweet 4 · Bitter 1 · Umami 1 (0-5, estimate). Sweet, toasty sesame. *Texture:* Sticky, gritty-oily sesame paste inside chewy rice.

Words to use: หวาน *wan* (sweet), ลำ *lam* (delicious), ลำขนาด *lam khanat* (super tasty)

### How to eat it

Eat warm on cool mornings. **With:** Hot tea. **When:** Cool season. **Etiquette:** Made at home and shared.

**Safety:** Sesame is a common allergen.

**Common mistakes:**
- Ordering 'nam oi' expecting cane juice - ask for 'น้ำอ้อยคั้น' (fresh-pressed cane juice) if that's what you want.

**Phrases:**
- น้ำอ้อยคั้นมีก่อเจ้า (*nam oi khan mi ko jao*) = Do you have fresh cane juice? (Central: มีน้ำอ้อยคั้นไหมคะ). At a drinks stall, to avoid the solid-sugar meaning
- อันนี้กี่บาทเจ้า (*an ni ki bat jao*) = How much is this? (Central: อันนี้กี่บาทคะ). Buying snacks and sweets at the market.

**Culture:** Matichon Weekly: 'In Lanna we boil fresh cane juice and make it into blocks, and call that nam oi.' Red sugarcane (oi daeng) is used in medicine and rituals.

**To verify:** Proportions for nga tam oi are a home ratio, not sourced

Sources: <https://www.matichon.co.th/weekly/column/article_710513> · <https://www.agrinewsthai.com/did-you-know/161361>

## Cha miang - brewed Northern Assam ('wild') tea

**ชาเมี่ยง / ชาอัสสัม (ชงดื่ม)** · Kham Mueang: **น้ำเหมี้ยง? (ยังไม่ยืนยัน) / ชา** · *cha miang*  
Say it: ชา *cha* (33) · เมี่ยง *miang* (42)  
Tip: Central spelling given; see miang entry for Northern เหมี้ยง.  
From: เชียงใหม่ Chiang Mai, เชียงราย Chiang Rai, น่าน Nan, แพร่ Phrae - highland tea villages · Season: All year · Occasion: Everyday; homestays in tea villages · Serves: 2 · Confidence: **low**

HRDI (Highland Research and Development Institute) describes Assam tea - locally 'wild tea', 'native tea' or 'cha miang' - as widely grown in Northern highlands and rich in polyphenols and flavonoids. Besides fermenting for chewing, it is increasingly processed as brewing tea. Traditional Lanna use of miang was chewing, not drinking (Matichon Academy).

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Thai / Kham Mueang | Amount | Where to buy / choose | Abroad |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dried Assam/wild tea leaves | ใบชาอัสสัมแห้ง (*bai cha*) | 2 tsp (4 g) | Warorot. Buy from hill-village producers or Warorot tea stalls | - |
| hot water ~90-95 C | น้ำร้อน (*nam ron*) | 400 ml | Ton Lamyai market | - |

**Equipment:** teapot

**Prep ahead:** None.

### Method

1. Warm the pot, add leaves, pour hot water. *(1 min)*
2. Steep 2-3 minutes; pour. *(3 min)* **Look for:** Amber liquor, brisk and slightly smoky.

### Taste

Sour 0 · Salty 0 · Spicy 0 · Sweet 0 · Bitter 2 · Umami 1 (0-5, estimate). Brisk, lightly astringent. *Texture:* Light-bodied, astringent finish.

Words to use: ฝาด *fat* (astringent), ขม *khom* (bitter)

### How to eat it

Drink hot, plain. **With:** Sweets like khanom pat. **When:** Any time. **Etiquette:** Hosts refill cups; leave a little if you've had enough.

**Safety:** Caffeine.

**Common mistakes:**
- Assuming it's the orange Thai milk tea (cha yen) - a different product.

**Phrases:**
- ปอแล้วเจ้า (*po laeo jao*) = That's enough, thank you (Central: พอแล้วค่ะ). When a host keeps refilling your bowl.

**Culture:** Tea villages such as Mae Kampong (Chiang Mai) are community-tourism destinations (not verified with an opened source this round).

**To verify:** Kham Mueang name for brewed tea; Brewing parameters are generic

Sources: <https://hkm.hrdi.or.th/Knowledge/detail/657> · <https://www.matichonacademy.com/tour-story/%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B5%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%87-%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD-%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B5%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%87-%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD>

## More Lanna sweets, in brief

- **ขนมลิ้นหมา / ข้าวหนมเปี่ยง** (*khanom lin ma / khao nom piang*): 'Dog's tongue' - black sticky-rice flour dough wrapped flat in leaf and steamed; eaten with coconut and sugar. [medium] <https://www.lannapost.net/2021/09/blog-post_15.html> <https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/page/archives/587453/>
- **ขนมวง / เข้ามูนข่วย** (*khanom wong*): Tai Yai-origin fried sticky-rice rings coated in sesame and glazed with palm syrup (Lanna Post); Chiang Mai News version uses banana or pumpkin in the dough. [medium] <https://www.lannapost.net/2021/09/blog-post_15.html> <https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/page/archives/587453/>
- **ขนมตายลืม** (*khanom tai luem*): Very soft salty rice-flour sweet like khanom kluea, made for children, the sick and elderly. [medium] <https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/page/archives/587453/>
- **ขนมกล้วย** (*khanom kluai*): Ripe banana, rice flour, coconut and sugar steamed in cone-shaped leaf parcels. [medium] <https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/page/archives/587453/>
- **ข้าวปั้น / ขนมมี้ปัน (ลำปาง)** (*khao pan*): Lampang cup-shaped Chinese-influenced sweet with syrup, salted pork and fried garlic (Lanna Post) - details unverified. [low] <https://www.lannapost.net/2021/09/blog-post_15.html>
- **ข้าวจี่** (*khao ji*): Grilled sticky-rice patties offered with khao lam at tan khao mai (4th Northern month). [medium] <https://today.line.me/th/v3/article/GgWNP5Q>

## How the tones were worked out

Tones are DERIVED from spelling with the Chiang Mai tone-box mapping used across this dataset (high/mid class live unmarked=24 low-rising; low class live unmarked=33 mid; high/mid + mai ek or dead=21 low-falling; low + mai ek or dead long=42 high-falling; high/mid + mai tho=44ʔ high-level-glottal; low + mai tho or dead short=45 high-rising; Northern ๋ on a mid-class consonant read as 24, ๊ read as 45). Not from a recorded dictionary: needs a native Chiang Mai speaker check. Nan, Phrae, Lampang and Chiang Rai contours differ.

## Sensitivity

- 'Ngiao' (เงี้ยว) is an old Thai exonym for the Shan people; considered impolite for people today - use 'Tai Yai' (ไทใหญ่) or 'Tai' (ไต). The dish name 'nam ngiao' is used normally.
- Khao soi is presented in Thai sources as a Yunnanese Muslim (Chin Haw / Haw) and Shan dish later adapted in Chiang Mai; Muslim shops are halal (no pork).
- Beliefs/rituals (tan khao mai, hing fai phra jao) are practised by many Northern Buddhists with local variation.
