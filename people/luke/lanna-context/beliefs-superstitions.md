# Beliefs, spirits and superstitions in Lanna / Northern Thailand

Context data for Contextual Translate. Structured entries are in `beliefs_superstitions.json` (23 entries). Beliefs below are held by **some** people and communities. They vary by village, family and generation, and many younger urban people half-believe them or treat them as custom. Never present them as universal.

## Key points for the translation model
- **Markets:** the first sale of the day (*poet thung*, "opening the bag") matters to many vendors. They may tap the first money across their goods (Northern term reported as ลองหมาน, needs checking). If you are the first customer, bargain gently and buy once you have haggled. Nang Kwak figures and spirit houses at stalls are normal and should be treated respectfully.
- **Spirit houses** (*san phra phum*; Northern *ho phi*) are found everywhere. Don't touch the offerings. The red soda offering is a friendly thing to ask about.
- **Khwan and white string:** the Lanna *hong khwan* ceremony calls the khwan (life essence) back and ties white string on the wrist. Receiving it is an honour. Let the string wear off. Don't cut it in front of the giver.
- **Inthakhin** (city pillar at Wat Chedi Luang): women are not allowed into the pillar shrine. The flower-offering festival runs for 8 days from the 12th waning day of lunar month 6 (around May-June).
- **Pu Sae Ya Sae** (Mae Hia, around May-June): a buffalo offering to guardian spirits linked to the Lawa people. It is controversial, so don't bring it up in small talk.
- **Ancestral spirits / phi mot phi meng spirit dance:** clan and family matters. Let locals raise them.
- **Calendar:** Lanna good and bad days (*wan di / wan sia / wan chom / wan fu*) depend on the lunar month and the hour. The Northern month count is about 2 months ahead of the central Thai one. Accept date changes made for auspicious timing graciously.
- **Zodiac:** in the North, the pig year is the **elephant**. This is confirmed by the Thailand Foundation and Wat Ban Den material. Northern Buddhists visit the relic temple of their birth year.
- **Easy, friendly topics:** colours of the day, lucky 9 (*kao* ≈ "step forward"), no haircuts on Wednesday, rainbow pointing, birth-year animal, amulets, spirit houses.
- **Avoid or be careful:** mocking beliefs, the Pu Sae Ya Sae sacrifice, the Inthakhin gender rule, family ancestral rites, and a highland person's citizenship or legal status.
- **Highland groups:** use the names people call themselves. Say **Akha**, never "Ekaw/Kaw", which is derogatory. Say **Pgaz K'Nyau** or Karen. Akha village gates mark the boundary with the spirit world. The Akha swing festival is in late August. Karen communities keep umbilical-cord forests (*de paw thoo*), so don't damage trees.
- **Tattoos and amulets:** keep Buddha images above the waist and don't touch someone else's sak yant.
- **Tak bat (alms-giving):** buy a proper alms set, take off your shoes, stay quiet, and don't use flash. Women should not touch monks.

## Needs expert review
1. ลองหมาน / "long man": the Northern term for tapping goods with the first money. Romanisation and meaning are unverified.
2. The "phi pu nya" Northern pronunciation is inferred from Kham Mueang sound correspondences.
3. *ho phi* as the Northern everyday name for a spirit house. The source uses it only as a ritual term.
4. Pu Sae Ya Sae month: Wikipedia gives the 9th Northern month, another source the 7th (probably the central count). The exact 2026/2027 date needs checking.
5. Wearing the wrist string for at least 3 days and never cutting it: this comes from a general Thai/Lao source and practice varies.
6. Whistling at night: no reliable source was found, so it is listed as unverified. Pregnancy and food taboos are not covered beyond the vendor belief.
7. Akha gate "do not touch" etiquette: widely repeated, but no authoritative source was opened.
8. 9 = "rice": the travel source's claim is doubtful (*khao* ≠ *kao*).
