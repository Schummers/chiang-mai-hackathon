## 2. The three seasons (Thai government definition)
Source: https://www.thailand.go.th/issue-focus-detail/009_142 (high)
- **Hot season** (ฤดูร้อน, rue du ron): mid-Feb to mid-May
- **Rainy season** (ฤดูฝน, rue du fon): mid-May to mid-Oct. In 2025 TMD declared the start on 15 May (https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40050016).
- **Cool season** (ฤดูหนาว, rue du nao): mid-Oct to mid-Feb

The Thai names are standard Central Thai. The source page gives only the English names. [REVIEW]

In practice, Chiang Mai's cool season means cool nights (15-17 C) and warm, dry days of about 30 C.

## 3. Smoke / haze (burning) season
- **Timing:** the haze builds from Dec-Jan and peaks in **March**, often into April, until the pre-monsoon rains. In 2021, Chiang Mai's monthly mean PM2.5 was 42.5 ug/m3 in Jan, 47.7 in Feb and 83.3 in Mar (https://www.iqair.com/newsroom/thailand-2021-burning-season, high).
- **Causes:** forest fires, agricultural burning (maize, sugarcane, rice stubble), community waste burning, vehicles, and cross-border smoke. The valley traps the smoke. (https://en.thairath.co.th/news/local/2905468)
- **Rules:** the National Environment Board designates the North a control zone from February to May each year. Chiang Mai banned open burning from 1 Jan to 31 May 2026 (Thairath, high).
- **Thai standard:** 24-hour PM2.5 of 37.5 ug/m3 from 1 June 2023 (was 50); the annual standard is 15 (https://www.thaipbsworld.com/environment/thailands-new-air-quality-standard-to-become-effective-in-june-2023). The Thai AQI bands are Excellent 0-25, Good 26-50, Moderate 51-100, Unhealthy 101-200 and Very Unhealthy 201+. The Thai AQI and the US AQI are different scales. Label which one the app shows.
- **Crisis framing:** Thai PBS Policy Watch calls it "วิกฤตฝุ่นควัน-ไฟป่าภาคเหนือ" (the northern dust-smoke and forest-fire crisis). It notes worst-in-years "purple" levels in late March 2026 (https://policywatch.thaipbs.or.th/article/environment-179).
- **How people talk about it:** these are the everyday words.
  - ฝุ่น (fun, "dust"), usually "ฝุ่น PM2.5"
  - ฝุ่นควัน (fun khwan, dust-smoke)
  - หมอกควัน (mok khwan, haze/smog)
  - ไฟป่า (fai pa, forest fire)
  - การเผา (kan phao, burning)

  Common small-talk topics are masks, AQI apps (Air4Thai, IQAir, CMU DustBoy), air purifiers, not seeing Doi Suthep, and residents or nomads leaving town. [REVIEW: the Kham Mueang forms of these words, if any differ]
- **Cultural note for the app:** smoke is a sensitive topic. Blame often falls on hill farmers, and many of them are ethnic minorities. The IQAir piece links burning to farm economics: debt, and manual clearing costing 30-40% more. The app should avoid blaming language ("the farmers burning everything"). It should translate neutrally and add context if a user blames a group.

## 4. Rainy season and Ping River flooding
- The flood season is **Aug-Oct**. The biggest peaks came in late September and early October (https://www.chiangmaidoctor.com/flooding-chiangmai, medium; this is a clinic site that cites hydrological figures):
  - 2005: +4.93 m
  - 2011: +4.95 m
  - 2022: +4.75 m on 5 Oct
  - 2024: **+5.30 m on 5 Oct**, the worst in recent memory, lasting 102 hours
- The gauge is at Nawarat Bridge (P.1). The bank-full/critical level is about 4.2 m (https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40042033).
- Areas that flooded in 2024 include Chang Khlan and Charoen Prathet roads, the **Night Bazaar**, Pa Daet and Nong Hoi (https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2871931/chiang-mai-city-flooded-as-ping-river-overflows). These matter for market conversations. In Sep-Oct, vendors near the river may talk about water levels, closures and cleanup.
- **Daily life in the rains:** afternoon and evening downpours, motorbike riders sheltering or in ponchos, fewer tourists ("green season"), and vendors covering stalls.

## 5. Seasonal market foods (weather-linked)
- **Hed thop / hed phao** (เห็ดถอบ / เห็ดเผาะ, earthstar "puffball" mushrooms, Astraeus). These appear in early May after 2-3 weeks of alternating rain and heat. They are sold by the **litre** (ลิตร) at roadsides and markets. Early-season prices were about 450-500 THB/litre in 2025 (https://mgronline.com/local/detail/9680000041908). Hang Dong market later that month showed 100-240 THB/litre, with stock partly from Sukhothai (https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/social/3660967/). Good small-talk topic: "Are the hed thop out yet?" [REVIEW: one site mistranslates these as "straw mushrooms"; they are not straw mushrooms]
- **Kaeng kradang** (แกงกระด้าง): pork jelly curry, a Lanna cool-season dish because the gelatin sets in the cold (https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/here-are-local-dishes-inspired-by-summer-rain-and-winter-of-thailand, medium).
- **Rainy season:** wild mushrooms, bitter greens and forest vegetables (Michelin, same source).

## 6. Lanna calendar months (verified: two months ahead of Central Thai)
- Source: https://anowl.co/anowlrod/sa-bpa/lao-reuang_winner_ep12/ (medium-high). It gives the table and the agricultural reason for the offset.
- Source: https://www.komchadluek.net/news/10027, which quotes "คนพื้นเมืองล้านนานับเดือนเร็วกว่าคนภาคกลางราว 2 เดือน" ("Lanna people count months about 2 months ahead of Central Thais").

| Lanna month | Thai | = Central Thai lunar month | Approx. Gregorian |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | เดือนเกี๋ยง (duean kiang) | 11 | Oct |
| 2 | เดือนยี่ (duean yi) | 12 | Nov |
| 3 | เดือนสาม | 1 (เดือนอ้าย) | Dec |
| 4 | เดือนสี่ | 2 (เดือนยี่) | Jan |
| 5 | เดือนห้า | 3 | Feb |
| 6 | เดือนหก | 4 | Mar |
| 7 | เดือนเจ็ด | 5 | Apr |
| 8 | เดือนแปด | 6 | May |
| 9 | เดือนเก้า | 7 | Jun |
| 10 | เดือนสิบ | 8 | Jul |
| 11 | เดือนสิบเอ็ด | 9 | Aug |
| 12 | เดือนสิบสอง | 10 | Sep |

The source lists the months with a "เหนือ" (northern) suffix, e.g. เดือนยี่เหนือ. Lunar months drift against the Gregorian calendar each year, so compute festival dates from an almanac rather than from this table.

**Why this matters for translation:** a local saying "เดือนยี่" in Chiang Mai means November (Yi Peng time). A Central Thai speaker saying the same words means roughly January. The same applies to "เดือนแปด", "เดือนเจ็ด" and so on. The app should flag this ambiguity.

**Festival naming examples:**
- **Yi Peng** (ยี่เป็ง): yi = two, peng = full moon. It is the full moon of Lanna month 2, which is Central month 12, the same night as Loi Krathong, usually in November (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Peng_festival, high).
- **Pi Mai Mueang / Songkran:** falls in เดือนเจ็ด (Lanna month 7, mostly April). It is regarded as the passing of the old year into the new (https://www.matichon.co.th/weekly/column/article_95784, high).
- **Inthakhin** (city-pillar festival): Wikipedia gives the "6th lunar month" (Central count), which is Lanna month 8. It falls in late May-early June and marks the start of the rains (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inthakhin_(pillar)). [REVIEW: the rain-petition meaning is common knowledge; the page fetched did not state it]
- **Salak Phat** (ประเพณีสลากภัต / บุญข้าวสลาก): merit-making in Lanna month 1, เดือนเกี๋ยง (anowl source).

## 7. Weather small talk: useful Kham Mueang
