# Ethnic groups of Northern Thailand: context for respectful conversation

Data file: `ethnic_groups.json` (17 groups, 9 Chiang Mai places). Every fact there has a source URL and a confidence level. This page is the summary for humans.

## How the app should use this
- Add a group entry to the context packet **only when the person's profile card says which group they belong to**. Never guess someone's ethnicity from how they look, what they are called or what they sell.
- Use the group's own name for itself. The Thai umbrella term ชาวเขา *chao khao* ("hill tribe") dates from 1960s government documents. IWGIA considers it derogatory, so prefer the group name, *klum chatiphan* (กลุ่มชาติพันธุ์, "ethnic group") or "Indigenous peoples". ([Wikipedia: Hill tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_tribe_(Thailand)))
- **Never raise citizenship, ID cards, legal papers or migrant status.** Hundreds of thousands of highland people are stateless, and in 2025 a fast-track citizenship process was set up for about 480,000 pending cases. Leave these topics to the person to bring up. ([Minority Rights Group](https://minorityrights.org/communities/indigenous-peoples-thailand/))
- Frame notes around greetings, food, crafts and festivals. No stereotypes and no claims about individuals.

## Groups in brief
**Khon Mueang (Northern Thai).** Kra-Dai, Southwestern Tai. About 6 million people and the majority across the North. They speak Kham Mueang, written in Tai Tham script, which is being revived after Thaification. Greetings: *sa-wat-di jao* (women, สะหวัดดีเจ้า) or *khap* (men); thank you is *yin-di jao/khap* (ยินดี). This is a false friend: in Central Thai ยินดี means "you're welcome/glad".

**Tai Yai (Shan).** Self-name Tai. Mostly in Mae Hong Son, Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai (Mae Sai) and Lampang. About 90% of Chiang Mai Province's 100,000+ migrant workers are Shan. Greeting: *Mai Soong* ("Mai Soong Pi Mai Tai" means Happy Shan New Year, around late November). Foods: naam ngiao, kaeng hang lay (pork belly curry), nam phrik tua nao, thamin (sticky-rice coconut cake), Shan khao soi, pea tofu, khao kan jin. Festival: **Poy Sang Long**, the novice ordination of boys dressed as princes, in late March to early April (2026: 31 Mar to 3 Apr). It is biggest in Mae Hong Son and in Chiang Mai is held at **Wat Pa Pao** (founded 1883, the first Shan temple in the city).

**Tai Lue.** Southwestern Tai with its own script, originally from Sipsongpanna. Found in Nan, Phayao (Chiang Kham), Chiang Rai and Chiang Mai. Many were resettled in Lanna around 1782–1845.

**Tai Khuen.** Originally from Kengtung. Brought to the Wat Nantaram/Wualai area of Chiang Mai as war captives under Kawila. Known for lacquerware, with only a few artisans left. Written in Tai Tham.

**Tai Yong.** From Mueang Yong in Shan State. About 10,000 were resettled in Lamphun (Pa Sang) and are famed as weavers.

**Karen (Sgaw, Pwo).** Karenic (Sino-Tibetan) and the largest highland group (roughly 320–400k). They are animist, Buddhist and Christian, often in the same place. Greetings (Karen Org. of Minnesota, probably Sgaw): *Ghaw luh a ghay* (hello), *Ta bluh doh mah* (thank you). Karen New Year falls in Dec/Jan; the wrist-tying ceremony is in August. They are known for natural-dye weaving.

**Kayan.** Mae Hong Son. Neck-ring tourism is ethically contested (UNHCR concerns in 2008). Don't treat people as an attraction.

**Hmong.** Hmong-Mien family; White Hmong (Daw) and Green/Blue (Njua/Leng). Greetings: *Nyob zoo* (hello), *Ua tsaug* (thank you). Goods: batik and embroidered skirts, vintage textiles at the **Hmong Market** beside Warorot. The Doi Pui village (Green Hmong) on Doi Suthep has shops and cafés. Hmong New Year comes at the end of harvest (in Thailand often Nov–Dec) and features the *pov pob* ball toss.

**Iu Mien.** Mienic. About 40k people. Religion is Taoist-influenced, with ritual texts in Chinese characters, and they are known for fine embroidery.

**Lahu.** Loloish; Lahu Na is a highland lingua franca. *Aw bon ui ja* means thank you. **Kin Wo (กินวอ)** New Year falls after harvest, usually Feb–Apr and set by each village, with *o pu* sticky-rice balls and dancing through the night.

**Lisu.** Loloish, about 55k people. New Year coincides with Chinese New Year and involves silver jewellery and people's best clothes.

**Akha.** Loloish, 70–80k people, mostly in Chiang Rai. The **Swing Festival** (Yehkuja) is in late August. Don't touch the village spirit gate (*lokha*). Akha Ama Coffee (Lee Ayu Chuepa) is a well-known Chiang Mai café.

**Lawa/Lua.** Palaungic (Austroasiatic). They were in the North before the Tai, and Chiang Mai was founded on a Lawa site. Found in Mae Sariang and Bo Luang.

**Khmu.** Khmuic, about 10k people, in Nan, Phayao and Chiang Rai.

**Mal/Prai (H'tin).** Close to Khmu. Found in Nan (Pua, Chiang Klang, Thong Chang).

**Yunnanese Chinese (Chin Haw).** Descended from caravan traders and later KMT remnants; about a third are Muslim (Hui). **Ban Haw Mosque** near the Night Bazaar is the largest in the province. The **Friday-morning Yunnan market** on Chang Khlan sells cured ham and sausage, corn pancakes, dim sum, and Burmese mohinga and samosas. Khao soi's predecessor arrived through Chin Haw Muslim traders; *khao soi Islam* is halal.

**Myanmar migrant workers.** Say "migrant" or "undocumented", never "illegal". Many are Shan (Tai) rather than Bamar, so ask before assuming Burmese. *Mingalaba* (hello) is for Burmese speakers.

## Chiang Mai places
Warorot/Kad Luang, Ton Lamyai, Hmong Market, Wat Ket (museum founded by Jack Bain), Ban Haw Mosque and the Friday Yunnan market, Wat Pa Pao, Wat Nantaram, the Highland People Discovery Museum (formerly the Tribal Museum, next to Ratchamangkhala Park, with a monthly highland market), and Doi Pui Hmong village.
