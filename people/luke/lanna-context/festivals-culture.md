Data files: `festival_calendar.json` (19 entries) and `etiquette.json` (13 etiquette rules, 5 Lanna concepts, conversation topics). Where a date is `estimated: true`, no official announcement was found. Researched on 2026-09-26.

## Key rule: Lanna months
Lanna month numbers run **two ahead** of Central Thai months. Central month 12 is Lanna month 2, which is why the festival is called *Yi Peng*: "yi" means two and "peng" means full moon. The Inthakhin festival is described as "month 8 in, month 9 out", which is Central months 6 to 7. Source: [Wikipedia Yi Peng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Peng_festival).

## Notes by festival (for the translation cultural note)
- **Yi Peng**: Homes and temples hang lanterns called *khom khwaen* and *khom fai*, and light small clay lamps called *phang prathip*. People float krathong on the Ping River. **The municipality strictly asks that no sky lanterns be released inside Chiang Mai city.** Mass releases only happen at ticketed events outside the city, and many look-alike ticket sites exist. [chiangmainews](https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/news/chiangmai/4138857/)
- **Pi Mai Mueang**: The days run in this order:
  1. *Sangkhan Long*: firecrackers and house cleaning.
  2. *Wan Nao*: a no-quarrelling taboo, and people carry sand to temples to build sand chedis decorated with tung flags.
  3. *Wan Phaya Wan*: the true New Year. People do **dam hua**, offering elders scented water with som poi and asking their forgiveness.
  4. *Wan Pak Pi*.
  Foods: laap, and kaeng khanun (jackfruit curry). [MJU Library](https://thelibrary.mju.ac.th/?p=7514)
- **Tan Kuay Salak**: Families fill woven baskets with rice, pork crackling, sai ua, soap and similar goods. A slip on each basket names a dead relative, and monks draw the slips like lots. [Silpa](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_38356)
- **Salak Yom (Lamphun)**: Unmarried women offer salak trees 3 to 12 m tall. The practice is believed to earn merit equal to a man's ordination. It comes from the Yong people (related to the Tai Lue).
- **Inthakhin**: People bring *khan dok* flower offerings to the city pillar. **Women are traditionally barred from the pillar shrine.** If a user asks why, the app should give the neutral local explanation.
- **Poy Sang Long**: Shan (Tai) boys are dressed as princes and carried so their feet never touch the ground, then ordained as novices.
- **Hmong New Year**: Use the name "Hmong". The Thai term "Maeo" is considered derogatory (needs expert confirmation).

## Etiquette essentials (details in etiquette.json)
- **Temples**: Cover shoulders and knees, take off shoes and hats, and point your feet away from Buddha images. Monks may not touch women or take items from a woman's hand.
- **Head and feet**: Don't touch anyone's head. Don't point your feet at people or step over them.
- **Monarchy**: Article 112 carries 3 to 15 years per count and applies to foreigners. Stand for the royal anthem. **The app should never generate commentary on the royal family.**
- **Alcohol**: Sales are banned all day on the 5 major Buddhist days: Makha Bucha, Visakha Bucha, Asalha Bucha, Khao Phansa and Ok Phansa. Hotels, airport terminals and licensed entertainment venues are exempt. The penalty is up to 6 months and/or 10,000 THB. Elections have a ban from 18:00 the day before to 18:00 on the day. Songkran and Loy Krathong are *not* ban days.
- **Lanna concepts**:
  - *khwan* is the soul essence. The soul-calling ceremony is *hong khwan* or *su khwan*.
  - *phi* are spirits: *phi pu ya* are the family's ancestors and *phi suea ban* is the village guardian. Translate phi as "guardian/ancestor spirit", not "ghost".
  - *phit phi* means offending the ancestor spirits, for example through improper physical contact in a family's home.
  - Ancestor shrines and spirit houses: don't touch the offerings.
  - A khantoke is a low tray used as a table, with sticky rice.
  - *Sin tin chok* is a skirt with a decorated hem woven in the chok technique, from Mae Chaem.
- **Good topics**: food, the season, festivals, crafts, and trying a few Kham Mueang words. **Avoid**: the monarchy, politics and how someone votes, criticising Buddhism, public confrontation, and treating highland peoples as "exotic".
