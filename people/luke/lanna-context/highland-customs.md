# Highland Peoples and Tai Minority Groups: Customs for Respectful Interaction

Context data for Contextual Translate (Chiang Mai). Machine-readable version: `highland_customs.json`.
Compiled 2026-09-26. Every claim links to a source; items marked **[REVIEW]** need a check by the team's Thai-culture reviewer.

## Framing rules for the model

- Most people in these groups are **Thai citizens living modern lives** as traders, farmers, students and professionals. Never frame them as "primitive", "untouched" or "unchanging".
- **Use self-designations.** "Hill tribe" (*chao khao*, ชาวเขา) has been the official Thai term since the 1960s, but IWGIA and indigenous advocates consider it derogatory ([Wikipedia: Hill tribe (Thailand)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_tribe_(Thailand)); [Minority Rights Group](https://minorityrights.org/communities/indigenous-peoples-thailand/)).
- Describe beliefs as **held by some people or communities**. Practice varies by village, religion (animist, Buddhist, Christian, Muslim) and generation.
- **Topics to avoid in small talk:** citizenship, statelessness, refugee status, land and forest disputes, opium history.

## Signs that you should not enter

| Sign | Meaning | What to do | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Taleo**: woven bamboo star or lattice above a door, at a field edge or at a village entrance | Marks a protected space or an event in progress, and a sanction on entering or leaving. It comes from Kmhmu tradition and many groups use it. | Stop, call out and ask permission. | [TAEC Laos](https://www.taeclaos.org/featured_item/taleo/) (a Lao source) **[REVIEW: confirm Thai usage]** |
| **Sticks with green leaves**, sometimes in a cross shape, outside a Hmong house | The family should not be disturbed. The house is being protected from spirits during a ritual or healing. | Do not enter or knock. Come back later. | [EthnoMed](https://ethnomed.org/culture/hmong/) (a US diaspora source) **[REVIEW]** |
| **Akha village gate** (carved wooden gate with human figures and birds) and **village swing** | The gate marks the line between the human village and the spirit world and is renewed every year. People believe a human touch defiles it and that only ritual and sacrifice can restore it. | Don't touch the gate or swing, climb on them or pose with them. | [Wikipedia: Akha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akha_people); [Green Shinto](https://www.greenshinto.com/2018/09/17/the-akha-spirit-gate/) |

General etiquette: take off shoes before entering a home, don't touch adults' heads, don't point your feet at people, dress modestly, and buy crafts or pay for homestays and meals rather than handing out cash or sweets ([tour operator guidance, low confidence](https://www.thailandhilltribeholidays.com/visit-hill-tribe-villages-dos-donts/)). Ask before photographing anyone, and if you promise to send photos back, do it.

## Group summaries

### Karen: Sgaw (*Pgaz K'nyau*) and Pwo (*Phlong*)
- Roughly half of Thailand's officially listed highland population. Communities are animist, Buddhist or Christian.
- *Yang* and *Kariang* are outsider names. Prefer "Karen". **[REVIEW: whether these names are felt as offensive]**
- **Wrist-tying (*ki cu*):** held Feb–Apr at the start of the farming season, and again as *Laku* in Aug–Sep to seek the rice goddess's blessing. Thread is tied three times around each wrist with prayers, then the family shares a meal ([Karen Hilltribes Trust](https://karenhilltribes.org.uk/ceremony-and-community-in-the-hills/)). Karen Wrist Tying Day is in August.
- **Dress:** unmarried girls traditionally wear white, and married women wear different styles. Backstrap-loom weaving is still practised. Dress is identity, not costume.
- **Goods:** woven cloth and "Karen hill tribe silver" (widely sold). Ask where and by whom a piece was made.
- **Phrases** (from the [Karen Organization of Minnesota](https://mnkaren.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Basic-Karen-Language-Phrases.pdf), informal romanisation) **[REVIEW with Thai Sgaw speakers]**: *Ta bluh doh mah* ("thank you very much"), *Na oh sue oh klay ah?* ("how are you?").

### Kayan (Kayan Lahwi)
- Kayan in Mae Hong Son **object to "Padaung"**. Avoid "long neck Karen" and "giraffe women".
- **Ethics:** many Kayan came as refugees from Myanmar in the late 1980s and 1990s. UNHCR criticised the refusal to let registered Kayan refugees take up resettlement offers, reportedly for tourism revenue. Reports describe movement restrictions outside their home province, low stipends, and entrance fees going to operators ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayan_people_(Myanmar)); [Chiang Mai CityLife](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/clg/our-city/meet-the-locals/frozen-in-time-stuck-in-place-inside-the-controversial-world-of-the-long-neck-kayan/)).
- Residents' views differ. Some describe a "human zoo". Others, for example at Huay Pu Keng, welcome visitors who come for genuine exchange ([Huck](https://www.huckmag.com/article/kayan-rings)).
- Since at least 2006, some younger women have removed their rings to continue their education or in protest.
- Etiquette: don't ask to touch or try on the rings, buy crafts directly, and ask before taking photos. **[REVIEW: current status in 2026]**

### Hmong
- In Thai: ม้ง. Mahidol's museum glosses the name as "free people". Subgroups include Blue/Green and White Hmong.
- **"Meo" (แม้ว) is derogatory.** It was a former registration term and is often used as an insult.
- **Welcome:** *koj tuaj los* ("you have come"). **Hello:** *Nyob zoo* ([Study Hmong](https://studyhmong.com/basic-greetings-nyob-zoo/)).
- **House spirits and altar:** spirits are believed to live in the corners, the stove and the doors, and there is a household altar (*xwm kab*). Don't touch it. Many elders do not want strangers touching their heads or their children's heads. Traditionally, the reed organ (*qeej*) is not practised indoors ([Mahidol](https://museumlc.mahidol.ac.th/hmong/)).
- **New Year (*Noj Tsiab Peb Caug*):** held from the 1st day of the 1st lunar month, with each community setting its own dates, usually Dec–Jan. Household rites include soul-calling (*hu plig*) and ancestor offerings. Young people play a ball-tossing courtship game (*pov pob*). An urban Hmong New Year has been held in Chiang Mai city since 1999 ([Hmong Studies Journal](https://www.hmongstudiesjournal.org/uploads/4/5/8/7/4587788/yangcheepsutjarit_hsj_21.pdf)).
- **Textiles:** *paj ntaub* embroidery and appliqué. Indigo batik is made by Blue/Green Hmong but not White Hmong. Story cloths began in refugee camps in the 1970s. Hmong traders have sold at the Chiang Mai Night Bazaar since the 1970s.

### Iu Mien
- **Prefer "Mien".** "Yao" (เย้า) is an official classification that Mien people do not use for themselves.
- Religion centres on ancestor veneration, with Taoist-derived rituals and a ritual script based on Chinese characters. Treat altars, ritual paintings and ritual books as sacred.
- **Dress:** turbans, jackets with red trim, and densely cross-stitched trousers. Silver is ceremonial, for example a bracelet at engagement. Cross-stitch goods come especially from Nan ([Nan Creative City](https://www.nancreativecity.org/content/5866/mienembroideredfabric)).
- **Festivals:** Lunar New Year and "Spirit Day". **[REVIEW: lunar date]**

### Lahu
- Subgroups are named after dress colour: Lahu Na (Black), Lahu Nyi (Red), Lahu Shi (Yellow) and Shehleh.
- "Muser" (มูเซอ) means "hunter". Mahidol lists it without calling it offensive, but "Lahu" is preferred. **[REVIEW]**
- **New Year (Kin Wo):** no fixed date. It follows the harvest, usually Feb–Apr, and runs 12 days in two phases. It includes all-night dancing, top-spinning, cloth-ball games, and offerings to G'ui sha ([Thai Tour Study](https://thaitourstudy.com/routepiangluang/en/?modules=travel-detail&id=25)).
- Village elders hold collective authority. Ask for the headman when you arrive.

### Lisu
- ลีซู. "Lisaw" is an older Thai form.
- **Dress:** brightly coloured. Women wear red and white bead headwear; men wear red sashes and tasselled bags ([Mahidol](https://museumlc.mahidol.ac.th/lisu/)).
- **New Year:** coincides with Chinese New Year and lasts 3 to 7 days. It includes ancestor offerings, honouring elders and dancing around a planted tree. Hosts offer tea, snacks and liquor.

### Akha
- The Akha consider **"Ekaw", "Kaw" and "Gaw" derogatory**. Their customary law and way of life is called *Akha Zang*.
- **Do not touch the gate or swing** (see the table above).
- **Festivals:** the Swing Festival (*Yehkuja*, "Women's New Year") runs about 4 days in late August. Men's New Year is in late December.
- **Crafts:** indigo cloth and silver headdresses. Coffee is grown at **Doi Chaang**, where Akha, Lisu and Chinese farmers replaced opium with coffee; the company was founded in 2003 ([Doi Chaang](https://doichaangcoffee.co.th/en/about-us/the-legend/)).

### Lawa / Lua
- Palaungic language. Communities are at Bo Luang (Chiang Mai) and around Mae Sariang. Chiang Mai was founded on the site of a Lawa settlement.
- Known as ironsmiths.
- *Low confidence, thin data* **[REVIEW: add founding-legend context]**

### Khmu (Kmhmu')
- Avoid "Kha", which carries historical low-status connotations.
- Mainly in Nan. Many are well integrated, and some speak Kham Mueang at home.
- The *taleo* talisman comes from Kmhmu tradition.
- *Low confidence*

### Mal and Prai (formerly "Htin/Tin")
- In Nan. "Tin", "Kha", "Phai" and "Lua" carry low-status connotations ([Journal of the Siam Society, 1987](https://thesiamsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/1987/03/JSS_075_0h_Filbeck_NewEthnicNamesForTinOfNan.pdf)). Use Mal or Prai.
- *Low confidence*

### Tai Yai (Shan)
- Self-name: Tai. "Shan" is rarely used locally.
- "Ngiao" (เงี้ยว) is a Northern Thai name that may be felt as disparaging. **[REVIEW]**
- **Poy Sang Long:** boys aged about 7 to 14 are dressed as princes and carried on relatives' shoulders for 3 days, then ordained as novices. It is held at the end of March or early April, especially in Mae Hong Son ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poy_Sang_Long); [Chiang Mai à la Carte](https://www.chiangmai-alacarte.com/the-tai-yai-people-of-north-thailand/)).
- Communities include both long-settled citizens and recent migrant workers. Don't ask about documents.

### Tai Lue
- Excellent weavers, for example at Ban Luang Nuea and Ban Luang Tai in Doi Saket, and in Nan and Phayao.
- **Dress:** red, white or pink turbans, indigo jackets and sarongs. Traditional dress is mostly worn at festivals ([Chiang Mai à la Carte](https://www.chiangmai-alacarte.com/the-tai-lue-people-of-chiang-mai/)).

### Tai Khuen (Tai Khün)
- From Kengtung. Their language shares 90–95% of its vocabulary with Northern Thai and uses the Tai Tham script. It is spoken in Chiang Rai.
- *Low confidence* **[REVIEW: Chiang Mai communities and crafts]**

### Yunnanese Chinese (Chin Haw, จีนฮ่อ)
- About one-third are Hui Muslim (for example, Ban Haw Mosque in Chiang Mai). Don't offer pork or alcohol to Muslim hosts.
- **Mae Salong (Santikhiri):** a former KMT settlement whose residents received citizenship in 1982. It grows oolong tea from Taiwanese stock planted from 1973 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_Mae_Salong)).
- The KMT and opium history is sensitive. Don't open a conversation with it.

- Tai Khuen, Mal/Prai, Khmu and Lawa details are thin.
