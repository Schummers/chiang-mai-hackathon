# Regional dialect variation across Northern Thailand

Context data for **Contextual Translate**. Machine-readable version: `regional_dialects.json`. Status: **DRAFT, needs expert review.** Romanizations are RTGS-style and were added by the researcher from the sourced Thai script. They do not mark tone.

## TL;DR for the app

- **Two broad accents.** Chiang Mai and Lamphun have a longer, "drawn-out" accent. Lampang, Phrae, Nan, Chiang Rai, Phayao and Uttaradit have a shorter, more clipped accent, which LPRU describes as ออกสั้นและห้วนกว่า ([LPRU](https://culture.lpru.ac.th/WebCulture2553/CultureKnowledge/file/05-03.pdf)). Linguists split Northern Thai more finely than this, into about 5 tonal groups (Thanajirawat 2018, via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_language)).
- **Market-critical words:** ซาว *sao* = 20, so ซาวห้าบาท = 25 baht. Fruit and herb names change by province. Guava is บะก้วย in Chiang Mai but บะแก๋ว or บะมั้น elsewhere. Papaya is บะก้วยเตด vs บะเตด. Coriander is หอมป้อม vs หอมน้อย ([krunuttaya](https://krunuttaya.wordpress.com/2012/04/05/%E0%B8%A0%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A9%E0%B8%B2%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%96%E0%B8%B4%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%99%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%AB%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%B7%E0%B8%AD-%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B3%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B7/), low confidence).
- **Other Tai languages are close cousins.** Tai Lue has about 95% lexical similarity to Northern Thai, and Tai Khuen has 90-95%. Tai Lue and Yong flatten diphthongs: เมือง becomes เมิง and สวย becomes โสย.
- **Mae Hong Son is Tai Yai (Shan)-majority.** Never use the exonym "Ngiao" (เงี้ยว), which is derogatory. Use Tai Yai / ไทใหญ่ instead.
- **Young urban people** often speak Central Thai vocabulary with a Northern accent. **Older and rural vendors** are more likely to use full Kham Mueang.

## Generational, urban/rural and code-switching

- Northern Thai is usually code-switched with Standard Thai in urban areas, while remote areas keep exclusive Northern Thai use ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thai_language)).
- The 1921 Compulsory Education Act pushed regional languages out of schools. Younger people often use "Central vocabulary + Northern accent", and some no longer identify as *khon mueang*. Prof. Thanet Charoenmuang predicted the language could disappear within 30-50 years ([Citylife 2017](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/clg/our-city/culture/rip-kham-mueang-the-slow-death-of-a-language/)). That prediction is an opinion, not a measurement.
- Minority varieties are shifting as well. Younger Yong speakers are merging tones ([Yong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yong_language)), and Nantaram Tai Khuen is now mixed with Kham Mueang and Central Thai ([SAC](https://wikicommunity.sac.or.th/community/1116)).

## How the app should handle mixed speech (recommendations, not sourced facts)

1. **Tag, don't correct.** If the transcript contains any marker token (บ่, อู้, ซาว, ลำ, อะหยัง, ปิ๊ก, กิ๋น, เจ้า, อ้าย, and similar), tell Claude "Northern-mixed speech". It should translate the meaning and add a one-line note only for regional words.
2. **Normalize known correspondences before glossary lookup:**
   - r becomes h (เฮือ = เรือ)
   - Yong/Lue vowel flattening (เมิง = เมือง)
   - eastern tone-mark variants (น่ำ = น้ำ)
3. **Handle numbers and prices explicitly.** Echo ซาว- numbers back to the foreigner as digits.
4. **Replies to the local speaker should be polite Central Thai by default.** Adding the Northern politeness particle เจ้า should be an opt-in setting.
5. **Use profile cards to widen the accepted vocabulary** (province, age band, languages spoken). Never use them to guess anyone's ethnicity. For Tai Yai, Lue, Karen, Akha and other minority vendors, assume Central Thai is the contact language.
6. **ASR caveat.** Thai speech-to-text is trained on Central Thai, so it will spell Northern words with Central tone marks or mishear them. The prompt should tell Claude this.

## Gaps and items for expert review

- **No verified vocabulary for Nan, Phrae or Phayao.** The Phrae thesis (Silpakorn 1991) has maps for 70 meanings but was not accessible.
- **"Other provinces" forms:** confirm which forms (ปี้, เย้ย, บะแก๋ว, บะมั้น, หอมน้อย, บะเตด) are used where.
- **บ่ะฮู้ / บ่ะฮู่:** two readings of the same source disagreed on which form is Chiang Mai's.
- **Thai script the researcher supplied rather than sourced:** เงี้ยว, ไทยอง, สำเนียงเมืองยอง. The Shan greeting "Mai Soong" and the Chiang Mai "mua huan" have no Thai script from a source.
- **Tak and Uttaradit district-level language split** could not be verified.
