# Hackathon Brief

What the organisers asked for, as shown on the opening slides. The product decisions live in [PRD.md](PRD.md).

**Event**: Claude Code Impact Lab, Chiang Mai (Claude Community).
**Presentations**: 2026-09-27, after lunch. 4 to 6 minutes per team.

## Judging

Each criterion is scored 0 to 5.

| Criterion | What the jury asks |
|---|---|
| **Day-one impact** (counts double) | Could people use it right away, and would it make a real difference to them? |
| Product | Is it easy to use? Do people get it the first time they try? |
| Idea | However far you got, how creative and useful is the idea? |
| Demo | Are the problem, the user, and the outcome clear from your presentation? |

**Before presenting, push the code to a public GitHub repo** so others can use it after the weekend.

What this means for us: day-one impact counts double. A narrow problem, a precise user and a tool that works in 30 seconds beat an ambitious idea half built.

## Challenges

The ideas under each challenge are starting points. Any idea that fits the challenge counts.

### Challenge 02: Navigate Chiang Mai's cultural layers (our challenge)

> Make it easier for anyone to deal with the city's languages, customs, and everyday culture.

- **Languages**: Thai, Northern Thai (Kham Mueang), English and Chinese, whether written on a sign or spoken at a stall.
- **Cultural know-how**: name the vegetables at the market, or explain which condiment does what at a khao soi shop.
- **Icebreakers**: simple, friendly ways to start a conversation with locals who'd happily chat.

### Challenge 01: Open up nomad events to locals (not ours, kept for reference)

> Make nomad events and communities easier for Thai locals to find, join, and enjoy.

- **Live translation**: devices or apps that translate talks and conversations in real time, so language isn't what keeps people out.
- **Local-language listings**: event listings and cross-listings in Thai, posted where locals actually look for things to do.
- **Cultural confidence**: ways for locals and newcomers to understand each other's norms before and during an event.

Our later Thai-side use case (a Thai person who speaks some English and wants to express a complex idea) sits close to this challenge. See the PRD, "Later".
