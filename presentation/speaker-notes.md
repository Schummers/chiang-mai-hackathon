# Uu Muang pitch: speaker notes

## 1. อู้เมือง

0:15 · Criterion: Demo (clarity). Say the name in Thai first, then English: "อู้เมือง, Uu Muang, means speak Northern." One sentence: translation is transactional; Uu Muang is contextual. Replace [Team name].

## 2. One stall, two people, no shared words

0:30 · Criteria: Idea, Demo. Tell the fern vegetable story from our own visit. Make the judges see the stall. Both sides lose: the visitor walks away, the vendor loses a sale and a connection.

## 3. Generic translators miss the Lanna layer

0:25 · Criterion: Idea. Our differentiation. The example is real: ยินดี means "glad" or "you're welcome" in Central Thai but "thank you" in Kham Mueang. Before presenting, run it through a general translator and show the actual result.

## 4. Built for both sides of the stall

0:25 · Criteria: Day-one impact, Idea. Use real people you met or tested with; replace the brackets and add photos. The two-sided benefit is what makes this more than a translator.

## 5. Uu Muang turns every stall into a conversation

0:30 · Criteria: Product, Idea. Three features only, matching the Challenge 02 brief: languages, cultural know-how, icebreakers. Replace the placeholder with a real app screenshot.

## 6. You choose the layers the other person sees

0:20 · Criterion: Idea. Context is customisable: a visitor might share where they're from and what they like to eat; a vendor might share today's specials. Nothing is shared unless the user switches it on.

## 7. From pointing to talking in [X] seconds

0:20 · Criterion: Product (easy to use first time). Show how few steps it takes. Behind step 2, Claude gets only the context that matters (about 8,000 tokens: place, month, dialect words, both profiles), so it's fast and costs about $0.02 an exchange. Fill in [X] from real timing.

## 8. See it at the stall

1:15 · Criteria: Demo, Product. The longest part of the pitch: rehearse it. Have a screen recording ready in case the Wi-Fi fails. Invite a judge to scan the QR code. Luke to confirm phak kut is in season at the demo date and the Kham Mueang phrase.

## 9. What changes the first day someone uses it

0:40 · Criterion: Day-one impact (counts double). Use only real numbers from testing this weekend, even if small. One real quote beats any projection.

## 10. Built on Northern knowledge, not generic translation

0:15 · Criterion: Idea. A distinctly Lanna app. Dictionary from PyThaiNLP (Wiktionary, CC-BY-SA), tones checked against two independent sources, plus seasonal produce, festivals, etiquette and beliefs. All open in our GitHub repo.

## 11. A market basket that talks

0:20 · Criterion: Idea. Keep it short: basket, weave, open tail. The brand comes from the market itself, not from a translation app.

## 12. From one market to the whole North

0:15 · Criterion: Idea. The judges require a public GitHub repo before we present; the link is live.

## 13. Who built Uu Muang

0:10 · Don't read every name aloud; say how the team is split (build, research, design, testing). Names from the repo team folders; add roles and photos.

## 14. อู้เมืองได้ก่อ?

0:15 · End on the question locals already ask foreigners: "อู้เมืองได้ก่อ?" (Can you speak Northern?) "ได้แล้วเจ้า" (Now I can). Have a native Northern speaker confirm the Thai lines before the pitch.
